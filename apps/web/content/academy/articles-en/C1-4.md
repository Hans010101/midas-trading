# Identifying Trend Types

> Midas Academy · Chan Theory Advanced · Structure Advanced

"Is this a rise or a shock?" - This question is the starting point of almost all analyses. Ordinary technical analysis relies on "looking at the like", but Chan Theory gives a standard that can accurately determine the price by using the number of Central Hubs. In this article, we explain how Chan Theory strictly distinguishes between **trend (up/down)** and **consolidation**, and what is the "completion of Trend Type".

This article is placed at the end of the structural advancement because it assembles the previous parts (Segment, Central Hub, Level) into Chan Theory's **general classification** of the market.

**Quick overview of this article**

- Chan Theory divides trends at any level into only two categories: **trend (up/down)** and **consolidation**; the distinction is based on the number and direction of **Central Hub**.
- **Consolidation = only 1 Central Hub of this level**; **Trend = ≥2 Central Hubs in the same direction** (rising = Central Hubs are increasing in sequence, falling = decreasing in sequence).
- The essence of the trend is **"Central Hub's relay"** (going out of one range and rushing to the next higher/lower range), which is more structured and reviewable than "looking at the slope" - it can avoid "big box mistaking it as a trend".
- **Completion of Trend Type**: After the completion of one level of trend, it must be followed by another trend (opposite trend or consolidation); the completion of trend is often accompanied by Divergence**; "Completion" is a structural concept, **does not mean immediate reversal, and is not a buying and selling signal**.
- ⚠️ The entire article is an **analysis framework**, which is used to objectively describe the trend structure. It does not predict prices or constitute operational recommendations.

## 1. Chan Theory only divides trends into two categories

In Chan Theory, there are only two types of Trend Type at any level:

- **Trend**: divided into **rising** and **falling**;
- **Consolidation**.

Just these two categories, there is no third one. The standard for distinguishing them is not something vague like "how much it has increased" or "how big the slope is", but a very hard indicator - **This trend includes several Central Hubs (of this level). **

## 2. Consolidation: Only one Central Hub is included

**Definition of consolidation: A certain level of Trend Type contains only one Central Hub of that level. **

There is only one Central Hub, which means that the long and short forces repeatedly see-saw in a range and there is no directional "relay". It corresponds to "shocks and sideways" in our intuition, but Chan Theory gives it a countable hard standard: **Central Hub quantity = 1**.

## 3. Trend: at least two Central Hubs "sequentially moving in the same direction"

**Definition of trend: A certain (completed) level Trend Type contains at least two or more (≥2) Central Hubs of this level in the same direction. **

Take it apart and look at two key words:

- **At least two Central Hubs**: One Central Hub is consolidation, **two or more** may be a trend;
- **Same direction in turn**: These Central Hubs must "relay" in the same direction——
  - **Uptrend**: The range of the next Central Hub is overall **higher than** the previous Central Hub (Central Hub moves upward step by step);
  - **Downward trend**: The range of the next Central Hub is overall **lower than** the previous Central Hub (Central Hub is falling step by step).

Intuitive understanding: Trend = **"Central Hub + the trend of connecting Central Hub + another higher/lower Central Hub"**. The price does not stay in a range, but **walks out of a range and rushes to a higher (or lower) range**. This "Central Hub relay" is the essence of the trend.

So there is a very concise saying in Chan Theory: When ** goes up, it means that the Central Hubs are higher than the other; when it goes down, it means that the Central Hubs are lower than the other ones. **

## 4. Why "counting Central Hubs" is more reliable than "looking at the slope"

Traditional analysis often relies on Moving Average direction, high and low points, and slope to judge trends. These are all correct, but they have a common problem: they are subjective and fuzzy, and everyone has different opinions on the boundaries. **

The advantage of Chan Theory's "Central Hub" is that it turns "trend/consolidation" into a **structured and reviewable** judgment:

- It does not ask "whether the rise is steep or not", but only asks "whether you have exited the second and third Central Hub in the same direction";
- For a trend that seems to have risen a lot, if ** always repeats ** in only one Central Hub (even if the amplitude is large), Chan Theory will judge it as **consolidation**, not a trend;
- On the other hand, even if it rises slowly, as long as **Central Hub continues to rise** at the first level, it is a real upward trend.

This perspective can help you avoid the common misjudgment of "thinking it is a trend after a long rise, but the result is just a large box shock".

## 5. "Complete" of Trend Type

Finally, let's talk about "the trend is finally completed" - this word refers to a level of Trend Type, a state that is finally completed and can be confirmed.

A few key points:

- **Trend Type is completed "step by step"**: After one level of Trend Type is completed, **it must be followed by another Trend Type** - either a trend in the opposite direction, or a consolidation. The market is composed of such Trend Types, connected end to end and never ending.
- **The completion of a trend is often accompanied by Divergence**: a trend comes to an end and **Divergence** appears (power failure, see C1-7), which is often a sign of the "completion" of this trend - after Divergence, the price turns to the opposite trend or a larger level of consolidation (echoing the theorem of C1-3: Trend Divergence → Finally, the Central Hub level expands).
- **"Completion" is a structural concept, not "reversal when the point is reached"**: Trend Type completion describes the end of a structural paragraph. **It does not mean that the price will reverse immediately and violently**, nor can it be regarded as a signal of "it is time to buy/sell when it is completed".
