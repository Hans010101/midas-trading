# Combining Chan Theory with Traditional Technical Analysis

> Midas Academy · Advanced Chan Theory · Analysis of Difficulties

People who study Chan Theory often fall into two extremes: either "Chan Theory is supreme and looks down on Moving Average MACD", or "Chan Theory is too mysterious and just goes back to the old method." In this article, let’s talk objectively: Are Chan Theory and traditional technical analysis mutually exclusive or can they cooperate? ** Conclusion first - they are ** different perspectives, each good at their own strengths, they can cooperate, and they are neither conflicting nor mutually exclusive**.

**Quick overview of this article**

- Chan Theory is strong in **structure/level**, and traditional analysis is strong in **intuition/kinetic energy**; the two are different perspectives, **cooperable, not mutually exclusive**; both are references, not predictions.
- Three major points of coordination: **MACD is originally Chan Theory's tool for judging Divergence**; **Moving Average assists in looking at the direction, and Central Hub and Moving Average dense areas can be verified**; **Traditional form ≈ another description of Chan Theory's structure** (compare but not force one-to-one correspondence).
- Three principles: **No mutual exclusion, no superstition (multiple tools only improve grasp but do not eliminate misjudgments), use one set as the main tool and the others as supplements**.
- Objective view: Chan Theory **accurate but complex and subjective, not guaranteed to be accurate**, traditional analysis **simple but lagging and fuzzy** - **neither deified**, what determines the result is understanding, discipline and awe.
- ⚠️ The entire article is a discussion of the **analytical framework** and does not make predictions or constitute buying or selling advice.

## 1. First understand what they are.

Once the "long board" of the two is clearly laid out, it won't be either/or:

- **Chan Theory**: A set of **recursive analysis framework** about **trend structure** (Stroke / Segment / Central Hub / Level / Buying and Selling Point). The strength is "**Structure and Level**" - it can accurately tell you "how the trend is formed, which step it goes to, and what level it is."
- **Traditional technical analysis**: Moving Average (look at the trend), MACD (look at the momentum), patterns (head and shoulders/double top, etc.), various indicators - the strength is "**intuitive, easy to use, well-known**".

But one thing is the same: they are all "analytical frameworks/references", neither predicting prices nor guaranteeing profits. **This is the bottom line.

## 2. Coordination point 1: MACD is Chan Theory’s tool for judging Divergence.

Many people don’t realize that Chan Theory uses MACD to judge Divergence (yellow and white lines pull back + red and green columns shrink, see C1-7).

Therefore, MACD is not "another set of opposing things" for Chan Theory, but a ready-made "strength measurement tool" in the Chan Theory system**. People who study Chan Theory should not only not reject MACD, but should make good use of it to judge Divergence. This in itself is the best example of Chan Theory's "natural cooperation" with traditional indicators.

## 3. Coordination point 2: Moving Average assists in looking at directions and key areas

Moving Average can also confirm each other with Chan Theory:

- **Moving Average Direction** can help you **quickly and intuitively** determine the general direction (upward long, downward short, flat shock), as the first reference for "is it a trend or a shock now";
- Chan Theory's **Central Hub area** often coincides with the **Moving Average intensive area and support and resistance** - when the two point to the same "important area", they can confirm each other and increase confidence.

But be clear: Moving Average is a lagging approximation, while Chan Theory's trend definition (Central Hub relay, see C1-4) is more structured. The two conclusions may not be completely consistent - at this time, you should focus on understanding the structure and use Moving Average as an auxiliary visual reference. Don't let the lagging Moving Average overturn your judgment on the structure.

## 4. Coordination point three: traditional form ≈ "another description" of Chan Theory's structure

Many traditional forms, viewed using Chan Theory, are actually some kind of **structure**:

- **Head and Shoulders**: often corresponds to the **Divergence + Central Hub** structure at the end of a certain level of trend;
- **Double bottom (W bottom)**: often corresponds to the structure of **Bottom Fractal / 1 buy, 2 buy**;
-…

In other words, form is a description of "**experience and intuition**", and Chan Theory is a description of "**structural precision**" - the two often say the same thing. You can **compare** (use the Chan Theory structure to understand why the form is effective), but **don't force a one-to-one correspondence** (the form is a fuzzy experience, Chan Theory is a strict definition, and it is impossible to match them perfectly).

## 5. Three principles of cooperation

How can we cooperate without causing chaos? Keep three rules:

1. **Not mutually exclusive**: Chan Theory manages "structure and level", while traditional indicators manage "intuition and kinetic energy". Each is good at its own merits and can be used together;
2. **No superstition**: It is not "Chan Theory + Moving Average + MACD + RSI, it must be accurate if you look at it all" - **Multi-tool verification only improves confidence, but does not eliminate misjudgments** (echoing Group B, C1). More tools do not mean more accurate, but they may fight with each other, leaving you at a loss;
3. **Use one system as the main one and the others as supplements**: Don’t use five systems to fight each other at the same time. It is recommended to use the one you are most familiar with and believe in as the main body (for example, Chan Theory structure), and use the rest as confirmation and supplement.

## 6. Objectively speaking: Chan Theory is not deified, and traditional analysis is not lagging behind either.

Finally, correct your attitude and avoid the two extremes:

- Chan Theory has its **accuracy and depth**, but it is also **complex, highly subjective (thousands of people are involved), and accuracy is not guaranteed**;
- Traditional analysis is **simple and intuitive**, but also **laggy and fuzzy**.

**No one set beats the other, they are just tools to help you understand the market. ** What really determines the result is never "which set of artifacts are used", but your understanding of the tools, execution of discipline, and reverence for risks (echoing Group F). With this attitude, both Chan Theory and traditional analysis can be used well.
