# MACD Strategy

> Midas Academy · C3 Strategy Topic Library

MACD is one of the most popular momentum indicators and there are many strategies surrounding it. This article explains the principles, usage and boundaries of the MACD strategy - and nails the most commonly misunderstood definition.

**Quick overview of this article**

- MACD = **DIF (Fast Moving Average−Slow Moving Average) + DEA (DIF's Moving Average) + Red and Green Columns (DIF−DEA)**, reflecting kinetic energy.
- ★**MACD Golden Cross = DIF crosses DEA above (the column turns from green to red), Death Cross = DIF crosses below DEA** - the protagonist is DIF/DEA, **not Moving Average** (don't confuse it with Moving Average Golden Cross).

## 1. Strategy principles

MACD consists of three parts (see B4):

- **DIF (fast line) = fast moving average − slow moving average** (commonly used 12, 26);
- **DEA (slow line) = moving average of DIF** (commonly used 9), smoother and lagging behind DIF;
- **Red and Green Column = DIF − DEA** (DIF is positive/red at the top, negative/green at the bottom, and the intersection is zero).

MACD reflects the "momentum" of a trend - the strength of the force behind rising/falling prices. MACD strategy is to use it to judge the direction and strength of ** momentum and assist decision-making.

## 2. Applicable scenarios and failure scenarios

- **Applicable to: Markets with a certain trend/momentum. ** When the price has direction and strength, MACD's Golden CrossDeath Cross and histogram can better reflect the changes in momentum.
- **Invalidation: Shocking market conditions. ** During the shock, MACD will also frequently make Golden CrossDeath Cross and give false signals; divergence is also prone to **passivation and failure**.

## 3. How to use (signals and rules)

**1. Golden Cross / Death Cross (★Be sure to remember the definition accurately):**

- **MACD Golden Cross = DIF crosses DEA** (the column turns from green to red), which is a bullish reference;
- **MACD Death Cross = DIF crosses DEA** (the column turns from red to green), bearish reference.

⚠️ **Note: The protagonists of MACD Golden Cross are DIF and DEA, not Moving Average! ** "The short-term Moving Average crosses the long-term Moving Average" is **Moving Average Golden Cross** (C3-1). Don't confuse the two - this is the most common flaw.

**2. Red and green columns look at the strength of kinetic energy: **
The column ** lengthens = kinetic energy increases **, and the column ** shortens = kinetic energy weakens **; the red column peaks and shrinks, which often means that the upward momentum is fading.

**3. Divergence indicates kinetic energy failure (see B6): **
- **Top Divergence**: The price reaches a new high, but the MACD high **reduces** → the upward momentum is exhausted;
- **Bottom Divergence**: The price hits a new low, but the MACD low** actually rises** → the downward momentum is exhausted.
- Divergence prompts "**possible reversal**", but **not precise timing** (will be passivated and may fail).

## 4. Advantages, Disadvantages and Risks

**Advantages**: Taking into account both the **trend direction and the strength of kinetic energy**; divergence can indicate kinetic energy exhaustion in advance; it has one more dimension than simply looking at price.

**Disadvantages and Risks**:

- **Lagging**: MACD is derived from Moving Average. It is essentially a lagging indicator. When the signal appears, the price has often moved for a while;
- **Many false signals in shocks**: The same pain point as the Moving Average strategy;
- **Inaccurate divergence**: divergence may be **passivated** (continue to move despite looking at the divergence in a strong trend), may **fail**, and cannot be used as a "reversal guarantee" (echoing C1-18);
- **Profitability not guaranteed**.
