# Identifying the Three Buy/Sell Point Types—and Common Mistakes

> Midas Academy · Chan Theory Advanced · Practical Practice of Buying and Selling Points

In the introduction, we remembered the "definition" of Three Types of Buy and Sell Points. But in actual combat, just memorizing definitions is useless - the real difficulty is: how to recognize them on a picture that has not been completed? And, what is most likely to be mistaken for them? ** This article explains the key points of practical identification category by category, and highlights the most common "mistakes" in each category.

Let me start by nailing the red line that runs throughout:

> ⚠️ Three Types of Buy and Sell Points are Chan Theory’s terminology to describe the **trend structure position** ("the structure has reached this position"), **not an operation instruction** of "when the price reaches this point, you should buy/sell". All identification methods in this article are **analysis framework** and do not constitute any buying or selling advice or price prediction.

**Quick overview of this article**

- Three types of buying points (mirror of selling points): **1 buy = turning point after the downward trend Divergence**; **2 buy = 1 pull back after buying but not break the previous low**; **3 buy = break through the Central Hub and then pull back but not fall back to the Central Hub (not break ZG)**.
- The most common misjudgments in each category: 1 buy - treat **Falling relay rebound** as 1 buy (catching a flying knife), Divergence judgment is inaccurate; 2 buy - callback **broke the previous low** and still treat it as 2 buy; 3 buy - pull back **fall back to Central Hub** but still treat it as 3 buy, or repeatedly treat Central Hub as a breakthrough, **ZG value calculation is wrong**.
- **Central Hub upper edge ZG = the minimum value of the three high points** (not the maximum value). Miscalculation will make all 3 buy judgments wrong.
- ⚠️ Buying and selling points are **structural terms/signals, not operating instructions**; they will fail and are not predicted; they must be included in the comprehensive judgment of the complete system and do not place orders without thinking.

## 1. First use a picture to clearly illustrate the three types of buying points (mirror of selling points)

Taking "buying points" as an example (selling points are completely mirrored), three types of buying points appear at different stages of a trend:

- **Type 1 Buy Point**: A **turning point** (a decline turns to an increase) formed after a certain level of **downtrend** reaches the end and **trend Divergence** appears (failure of strength, see C1-7). It is closest to the bottom, has the most meaningful levels, but is also the most difficult to confirm.
- **Type 2 Buy Point (2 buy)**: After the 1 buy appears, the price rebounds and then pulls back. The low point of this ** callback does not fall below the low of the 1 buy point (does not create a new low)** - this callback low point is the 2 buy point. It is a confirmation that "the turning point has been established".
- **Type 3 Buy Point (3 buy)**: After a Central Hub is formed, the price **leaves (breaks through) the Central Hub** upwards, and then pulls back, **the low point of the pullback does not fall back to the Central Hub range (does not break the upper edge of the Central Hub ZG)** - this pullback low point is 3 buys. What it confirms is that "a new upward segment (trend) is about to unfold."

Mirror image of selling points: 1 Sell = turning point after the upward trend Divergence; 2 Sell = counterattack without breaking the previous high; 3 Sell = counterattack after falling below the Central Hub but not going back to the Central Hub (without breaking the lower edge of Central Hub ZD).

## 2. Type 1 Buy Point: Actual combat identification and the most common misjudgments

**How to find**: 1 The core of buying is "**Trend Divergence**". You need to first confirm that the current period is a **downtrend** (≥ 2 Central Hubs that decrease in sequence, see C1-4), and then determine whether the decline at the end is **exhausted** compared to the previous period (the MACD yellow and white lines pull back, and the green column area shrinks, see C1-7). Divergence was established before we could talk about 1 purchase.

**Most common misjudgments**:

- **Mistake "rebound from decline" as 1 buy**. This is the most fatal and common mistake - the decline has not really diverged yet, it just rebounded midway. You thought it was the bottom and copied it, but the result is "catching a flying knife". **Without confirmed trend Divergence, there is no 1 buy. **
- **Divergence is not accurately judged**: MACD is blunted, or the wrong comparison segment is taken, and "still creating strength" is misread as "strength failure".
- **The level is not clear**: Treat a small level Divergence as a large level 1 to buy (a small level 1 purchase has a small level and a small range, see C1-6).

## 3. Type 2 Buy Point: Actual combat identification and the most common misjudgments

**How to find**: To buy 2, you must **have 1 to buy** first. When the first wave of rise after 1-buy ends and the callback occurs, focus on the low point of this callback - as long as it does not fall below the low of 1-buy (does not make a new low), it will have the structure of 2-buy.

**Most common misjudgments**:

- **The callback has fallen below the previous low, and it is still regarded as 2 buy**. 2. The hard condition for buying is "**not to break the previous low**". Once a new low is reached, this structure will not hold (it is very likely that 1. buying itself is a misjudgment).
- **There is no 1 to buy first, find 2 to buy out of thin air**. 2 buys are "confirmation actions of 1 buys", and it is meaningless to talk about 2 buys without talking about 1 buys.

## 4. Type 3 Buy Point: Actual combat identification and the most common misjudgments

**How to find**: 3 The core of buying is "**Breakthrough of Central Hub + Cannot withdraw to Central Hub**". First, there must be a clear Central Hub (note that **the upper edge of Central Hub ZG = the minimum value of the three high points**), the price breaks through the Central Hub upwards and then pulls back, and the **pullback low does not fall below ZG** (does not return to the Central Hub range), which is 3 buys.

**Most common misjudgments**:

- **The withdrawal actually fell back to the Central Hub, and it was still regarded as a 3 buy**. 3 The hard conditions for buying are "**If you withdraw, you will not be able to withdraw the Central Hub, and you will not break the ZG**". If the retracement falls into the Central Hub range, it means that the breakthrough has not been established, and this is not a 3-buy (it is likely to be an extension or expansion of the Central Hub, see C1-2).
- **Mistaken a reiteration within Central Hub as a "3 buy after breakout"**. I'm still going back and forth in the Central Hub, and I haven't really left the Central Hub at all, so I can't talk about 3 purchases.
- **Wrong calculation of ZG on the upper edge of Central Hub**: ZG is taken as the maximum value of the three high points (it should be the **minimum value**), resulting in a completely wrong judgment of "ZG is broken or not" - this is a typical low-level flaw in Chan Theory, so it must be calculated correctly.

## 5. Separate "recognition" and "operation"

In actual combat, there is another key point at the psychological level: identifying certain types of buying and selling points does not mean "buying a full position immediately". **

- The buying and selling point is **structural judgment**, which tells you that "the trend structure has reached a position worthy of attention";
- But whether to do it, how much Go Long, where to put Stop Loss, and what level it corresponds to is a matter of your entire trading system (risk management, position, discipline), not the buying and selling point itself;
- And the buying and selling points will **fail** - 1. After buying, it continues to reach new lows, 3. After buying, it falls back to the Central Hub. It is very common. Chan Theory never guarantees that "buying and selling points will be honored".

Therefore, the correct usage is: **Treat the buying and selling points as "structural signals" and incorporate them into your system to make comprehensive judgments** instead of "place orders without thinking when you see the buying and selling points".
