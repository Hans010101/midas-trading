# Long/Short Ratio: Reference for Market Sentiment

> Midas Academy · Level 4 · Module 12 Contracts and Derivatives

## 1. Introduction of the concept: In the market, are there more people who are rich or those who are empty?

Funding Rate reflects sentiment from the side of "price premium and discount", while **Long/Short Ratio** is more direct: it tells you the balance of power between **standing on the long side and standing on the short side** in the market at this moment. It is a common reference for observing market sentiment.

## 2. What is the long-short ratio?

**The long-short ratio is the ratio of the power of Go Long and Go Short in the contract market. ** A ratio greater than 1 means there are more bulls; a ratio less than 1 means there are more shorts.

It reflects the current position tendency/sentiment distribution of market participants—whether everyone is more bullish or bearish overall.

## 3. Different calibers: different algorithms

It should be noted that the long-short ratio has **different statistical calibers** and the meaning is not exactly the same (and **varies by platform**):

- **Top-trader Long/Short Ratio**: Calculated based on the long and short positions of **large traders (accounts with larger positions)**, reflecting the tendency of "big funds".
- **Number of Accounts Long/Short Ratio**: Calculated based on the number of **Go Long/Go Short accounts**, reflecting the tendency of "number of people".

At the same moment, these two calibers may give different pictures (for example, the number of people is large, but the large investors are short). When looking at the long-short ratio, you must first figure out which caliber you are looking at.

## 4. Reference significance and limitations

The long-short ratio can help you perceive the distribution of emotions, but its limitations are equally important and must be made clear:

1. **It is not that "if there are many bulls, it will definitely rise". ** This is the biggest misunderstanding. The market will not necessarily rise just because there are many people Go Long - in many cases it is just the opposite.
2. **Controversy over "reverse indicators"**: Because of the previous point, some people regard extreme long-short ratios as **reverse references** (for example, when most people go long and are overcrowded, they are wary of risks). But this is only a controversial perspective, not a reliable rule, and cannot be applied mechanically.
3. **It is an emotional reference, not a signal or prediction. ** The long-short ratio describes "how everyone stands at the moment" and cannot be used to infer the price direction.

## 5. How to treat the long-short ratio

The reasonable attitude is: **Regard the long-short ratio as a reference dimension for perceiving market sentiment, rather than as a basis for buying and selling. ** It is suitable to be put together with price, OI, Funding Rate, etc. to help you understand "what is the current emotional state of the market" more three-dimensionally, but any single data is not enough to predict the trend. It is also one of the dimensions of Midas contract analysis (for reference only, **does not constitute investment advice or price prediction**).

## 6. Main points of this article

- **Long-Short Ratio = The ratio of the power of Go Long to Go Short**, reflecting the position tendency/sentiment distribution of market participants.
- There are **different calibers**: Top-trader Long/Short Ratio (depending on large funds), number of accounts long/short ratio (depending on the number of people), which have different meanings and **vary depending on the platform**.
- **Limitations are very important**: It is not that "long bulls will definitely rise"; "reverse indicators" are controversial perspectives and unreliable rules; they are not signals or predictions**.
- It is one of the **reference dimensions** for perceiving emotions. It needs to be combined with other information and does not constitute an operation recommendation.
