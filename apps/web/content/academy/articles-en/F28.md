## 2. Review: Six modules of the system (continue from F1)

First, let’s quickly review what F1 talked about, the six modules of a complete trading system:

1. **Analysis method**: How to judge the market;
2. **Exit and entry rules**: when to enter and when to exit;
3. **Risk Management**: How much is the maximum loss for a single Stroke and how to set Stop Loss;
4. **Fund/Position Management**: How much to invest per Stroke;
5. **Psychology and Discipline**: How to ensure that rules are followed;
6. **Review**: How to improve from every Stroke transaction.

They look like six pieces individually; but in actual combat, they are not isolated, but interlocking and working together**. The following uses a case to demonstrate this collaboration.

## 3. How the six modules cooperate in a complete case (illustration)

Set up a schematic scenario (without naming the species): a trend-following trade in an upward trend. Let’s see how the six modules appear in sequence and cooperate with each other in this Stroke:

**① Analysis method - first see clearly "what structure was at that time". **
Use tools (trend, high and low point structure, Moving Average, Chan Theory when necessary) to judge: It is currently in the callback phase of the upward trend (based on the information available at the time, no prediction). This is the **starting point** of this Stroke transaction - without analysis, there is no way to talk about it later.

**② Rules for entry and exit - determine "how to do it". **
Based on the analysis, it is determined according to the rules: **Callback Stabilizes Entry**; **Stop Loss** is set below the callback low point/key Moving Average (where the judgment is falsified); **Take Profit** uses moving Stop Loss to follow the trend. Make a decision before entering the market (follow F2 trading plan).

**③ Risk management - first calculate "what will happen in the worst case". **
For this Stroke, set the maximum loss of the first single Stroke to 1% of the total funds** (then F4); the Stop Loss is set at a reasonable position. (If it is a contract: Stop Loss must be set before **Liquidation price**, then go to E9; and to control the leverage, go to F23.)

**④ Fund/position management - decide "how much to invest". **
Use the **1% risk method + Stop Loss amplitude to reverse the position** (continue with F4: Position = risk amount ÷ Stop Loss amplitude); **No heavy positioning**. Even if this Stroke stops loss, the loss will only be an affordable 1%.

**⑤ Psychology and discipline - ensure "really doing everything as planned". **
After entering the market, **strictly implement**: do not hold orders because of floating losses, do not run early because of floating profits, and do not temporarily withdraw or move Stop Loss (receive F5 mood, F6 discipline). Rationality in the planning stage is realized through discipline in the execution stage.

**⑥ Review - "Learn from it" after the transaction. **
After the transaction is completed, use the four quadrants of **F7** to review: Is the process correct? Is it a matter of judgment or execution? Are there any emotional disturbances? Summarize areas that can be improved and feed them back into the rules.

## 4. Key: The six modules are indispensable and mutually supportive.

Why emphasize "collaboration"? Because these six pieces support each other, without any one piece, other efforts may be in vain:

- **The analysis is correct, but there is no risk management** → One misreading may result in a big loss, and no matter how accurate the analysis is, it cannot save you;
- **The entry and exit rules are very good, but there is no discipline to enforce them** → The rules are just on paper, and they are carried away by emotions on the spot, and the rules are in vain;
- **Risk and position are under control, but the analysis method is wrong** → Loss will be slower, but loss will still occur in the long run;
- **I’ve done it all before, but never repeated it** → Unable to discover and correct problems, **making the same mistakes over and over again**, and no progress will be made.

Therefore, the **system is a whole**: its value does not lie in the strength of any one part, but in the synergy of the six parts and the absence of fatal shortcomings. Whether a system can make you "long-term stable and live longer" depends on the weakest link - this is what F1 means by "complete and repeatable".
