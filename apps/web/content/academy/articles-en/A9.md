# Stop Loss and Take Profit: Discipline to Protect Capital

> **Midas Academy · Phase 1 · Module 4: Basic Concepts of Trading**

**Overview**

- **Stop Loss**: A preset price to exit at a loss (set below the entry price for Longs) to control the maximum loss per trade.
- **Take Profit**: A preset price to exit at a profit (set above the entry price for Longs) to lock in gains.
- Clearly defining **Entry Price, Stop Loss Price, and Take Profit Price** before entering a trade is a fundamental skill for mature trading.
- **Stop Loss is the core of trading discipline**; its purpose is to "prevent small losses from dragging into large losses." However, it is a risk control measure and **does not guarantee profit**, and the actual exit price may deviate under extreme market conditions.

## I. Concept Introduction: "When to Exit" is as Important as "When to Enter"

Many beginners focus all their energy on "when to buy" but rarely seriously consider "when to sell," especially what to do if they are wrong. However, mature traders understand: **Exit plans often determine long-term success or failure.**

The **Stop Loss** and **Take Profit** discussed in this section are two of the most basic "exit plans." Their commonality is: **decide and set the exit position before entering the market**, rather than making emotional decisions on the fly once the market moves.

## II. Stop Loss: Controlling Single-Trade Losses Within an Acceptable Range

**Stop Loss** refers to **pre-setting a price to exit at a loss; once the price moves unfavorably to this level, you exit (automatically), thereby controlling the loss of that trade.**

Taking a Long position as an example: You buy at a certain price and simultaneously set a Stop Loss price **below the entry price**. If the price drops and hits this Stop Loss, you exit—limiting the loss to the distance between the entry price and the Stop Loss price, preventing it from expanding further. (For Shorts, it is the opposite: set Stop Loss above the entry price.)

Its function is to draw a "bottom line" for your losses.

## III. Take Profit: Locking in Profits When Profitable

**Take Profit** refers to **pre-setting a price to exit at a profit; when the price moves favorably to this level, you exit, securing the floating profit.**

Taking a Long position as an example again: You set a Take Profit price **above the entry price**. When the price rises and hits it, you exit, locking in the profit between the entry price and the Take Profit price. (For Shorts, it is the opposite: set Take Profit below the entry price.)

Its function is to prevent the regret of "making money on paper but failing to hold it, only to see it drop back."

## IV. Why Stop Loss is the Core of Trading Discipline

Among Stop Loss and Take Profit, **Stop Loss is particularly critical** and is even viewed by many as the core of trading discipline. This is due to a simple but harsh truth:

**Prevent small losses from dragging into large losses.**

The most damaging aspect of the market is often not a single judgment error, but "holding on despite being wrong"—hanging on to the hope of "wait and see, maybe it will come back," watching a small loss slowly turn into an unbearable large loss. The significance of Stop Loss is to use a pre-determined discipline to **forcefully interrupt this psychological hope**, exiting decisively while the loss is small to preserve capital and face the next opportunity.

It is important to emphasize here to avoid misunderstandings:

1. **Stop Loss is "Risk Control," not "Profit Guarantee."** Setting a Stop Loss does not mean this trade will make money, nor does it mean "with a Stop Loss, you will profit for sure." It manages "don't lose too much when losing," not to make you profitable.
2. **Stop Loss may be triggered frequently.** If the Stop Loss is set too close, normal minor price fluctuations may repeatedly trigger an exit. How to set a Stop Loss is an art that requires practice; there is no standard answer.
3. **Under extreme market conditions, the actual exit price may deviate.** During violent price movements or gaps, the actual execution price may differ from your preset Stop Loss price (slippage). Stop Loss significantly reduces risk but cannot guarantee an exact exit at that price.

What truly matters is internalizing **discipline**—the act of "pre-determining an exit plan and executing it strictly." This is the dividing line between "rational trading" and "gambling by feeling."

## V. Key Takeaways

- **Stop Loss**: A preset loss exit price (set below the entry price for Longs) to control the expansion of single-trade losses.
- **Take Profit**: A preset profit exit price (set above the entry price for Longs) to lock in existing profits.
- Clearly thinking through **Entry Price, Stop Loss Price, and Take Profit Price** before entering a trade is a fundamental skill for mature trading.
- **Stop Loss is the core of trading discipline**; its meaning is to "prevent small losses from dragging into large losses." However, it is a risk control measure and **does not guarantee profit**, and the actual exit price may deviate under extreme market conditions.
