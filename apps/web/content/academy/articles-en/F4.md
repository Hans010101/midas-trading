# Position Management: Deciding How Long You Can Survive in the Market

> Midas Academy · Level 5 · Module 13 Trading System and Practical Combat

**Quick overview of this article**

- **Position management determines how long you can survive in the market**: Winning or losing a single Stroke depends on the market, and long-term survival depends on the position.
- **Fixed Proportion Risk Method**: Each Stroke only risks a fixed small proportion of the total funds (commonly 1%–2%), which refers to the "proportion of maximum loss when Stop Loss".
- **No heavy position, dissatisfied position**: There is an asymmetry between loss and return of capital (a loss of 50% requires a profit of 100%). If you make a mistake in a heavy position, you may be out of the game.
- **Position = Risk Amount ÷ Stop Loss Range**: The wider the Stop Loss, the smaller the position will be, and the narrower the position, the larger it will be. This ensures that the risk of a single Stroke is constant.
- ⚠️ "Survive" takes priority over "earn more"; this article talks about risk control methods, **no promises of returns or predictions**.

## 1. Concept introduction: What determines "how long you live" is your position.

Novices like to ask "what should I buy", while veterans are more concerned about "how much should I buy". Because the winning or losing of a single Stroke transaction is determined by the market trend, but how long you can survive in the market is largely determined by your position. **

It’s also a misunderstanding: a person who bets 5% of his capital may lose a little, but can still come back calmly; a person who bets a full position may be injured in one go, or even be out of the game. **Position management is the lesson that "determines how long you can live." **

## 2. The core of position management

Position management needs to answer two questions:

1. **How much to invest in a single Stroke**: What is the maximum risk for this Stroke transaction?
2. **Total position control**: What is the overall risk exposure of all positions added up?

The core idea is only one sentence: **Let any loss in a stroke (or a period of time) be controlled within the range that you can bear and make a comeback. ** It is not the pursuit of "making the most money with this Stroke", but ensuring that "you will not be eliminated because of a certain Stroke".

## 3. Fixed proportional risk method: only risk a little bit for each Stroke

A widely used general method is the **fixed proportion risk method**: **Only risk a fixed small proportion of the total capital for each Stroke transaction (commonly 1%–2%). **

Note - the "risk ratio" mentioned here refers to the maximum percentage of the total capital you will lose once the Stroke transaction stops and loses, not "the amount of principal invested".

For example (numbers are for illustration only): The total capital is 100,000 yuan, and the single Stroke risk is set to 1%, then the maximum tolerated loss for each Stroke transaction is **1,000 yuan**. No matter how this Stroke transaction is done, as long as Stop Loss is triggered, the loss will be controlled within 1,000 yuan (1%).

The significance of this is: **Even if you make mistakes many times in a row, your principal will only decrease slowly and will not collapse. ** After losing 10 strokes in a row, the loss is only about 10% based on 1%. The account is still there and there is still a chance.

## 4. Why can’t we have heavy or full positions?

After understanding the above, you will understand why heavy/full positions are dangerous:

- **Heavy position / Full position = Maximize the risk of single Stroke. ** One misreading may result in a loss of dozens of percentage points.
- What is even more fatal is the asymmetry between losses and capital recovery: a loss of 50% requires a profit of 100% to recover the capital; a loss of 80% requires a profit of 400%. The more serious the loss, the harder it will be to get back the money, and you may never get it back.
- Heavy positions will also **amplify emotions**: the heavier the position, the greater the impact of floating losses and floating profits on the mentality, and the easier it is to make impulsive decisions.

Therefore, "survive" always takes precedence over "earn more". ** Qingcang allows you to afford to lose, and only when you can afford to lose can you wait for the opportunity to win. **

## 5. Cooperation between positions and Stop Loss: use Stop Loss to push back positions

How to implement the fixed proportional hazards method? The key is to match the "position size" and "Stop Loss range". The formula is:

> **Position value = Single Stroke risk amount ÷ Stop Loss range (%)**

Continuing the above example: Single Stroke risk amount = 1,000 yuan (1% of total funds).

- If the Stop Loss amplitude is **5%**: Position value = 1,000 ÷ 5% = **20,000 yuan**. (If the price reverses by 5% and triggers Stop Loss, the loss will be 20,000×5% = 1,000 yuan = 1%.)
- If the Stop Loss range is narrower, only **2%**: Position value = 1,000 ÷ 2% = **50,000 yuan**.
- If the Stop Loss range is wider and has **10%**: Position value = 1000 ÷ 10% = **10000 yuan**.

An important logic can be seen: **The wider the Stop Loss, the smaller the position must be; the narrower the Stop Loss, the larger the position can be** - This can ensure that "no matter where the Stop Loss is set, the maximum loss for a single Stroke will be the same (the same 1%)". The position is not determined by patting one's head, but calculated by "how much you are willing to lose" and "how wide the stop loss is."
