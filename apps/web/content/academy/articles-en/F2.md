# Trading Plan: What to Clarify Before Every Trade

## 1. Introduction of concepts: plan first, then trade

For many losing trades, the problem lies not in "misreading" but in "making an order without thinking clearly" - as soon as the price moves, you get excited and enter the market. After entering, you start to think about "why I bought it and what to do if I lose."

Mature traders do exactly the opposite: plan first, then trade. ** Before placing an order, think clearly and finalize all aspects of the Stroke transaction. This article is about - what should you think clearly before trading a Stroke.

## 2. The significance of trading plan: advance decision-making to "calm time"

The greatest value of a trading plan is to advance key decisions from "the moment when emotions are surging when holding a position" to "when the order is not placed and the mind is calm"**.

When people hold positions and face the floating profits and losses of real money, it is difficult for people to be rational - they want to carry on when the losses are floating, and want to run away when the profits are floating. And if you have decided "when to leave and when to admit defeat" before entering the market, then you only need to **execute** instead of making temporary decisions emotionally. This is the meaning of planning: **Use rationality beforehand to confront emotions during the event. **

## 3. What does a complete trading plan include?

A trading plan should at least clearly answer the following points:

1. **Reason for Entry**: **Why** should I do this Stroke transaction? On what basis (trend, structure, signal)? ——If you can’t give a reason, you shouldn’t do it.
2. **Entry point**: At what price / under what conditions to enter the market.
3. **Stop Loss**: Where does the price go, do I admit that I was wrong and leave the market? (This is a must)
4. **Take Profit Goal**: Where does the price go for me to realize my profit?
5. **Position size**: How much is invested in this Stroke and how much risk is taken at most (see F4).
6. **Worst-case scenario**: What is my plan if an extreme situation occurs (gap, slippage).

Write down these points, and it will be clear at a glance "how big the risk is, where is the goal, and what is the worst case scenario" in a Stroke transaction.

## 4. Planned vs. Unplanned: The Fate of Two Transactions

Compare two types of traders:

- **Those without a plan**: Enter the market based on feeling → Don’t know where to stop loss → Just bear the loss and be lucky → A small loss will turn into a big loss; or run away in a hurry after making a little → Can’t catch the big trend. Decisions are driven by emotions.
- **Those with a plan**: Determine the reason, Stop Loss, target, and position before entering the market → When the market comes, just execute according to the plan → Leave when the Stop Loss is reached, and cash out when the target is reached → Emotional interference is blocked by the rules.

The difference is not "who is smarter", but who leaves the decision-making power to reason in advance rather than emotions during the matter. **

## 5. Separation of planning and execution

There is an important principle here: **Planning and execution must be separated. **

- **Planning Phase** (when calm and without holding a position): Fully analyze and clearly define the rules.
- **Execution phase** (when holding a position and making a profit or loss): You are only responsible for "doing it as planned" and **not changing the plan temporarily** due to the emotions of holding a position.

The most common mistake is to hold a position with floating losses while finding reasons for yourself to move the Stop Loss down - this is equivalent to changing your mind when you least should. **True discipline is to respect yourself in the execution stage and in the planning stage. **(F6 will also talk about discipline specifically.)

## 6. The habit of writing transaction logs

Recording the trading plan and execution process to form a trading log is a very valuable habit:

- Write down the plan (reason, Stop Loss, target, position) before entering the market;
- After leaving the venue, note down the results and "whether the execution was carried out as planned and where it deviated".

It is not only the "supervisor" of the execution (it is written in black and white, you can't rely on it), but also the original material for subsequent review (F7). If you persist for a long time, you will become more and more aware of your own patterns and problems.
