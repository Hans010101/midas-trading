## 1. Scenario setting: first have a plan, then discuss the deal

The principle of M is repeatedly emphasized by Group F - **Plan first, then trade**. Before opening a position, he first determined all the elements of the Stroke transaction in a paper plan (numerical fiction):

- **Total Funds**: 100,000 yuan;
- **Single Stroke Risk**: Using the **1% Risk Method** (F4), this Stroke** will cost up to 1% of the total funds = 1,000 yuan**;
- **Direction and Entry**: Based on analysis (trend/structure, please refer to Chan Theory and indicators), the decision is made at **100** Go Long;
- **Stop Loss**: set at **95** (drop 5% and exit);
- **Position (reverse push)**: Nominal exposure = risk amount ÷ Stop Loss amplitude = 1000 ÷ 5% = **20000 yuan**;
- **Leverage and Margin**: Use **4 times leverage** → Margin = 20000 ÷ 4 = **5000 yuan**;
- **Verify Liquidation price**: 4 times the theoretical Liquidation price of a long order ≈ 100×(1−1/4) = **75**——**Far below Stop Loss 95**, which means "Stop Loss has been triggered before Liquidation is reached", which is safe (echoing C2-2 and E9);
- **Take Profit / Exit Rules**: Take Profit in batches to the target position, or use mobile Stop Loss to follow the trend.

**This step is the soul of the entire Stroke transaction - "the worst loss is 1,000 (1%), and the Liquidation price is far behind the Stop Loss", all of which are locked before the position is opened. **

## 2. Operation process: Complete the process as planned

**① Open position**: At the planned entry point of 100, M **Open Long** as planned, the position and leverage are consistent with the plan - no temporary increase in weight, no temporary change in leverage.

**② Management (discipline in holding positions)**: This is the most testing stage. M must adhere to a few iron rules:

- **Change Stop Loss without spot**: Price fluctuations make him uncomfortable, but he **never move Stop Loss down** (that is the beginning of changing "maximum loss of 1%" into "more loss");
- **Do not increase positions at random**: When the price rises and floating profits appear, he **does not get carried away and increase positions + increase leverage** (lesson from C2-8);
- **Move Stop Loss and Take Profit in batches when necessary**: When the trend is improving, move the Stop Loss up with the price to lock in profits, and then take Profit in batches to the target (C2-6 method);
- **Pay attention to the funding fee**: If the position is held for a long time, he will include the **funding fee** into his account (C2-3) to evaluate whether long-term holding is still worthwhile.

**③ Close the position**: The price touches his **Take Profit target** (or the trend goes bad or falls below the mobile Stop Loss) → M **Close the position and leave the market according to the rules**, do not delay because of "wanting to make more", nor run around in advance due to temporary panic. **Closing a position is the same as opening a position, and is executed according to preset rules. **

**④ Review**: After the transaction, M uses the four quadrants of **F7** to review the Stroke - **Is the process correct** (direction judgment, entry, Stop Loss, discipline execution)? Is it a matter of judgment or execution? Are there any emotional disturbances? Summarize areas that can be improved and provide feedback to next time. **No matter whether the Stroke makes a profit or a loss, what is reviewed is the "process", not the "result". **

## 3. Dismantling of key nodes

**Node 1: Risk first - this is the "foundation" of Stroke trading. **
The biggest difference between M and the previous negative cases is that he first determines the "worst loss amount" and then works backwards on the position and leverage, instead of "first wanting to earn as much as possible and use as much leverage as possible." **1% risk method + Liquidation price is far behind Stop Loss**, making this Stroke transaction **risk controllable and long-lasting** from the beginning (echoing F4 and F23).

**Node 2: Leverage is a "calculated result", not a "head-beating choice". **
Note that M's 4 times leverage is derived from "exposure 20,000, margin 5,000", not "I can use it as many times as I want". In the 1% risk method, **determine the risk and Stop Loss first, and then reverse the exposure and leverage** - Leverage only determines the margin occupation and does not determine the loss of a single Stroke (the loss of a single Stroke is always 1%). This avoids the pitfall of "the leverage is too high and the Liquidation price is close to the current price" (C2-1, C2-4).

![C2-10 with picture 3](/academy-img/C2-10-配图3.png)

**Node 3: Management phase - the "antidote" to all negative cases. **
The several disciplines that M adheres to in the management stage correspond to the previous negative teaching materials one by one: **Don't change Stop Loss** (Solution to C2-2 to carry orders), **Don't add positions + leverage** (Solution to C2-8), **Don't be emotional** (Solution to C2-9), **Calculate capital fees** (Solution to C2-3). **A good Stroke trade is "controlling" 70% of the time. **

**Node 4: Review - Make progress in every Stroke. **
Regardless of profit or loss, M will review the trading process (F7). This allows him to learn from every Stroke transaction and continue to iterate, instead of "getting happy when you make money and forgetting when you lose." **Review is the key to converting trading experience into ability. **

## 4. Key points of review

This Stroke transaction connects the core of the entire training camp:

- **Risk first (1% risk method + Liquidation price is far higher than Stop Loss)**: First determine the worst loss (1%), and then work backwards on the position and leverage - this is the foundation for "long-lasting" contract trading (Group E mechanism + F4 position method);

- **Discipline is most tested in the management stage**: The "antidote" to all the previous negative cases (taking orders, adding randomly, being emotional, ignoring capital fees) lies in the discipline of the management stage;

It reduces the probability of "losing money due to confusion and emotion", rather than eliminating market uncertainty (echoing C1-20 probabilistic thinking, F38 long-termism).
