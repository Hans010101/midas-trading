# Upgrading the Central Hub Level

> Midas Academy · Chan Theory Advanced · Structure Advanced

The previous article talked about the extension, expansion, and expansion of Central Hub, and one word was mentioned repeatedly: **Upgrade**. This article is dedicated to explaining the matter of "upgrading" - how does a low-level Central Hub become a high-level Central Hub step by step? How is level improvement determined? ** This is the key to understanding Chan Theory's "level recursion" (the core of the next article).

**Quick overview of this article**

- The level is built **recursively** (K line→Stroke→Segment→Central Hub→higher level), Central Hub upgrade = Central Hub evolves into a higher level Central Hub, **according to the power of 3 recursively**.
- **Three upgrade paths**: ① **Extend to Stage 9** (no Three Types of Buy and Sell Points, 5/7 Stages are not upgraded); ② **Expansion** (out of Three Types of Buy and Sell Points, return to the Central Hub range ZG~ZD); ③ **Expansion/Half-Expansion** (return to the shock range GG~DD).
- **Theoretical Engine (Theorem)**: **Divergence of a certain level of trend will cause the level of the last Central Hub of the trend to expand** and evolve into a larger level consolidation or a higher level counter trend - upgrades are often triggered by **Divergence**.
- Practical reminder: Upgrading is always confirmed** after the fact (it is impossible to predict that you will reach 9 stages at the moment); **level ≠ time period**; don’t get obsessed with calculating the number of stages.
- ⚠️ Level/upgrade is an **analysis framework and structural judgment**. It does not predict prices and does not constitute a buying or selling order. It is recommended to refer to the original text for details.

## 1. First understand: the level is created by "building"

The levels of Chan Theory are not determined out of thin air, but are built up layer by layer, like building blocks:

- Several K lines are connected according to the rules to produce **Stroke**;
- Several Strokes, **Segment** in a row;
- Several segments (sub-level trends) overlap to form a **Central Hub**;
- The trend of this Central Hub is **one level higher** than the "sub-level".

Therefore, the essence of "level" is a **relative and recursive** concept: the Central Hub of this level is composed of the trend of **sub-level**; if the trend of this level is included in a larger structure, it becomes a higher-level sub-level... (This level of recursion will be discussed specifically in the next article C1-9.)

And "Central Hub upgrade" means: **The Central Hub that originally belonged to a certain level has evolved into a Central Hub of a higher level due to some changes. ** In Chan Theory, levels progress upwards roughly according to the power of 3 (this is why "9th stage" is a key number - it corresponds to a jump in level).

## 2. Upgrade Path 1: Extended to Stage 9

The most direct upgrade comes from the **extension** mentioned in the previous article:

- After a Central Hub is formed (the first three segments), if the Three Types of Buy and Sell Points** do not appear, the trend will repeat in the Central Hub interval;
- When repeated to **9 levels**, this Central Hub will be **upgraded** to a higher level Central Hub.
- Note: Levels 5 and 7 are still "extending" and **not upgraded**; levels must be accumulated to level 9 before the level truly jumps.

Intuitive understanding: 3 segments enclose a Central Hub of this level, and when it is repeatedly accumulated to 9 segments (approximately equal to the volume of "3 3 segments"), it forms a high-level Central Hub on a larger scale - this is the embodiment of the recursion of the power of 3.

## 3. Upgrade Paths 2 and 3: Expansion and Expansion

Another type of upgrade comes from **expansion** and **expansion** (detailed in the previous article):

- **Extension**: Three Types of Buy and Sell Points appear, and after leaving, you return to the original **Central Hub interval (ZG~ZD)** - the two Central Hub intervals overlap and are merged and upgraded;
- **Expansion (semi-expansion)**: Three Types of Buy and Sell Points appear, and the pullback only returns to the **oscillating range (GG~DD)** - the same leads to an upgrade, but in a different form.

What these two types have in common is: **Three Types of Buy and Sell Points** first appeared (the trend tried to leave for a time), and then through "turning back and entangled", the Central Hub level became larger. The biggest difference between them and "extension upgrade" is: **Extension does not produce Three Types of Buy and Sell Points in the whole process, and expansion/expansion occurs only after Three Types of Buy and Sell Points are produced. **

## 4. Upgraded "Theoretical Engine": Trend Divergence → The last Central Hub level expansion

The first three items are how to upgrade "in terms of form". But Chan Theory also gave a more essential and important **theorem-level** judgment, which linked "upgrade" and "Divergence":

> **The Divergence of a certain level of trend will lead to the level expansion of the last Central Hub of the trend** - the result is that it evolves into "a larger level of consolidation at that level" or "a higher level of counter-trend".

How to understand? After a trend (such as an upward trend, which contains at least two Central Hubs that rise in sequence) comes to an end and **Divergence** (strength failure, see C1-7) occurs, the price turns back and often gets entangled with the **last Central Hub** of the trend, increasing its level - so the low-level trend is upgraded to a high-level consolidation or counter-trend.

The reason why this theorem is important is that it reveals the driving force of upgrades: many times, level jumps do not happen in vain, but are "triggered" by the Divergence of a trend. Understanding this is the key to connecting "Structure (Central Hub/Level)" and "Divergence".

## 5. The easiest pitfalls when determining upgrades

After talking about how to upgrade, we must emphasize several points that are extremely easy to make mistakes in actual combat:

**1. Upgrades are often "confirmed after the fact". **
Especially the stretch - **You can't predict at the moment that it will definitely go to 9 paragraphs**. By the time you get to level 8, it's entirely possible that it could end there instead of making up the full 9 levels of upgrades. Therefore, "upgrade" is often a structural judgment that can only be finalized by looking back. It cannot be regarded as a prediction of "upgrade in a short period of time and you can place bets in advance."

**2. Level ≠ time period. **
This is a big pitfall, and I will talk about it in the next article: Central Hub upgrade refers to a level jump in the trend structure, which does not mean "switching from the 5-minute chart to the 30-minute chart"**. The structural level is related to the time period of viewing the chart, but it is not the same thing.

**3. Don’t get obsessed with “counting segments”. **
It is easy to fall into over-division if you are confused about "which paragraph is now and whether it is exactly 9 paragraphs" (see C1-12, C1-15). Level judgment is to understand the "size" of the trend and serve analysis, not for precise stuck points.
