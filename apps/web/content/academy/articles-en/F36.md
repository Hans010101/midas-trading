## 1. Concept introduction: Looking back, you have come a long way

Congratulations on reaching the graduation chapter.

Looking back, from the first time you got to know the K-line to now being able to discuss the trading system, contract practice, and case review - you have gone through a quite complete learning path. But I have learned a lot, but it may still be scattered in my mind: sometimes it is indicators, sometimes it is Chan Theory, sometimes it is position management...

In this article, we string together this **entire knowledge map** to help you establish a **global cognition** - see clearly what you have learned, what is the relationship between them, and **what should be remembered most after learning**.

## Review of second- and fifth-level learning paths

The entire training camp is a path of progressive progress. Let’s review it step by step:

**Level 1·Introductory foundation building (Group A). **
This is the foundation. You know: what is financial trading, how to read the K line, what does the shadow line mean, Go Long and Go Short, what is leverage, support and resistance levels, what is the trend, market orders and limit orders, Stop Loss and Take Profit, the difference between the four major markets (crypto/US stocks/A shares/Hong Kong stocks), spot and contract, and what is virtual trading. ——**These are the basic vocabulary and concepts for everything that follows. **

**Level 2 · Technical Analysis (Group B). **
You learned to use indicators to read price and momentum: Moving Average (MA), MACD (Principle / Golden CrossDeath Cross / Divergence), Bollinger Bands (BOLL / Opening and Closing), RSI (Overbought and Oversold). ——**These are tools for "reading pictures". **

**Level 3 · Chan Theory (Group C). **
You are exposed to a set of structured methods for understanding trends: K-line includes relationship, Fractal, Stroke, Segment, Central Hub, and Three Types of Buy and Sell Points. (Remember - Chan Theory's "buy and sell points" are structural terms, not operational instructions.) - **This is a more advanced "reading structure" framework. **

**Level 4 · Contracts and Derivatives (Group E). **
You understand high-risk contract tools: Perpetual Contract, Funding Rate, Cross Margin and Isolated Margin, Liquidation price mechanism, position OI, long-short ratio, basis, contract risk, and Liquidation prevention. ——**This is the first step to understand "high-risk tools". **

**Level 5 · Trading system and actual combat (Group F). **
You integrate scattered knowledge into a system and put it into practice and practice:
- **F1–F10 Trading System**: What is the system, six modules, plan / profit and loss ratio / position / psychology / discipline / review / trend and shock system / build your own system;
- **F11–F18 Strategy Types**: Trend Following / Breakout / Mean Reversion / Grid / Left and Right / No Holy Grail / Backtest Validation;
- **F19–F24 Contract Practice**: Overlay strategies with leverage and understand how leverage changes risks, positions and liquidation prevention;
- **F25–F28 Practical Case Review**: Use case study methods to understand the issues rather than the results;
- **F29–F35 Practice with Midas**: Put the method into the full virtual environment to develop abilities.

## 3. The logic of this map

Looking at the five levels together, it has a clear internal logic:

**From "understanding the market and tools" to "organizing the tools into a system and putting them into practice". **

- **A / B / C / E** (the first four levels): The solution is "**understanding**" - understanding the market, understanding the K-line and indicators, understanding the structure, and understanding the contract tools. This is **bricks, cement, steel**.
- **F** (Level 5): What is solved is "**organization and application**" - organizing the previous tools into a complete, repeatable, practical, and practiceable **system**. This is **the drawings and construction of building materials into a house**.

![F36 with picture 2](/academy-img/F36-配图2.png)

Knowledge is advanced layer by layer: without the "knowledge" of the first four levels, the "system" of the fifth level is just a castle in the air; and with only the first four levels and no fifth level, no matter how many tools you have, you can't build a house.

## 4. ★The most important thing to remember: after studying ≠ you will make money

This is a red line that must be explained clearly in the graduation chapter, and it is also something that many people will ignore:

**After learning this entire knowledge map, it does not mean that you will make money. **

- This map is the **"foundation"** of your trading ability - but **knowledge itself does not equal profitability**.
- Knowing 10,000 indicators and strategies and being able to use them stably and disciplinedly in the real market are two different things (Review F6: Know ≠ Do it).

**So, what really determines how far you can go? ** are several things besides knowledge:

- **Execution** (F6): Can you really do the right thing;
- **Discipline**: Can you follow the rules and not be carried away by emotions;
- **Risk Management** (F4): Can you control risks and survive;
- **Review Iteration** (F7): Can you learn from each Stroke transaction and continue to improve;
- **Awe of the market**: Can you always assume that you may be wrong and never gamble your life.

These are the "mentality" aspects that F37 and F38 will talk about next - they are the key to the long term. **

## 5. Knowledge is the starting point, not the end

So, think of everything you learn this way:

**After studying the training camp, what you have is a "map" - it tells you what the trading terrain looks like, what tools and paths are available. But having a map doesn’t mean you’ve finished the journey. **

True ability depends on:

- Repeated practice in **(virtual) practice** (Module 18);
- Use **discipline** to execute and **review** to improve;
-In the **long-term** practice, little by little accumulation.

The map gives you the direction, but you have to walk the road step by step.
