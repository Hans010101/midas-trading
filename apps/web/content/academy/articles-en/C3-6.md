# RSI Strategy

> Midas Academy · C3 Strategy Topic Library

RSI is the most common "overbought and oversold" indicator. But many people make big mistakes using it - because they don't know that **RSI will "fail" in trending markets**. This article explains the usage of RSI strategy and the fatal boundary.

**Quick overview of this article**

- RSI: **0–100, 50 central axis**, >70 overbought/<30 oversold (reference and threshold are not unique); **Overbought ≠ will fall soon, oversold ≠ will rise**.
- **Applicable to shock markets, but invalid in trend markets**: ★In strong trends, RSI **passivates** (long-term stop extreme zone), "overbought Go Short/oversold Go Long" will be repeatedly slapped in the face.
- Usage: Overbought and oversold** only for reference in the volatile market + with Stop Loss**; **divergence** indicates kinetic energy exhaustion (inaccurate timing, will be passivated/failed).
- Disadvantages: **Invalid in trend + Overbought and oversold non-reversal signals + Inaccurate divergence + No guarantee of profit**.

## 1. Strategy principles

RSI (relative strength index) is a **0–100** value that reflects the recent **comparison of rising and falling forces** (see B9):

- **50 is the central axis**: the upper part indicates that the upward trend is dominant, and the lower part indicates that the downward trend is dominant;
- **The overbought zone is usually >70, and the oversold zone is often <30** as a reference (the threshold is not unique).

The RSI strategy uses "what area the RSI is in and whether there is any deviation" to assist judgment. But please remember the most critical sentence (red line) first: **Overbought does not mean that it will fall immediately, and oversold does not mean that it will rise immediately - it is a reference area, not a reversal signal. **

## 2. Applicable scenarios and failure scenarios

- **Applicable: shock market. ** When the price goes back and forth in the range, the RSI is often higher when it reaches the overbought zone and lower when it reaches the oversold zone, which has certain reference significance for "return within the range".
- ★**Expired: Trend market (biggest pit). ** In a **strong trend**, RSI will be **passivated** - **staying in the overbought zone (strong rise) or oversold zone (strong fall) for a long time while the price continues to move unilaterally**. At this time, if you "Go Short when you see it is overbought, and Go Long when you see it is oversold", you will be repeatedly slapped in the face by the trend market, or even hit hard.

**RSI is "a reference for shock markets and a trap for trending markets" - you must understand the environment before using it. **

## 3. How to use (signals and rules)

1. **Overbought/Oversold (only for reference in volatile markets)**:
   - RSI enters the **overbought zone (>70)** → a reference for the dominance of the upward trend (**may be higher or lower during the shock);
   - RSI enters the **oversold zone (<30)** → a reference for the dominance of downward trend (**may be low or rebound during the shock);
   - ⚠️ Must be used in conjunction with "Is it a shock now" and with Stop Loss.
2. **Divergence (see B9, the principle is the same as MACD divergence)**:
   - **Top Divergence**: The price reaches a new high, but the RSI high point decreases → Reference for rising momentum exhaustion;
   - **Bottom divergence**: The price hits a new low, but the RSI low rises instead → Reference for downward momentum exhaustion;
   - Divergence prompts "possible reversal", but it is not precise timing (it will be blunted and may fail).

## 4. Advantages, Disadvantages and Risks

**Advantages**: **intuitive** (one number can tell the strength); **there is reference value in the shock**; divergence can indicate the exhaustion of kinetic energy.

**Disadvantages and Risks**:

- ★**Invalidation (passivation) is the biggest pitfall in the trend**: In a strong trend, RSI stays in the extreme zone for a long time, and "overbought Go Short/oversold Go Long" will be repeatedly harvested by the trend;
- **Overbought and Oversold ≠ Reversal Signal**: The most common misuse;
- **Inexact deviation**: will be passivated and may fail;
- **Profitability not guaranteed**.
