# Strokes: The Basic Unit Connecting Fractals

> Midas Academy · Level 3 · Module 10 Basic Concepts of Chan Theory

**Quick overview of this article**

- **Stroke = connects an adjacent Top Fractal and a Bottom Fractal**, which is the most basic trend unit.
- **Up Stroke** = Bottom Fractal → Top Fractal; **Down Stroke** = Top Fractal → Bottom Fractal; the direction of Stroke **alternates up and down** and will not continue in the same direction.
- The core of the **establishment conditions** of Stroke is: there must be **independent K lines between the top and bottom fractals, separated and not shared**; there are two sets of standards for specific tightness - **Old Stroke** (at least 1 independent K line between top and bottom / at least 5 K lines in total) and **New Stroke** (at least 3 K lines between top and bottom), but "no shared K lines" is the common bottom line of both.
- Drawing Stroke is a structural description, not a prediction; Stroke does not promise future direction.

## 1. Concept introduction: connecting Fractal

In the previous article, we learned to identify the Top Fractal and Bottom Fractal on the K-line sequence. These Fractals are isolated "local turning points", and **Stroke** is the most basic trend unit formed by connecting the adjacent Top Fractal and Bottom Fractal**.

If Fractal is a "point", then Stroke is the "line" connecting these points - it is the first step for Chan Theory to move from "point" to "structure".

## 2. The definition of Stroke: connecting an adjacent Top Fractal and a Bottom Fractal

The definition of **Stroke** is: **Connect an adjacent Top Fractal and a Bottom Fractal. **

- There are two endpoints of a Stroke, one is Top Fractal and the other is Bottom Fractal (one high and one low).
- Stroke has direction:
  - **Up Stroke** = Connect from **Bottom Fractal** to **Top Fractal** (from low to high).
  - **Down Stroke** = Connect from **Top Fractal** to **Bottom Fractal** (from high to low).

## 3. The conditions for the establishment of Stroke: the top and bottom must be "separable"

Not just any Top Fractal and a Bottom Fractal coming together can form a Stroke. Stroke has **establishment conditions**, and the core idea is: **There must be enough "independent K lines" between the adjacent Top Fractal and Bottom Fractal to separate them, and they cannot stick or overlap. **

Intuitive understanding: If a Top Fractal and a Bottom Fractal are too close to each other, or even share the K line, then the "top" and "bottom" will not be clear, and the connected "Stroke" will be meaningless. The requirement to have an independent K-line in the middle is to ensure that the top and bottom are the two turning points that are truly separated.

### Old Stroke and New Stroke: Two sets of common standards

Regarding "how many independent K-lines are needed between two Fractals", Chan Theory's original work has given **two sets of standards**, which are often referred to as "**Old Stroke**" and "**New Stroke**" in circulation. The only difference between them is the number of K lines, which is worth knowing:

- **Old Stroke (original definition)**: Between Top Fractal and Bottom Fractal, there must be at least 1 independent K line** to separate them. Converted into the total number of K lines: From the highest K line of Top Fractal to the lowest K line of Bottom Fractal (all processed by inclusion), there must be at least 5 K lines** (Top Fractal 3 + Middle connection + Bottom Fractal 3, a total of 5 after overlapping connection), otherwise it will not be a Stroke.
- **New Stroke (relaxed definition)**: The author later felt that the old Stroke was sometimes too harsh, so he relaxed it slightly - on the premise that "the top Fractal does not share K lines", it is required that there be at least 3 K lines between the **Top Fractal highest K line and the Bottom Fractal lowest K line (not including these two)**. This is slightly looser than the old Stroke.

**The two sets of standards have a common bottom line that must not be relaxed: after Top Fractal and Bottom Fractal are included, they are not allowed to share K lines** (there cannot be a certain K line that belongs to both Top Fractal and Bottom Fractal at the same time). This is to ensure that a Stroke has enough "strength". This holds true no matter which set of standards is used.

> In practice, the old Stroke (at least 1 independent K-line between the top and bottom / 5 in total) is the most widely circulated and most often regarded as the "standard Stroke"; many market-reading software also allows switching between the old Stroke / new Stroke. **Which set to adopt, it is recommended to keep it consistent in your analysis** - the important thing is not to get entangled in a certain number, but to understand the essence of "the top and bottom must be separated and cannot share the K line" (this also echoes what was said in the previous article: understand the logic, rather than memorize the numbers).

## 4. How to draw Stroke: Alternate up and down

When drawing a Stroke on a trend, follow a clear pattern - **The direction of the Stroke alternates up and down**:

Bottom Fractal → (Stroke up) → Top Fractal → (Stroke down) → Bottom Fractal → (Stroke up) → Top Fractal…

In other words: after an upward Stroke, there must be a downward Stroke; and vice versa. **It is impossible for two consecutive Strokes to go in the same direction** (because the Stroke is connected to the top Bottom Fractal, which alternates between high and low). By connecting these strokes, the original messy K-line is turned into a clear, alternating up and down polyline skeleton.

This skeleton is the basis for higher-level structures such as "Segment" and "Central Hub" later.

## 5. Key reminders

As with all previous concepts, it must be emphasized:

- **Drawing Stroke is "describing the structure", not "predicting". ** When a Stroke goes up, it does not mean that it will continue to rise; it is just a structured description of a trend that has already occurred.
- Top Fractal, Bottom Fractal, and Stroke all describe the local structures that have been formed. Chan Theory uses them to understand the "skeleton" of the trend, but cannot promise future direction based on them.
