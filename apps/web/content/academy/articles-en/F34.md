## 1. Concept introduction: After learning a bunch of other people’s methods, what next?

At this point, you can use tools to read structures (F30), use virtual transactions to practice execution (F31), use AI as a reference (F32), and use backtesting to polish rules (F33). But there is still one last step left, and it is also the step that many people get stuck on:

**How to develop these "learned, other people's" methods into "our own, stable execution" style of play? **

F10 said that "there is no best, only what suits you" and "from imitation to originality". In this article, I will turn it into a practice path that can be actually followed in Midas - taking you from imitation to forming your own method.

## 2. A practice path (in the Midas virtual environment)

This path is a **loop** that can be walked repeatedly in the full virtual environment of Midas at zero cost:

**Step one: observe the structure (imitation learning). **
Use Midas' tools (F30) to observe a lot of historical and current market structures and learn how mature methods (trends, breakthroughs, shocks, mean reversion, etc.) are used in real charts. This is the "imitation" stage - first understand and become familiar with other people's methods.

**Step 2: Small-scale virtual trial. **
Pick a strategy/style of play you want to try, and use virtual trading to try it with a small position** (continue F31) and see if you are comfortable with it and whether you can bear it**. This step is to "touch it with your own hands" - just seeing it is not enough, you have to use it yourself to know whether it is suitable.

**Step 3: Review. **
Use **F7’s four quadrants + transaction log** to review these virtual transactions: is the process correct? Is it a matter of judgment or execution? Where does this style of play suit me and where does it make me uncomfortable? (For example, does the required holding time, Stop Loss range, and frequency of market tracking suit my personality and time)?

**Step 4: Adjust. **
Based on the review, leave what suits you best and remove the awkwardness, and adjust the rules and parameters (you can use the Strategy Lab backtest F33 to assist in verifying the direction of adjustment).

**Step 5: Develop a stable and executable style of play. **
Repeatedly "** try → review → adjust**", slowly, a set of methods with your own imprint and that you can execute stably will be precipitated (echoing F10's "original").

The key word of this path is **loop** - it is not done once, but it is iterated round by round.

## 3. ★Key: What is formed is a method that is "suitable for you + can be executed stably"

To take this path, the goal must be set correctly (continue F10, F17):

**What you are looking for is not "the strongest strategy", but "a method that I can execute stably and match my own." **

- Match your** personality, available time, financial status, and risk tolerance** (F10);
- **"Able to perform stably" > "It's beautiful in theory but you can't do it"**: A method that has a beautiful win rate and profit-loss ratio, but requires you to keep an eye on the market all day long/operate frequently/carry large retracements. If you can't do it, it will be ineffective for you. A good method is the one that allows you to execute it in a long-term, stable, and non-awkward manner.

Remember the words of F17: **There is no Holy Grail. ** Don't fall into the trap of "looking for the strongest method" again on this path - what you are looking for is "the one that suits you best", not the "strongest".

## 4. ★The Boundary of Honesty

This path can help you form a method, but there are two boundaries that must be explained honestly:

**1. What is formed is a method, not a "guaranteed profit formula". **
Even if you go through this path and form a method that suits you and can be executed stably - it still does not guarantee profitability. No matter how good the method is, it can only improve the long-term probability of winning and stability, but it cannot eliminate market uncertainty**. Don't expect to develop a "guaranteed winning style", that doesn't exist.

**2. Practice well in the virtual world, but still remain in awe of the real market. **
This path is taken in a **virtual environment**. However, the real market has real financial pressure, stronger emotions, and more complex realities (slippage, liquidity, etc.), which cannot be completely replicated by virtual markets. **Methods formed in the virtual world must still be awe-inspiring and carefully verified when switching to the real market** - **Virtual results do not represent real results**.

## 5. This is a continuous path (continued from F10 iteration)

Finally, I emphasize: **The formation method is not the end point. **

- Markets will change and methods that were once suitable may need to be adjusted;
- You yourself will also grow, and your understanding of risks and the time you can invest will change;
- Therefore, this path of "observation → trial → review → adjustment → formation" must be continued** - let your method iterate and grow with you and the market (echoing F7 and F10).
