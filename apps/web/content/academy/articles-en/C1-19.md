# What to Do When Trade Signals Fail

> Midas Academy · Chan Theory Advanced · Advanced Special Topics

**Buying and selling points will fail** - This is a fact that students of Chan Theory must accept. 1. After buying, it continues to reach new lows, and 3. After buying, it falls back to the Central Hub. It is too common. However, many people regard the buying and selling point as "inevitable cashing". Once they fail, they will be confused, carry on, or even double the price. This article talks about: How to identify the failure of buying and selling points, and how to deal with them if they fail.

**Quick overview of this article**

- **The buying and selling point is a structural concept and will fail**, it is not a signal of inevitable success or an operation instruction.
- Various types of failure identification: **1 after buying and falling below 1 buying low**, **2 buying back and pulling below the previous low**, **3 buying back and pulling back to Central Hub (breaking ZG)**; selling point mirroring (such as 3 selling and pulling back and pulling back to Central Hub breaking ZD).
- The nature of the failure: **Wrong level (level mismatch)** or **Divergence misjudgment**; more fundamentally, the buying and selling point is **probability judgment, non-guaranteed**, and some of them are destined to fail.
- Three steps to deal with: **Stop Loss/Admit your mistake (highest priority, don’t bear it) → Review (wrong judgment/execution of four quadrants) → No retaliatory trading**.
- Mentality: Failure is part of the game of probability; a master is "Strictly Stop Loss when failure occurs and control the single cost."
- ⚠️ The buying and selling points are an analysis framework/probability reference, not buying and selling instructions, and are not guaranteed**; be sure to cooperate with Stop Loss and risk management.

## 1. Reiterate: Buying and selling points are structural concepts, not guarantees

First nail the red line:

> Three Types of Buy and Sell Points are terms/analysis concepts that describe the **trend structure position**, **not a "sure signal of success", let alone an operation instruction**. It will fail. **

This line runs through the entire training camp. Admitting that "the buying and selling point will fail" does not deny Chan Theory, but is precisely the premise of making good use of Chan Theory - because Chan Theory gives **structure and probability** (C1-20), not certainty.

## 2. How to identify the "failure" of various buying and selling points

Each type of buying and selling point has a clear "invalidation" sign (mirror of selling point):

- **1 buy failed**: After 1 buy, the price**continues to reach new lows (falling below the low of 1 buy)** - indicating that the decline has not ended (it is likely that Divergence's judgment was wrong, or this is just a small rebound in a larger decline).
- **2 buy invalid**: The one bought by 2 pulled back and ** fell below the previous low** - the condition of the 2 buy "withdrawing without breaking the previous low" is no longer met, and the structure is invalid.
- **3 buy invalid**: After the 3 buy, the pullback **falls back to the Central Hub range (breaking ZG)** - indicating that the upward breakthrough has not been established, and this 3 buy is not established (it is likely to evolve into an extension/expansion of Central Hub, see C1-2).

The selling point is a complete mirror image (for example, 3 sells failed = counterattacked and rose back to Central Hub, breaking ZD).

## 3. What is the essence of failure?

Why do buying and selling points fail? The two most common root causes:

1. **Wrong level** (continued from C1-6 level mismatch): You think it is a large-level buying and selling point, but in fact it is only a small-level one - it is quickly overwhelmed by **larger-level trends**. Use small-level buying and selling points to expand the market situation, and failure is the norm.
2. **Divergence is wrongly judged** (continued from C1-7): Divergence is originally a strength reference and will be blunted; if you misread "still creating strength" as "strength failure", the buying and selling points found by Divergence based on this error will naturally be ineffective.

More essentially: ** buying and selling points are "probability judgments based on the current structure", not guaranteed** (C1-20). Since it is probability, some of it is bound to fail - this is not Chan Theory's fault, it is the normal state of probability games.

## 4. If it fails, how to deal with it?

This is the most practical part. When the buying and selling point fails, the correct response is:

**First place: Stop Loss/Admit your mistake. **
Stop Loss should be set when entering the field (Group F said this repeatedly). **Once the buying and selling point expires, Stop Loss is the only correct response** - don't hold on to it, don't expect it to "come back". The failure of the buying and selling point means that "the structure you judged has not been realized", and continuing to hold on will only drag a small loss into a big loss. **This one is the most important. **

**The second position: review, distinguishing between wrong judgment and wrong execution. **
Use F7's four-quadrant review: This failure is a **level mismatch** (wrong judgment)? **Divergence misjudgment** (wrong judgment)? Or is the structure inherently vague and should not be introduced? Distinguish whether it is a "method/judgment" problem or an "execution" problem, and make symptomatic improvements (echoing C1-11, F7).

**Third position: No retaliatory transactions. **
If a buying and selling point fails, it does not mean that you should immediately backhand and double your bets. Getting emotional and wanting to "get it back" after failure is the retaliatory trading trap mentioned by F5 - it will only make the losses bigger. If it fails, calm down, stop loss, review, and wait for a clear structure instead of rushing for revenge.

## 5. Correct mentality: Failure is part of the game of probability

The last thing is mentality. **Failure at buying and selling points is normal and part of the game of probability** (C1-20):

- What masters say is not "never fail at buying and selling points"** (no one can do that), but "strictly stop loss when failure occurs and control the cost of a single failure"**;
- What really widens the gap is not "the number of correct judgments", but "the loss is less when the judgment is wrong, and the ability to hold on when the judgment is right"** (echoing the profit-loss ratio and risk management of Group F).

If you use the buying and selling point as a "structural probability signal + strict risk control", it is a good tool; if you use it as a "guarantee that it will be fulfilled", sooner or later you will be hit hard by a failure.
