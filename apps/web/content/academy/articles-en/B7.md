# Bollinger Bands (BOLL): A Channel of Volatility

> Midas Academy · Phase 2 · Module 6 Classic Technical Indicators

**Quick Overview**

- **Three Bollinger Bands**: Middle Band (Moving Average, commonly 20-period), Upper Band, Lower Band.
- **Upper/Lower Bands = Middle Band ± K times Standard Deviation** (commonly K = 2), symmetrically distributed on either side of the Middle Band.
- **Standard Deviation Reflects Volatility**: High volatility means a wide channel; low volatility means a narrow channel — Bollinger Bands "breathe."
- Prices **mostly run within the channel** (not an absolute rule); **touching the Upper/Lower Bands is a positional reference, not a reversal signal** (in strong trends, prices can run along the bands).

## I. Concept Introduction: Drawing a "Breathing" Channel for Price

The Moving Averages (MA) you learned earlier are a single line. **Bollinger Bands (BOLL)** go a step further — on top of the Moving Average, they draw a **fluctuating channel** for price: one line in the middle, and two boundary lines above and below, with prices mostly running within this channel.

Even better, the width of this channel **automatically changes** with market volatility — it widens when volatility is high and narrows when volatility is low, like it is "breathing." This section clarifies the structure and origin of the three bands of Bollinger Bands.

## II. The Three Bands: Middle, Upper, and Lower

Bollinger Bands consist of three lines, commonly known as the "Three Bands":

- **Middle Band**: A **Moving Average** (most commonly the 20-period MA). It is the "Central Hub" of the entire channel.
- **Upper Band**: The boundary line **above** the Middle Band.
- **Lower Band**: The boundary line **below** the Middle Band.

The Upper, Middle, and Lower Bands are arranged from top to bottom, enclosing a channel that wraps around the price.

## III. How the Three Bands Are Derived: Middle Band = MA, Upper/Lower Bands = Middle Band ± Standard Deviation

This is the core of Bollinger Bands and the most important part to remember accurately. The algorithm for the three bands is:

- **Middle Band = N-period Moving Average** (commonly N = 20).
- **Upper Band = Middle Band + K times Standard Deviation**.
- **Lower Band = Middle Band − K times Standard Deviation**.

Where K is most commonly **2** (i.e., Upper/Lower Bands = Middle Band ± 2 times Standard Deviation).

The key here is the concept of "**Standard Deviation**." Standard Deviation is a statistical measure of **price volatility (degree of dispersion)**:

- The **greater the price volatility**, the larger the Standard Deviation, the further the Upper and Lower Bands are from the Middle Band, and the **wider the channel**.
- The **smaller the price volatility**, the smaller the Standard Deviation, the closer the Upper and Lower Bands are to the Middle Band, and the **narrower the channel**.

This is the reason Bollinger Bands "breathe": **the width of the channel directly reflects the current market volatility** (the next section "Opening and Closing" will specifically cover this). Note that the Upper and Lower Bands are **symmetrically** distributed on either side of the Middle Band (with the same K times Standard Deviation), not arbitrarily drawn lines.

## IV. Prices Mostly Run Within the Channel

A statistical characteristic of Bollinger Bands is: **prices mostly run within the channel formed by the Upper and Lower Bands.**

Intuitive understanding: The Upper and Lower Bands are drawn based on "Middle Band ± 2 times Standard Deviation," and statistically speaking, a significant proportion of price fluctuations fall within the range of the mean plus/minus 2 times Standard Deviation. Therefore, prices usually oscillate within the channel, occasionally touching or briefly breaching the Upper and Lower Bands.

What does touching the Upper and Lower Bands imply? It can be understood as the following **reference**:

- Price **touches the Upper Band**: Indicates the price has run to the **upper end** of the recent volatility range, relatively strong / high.
- Price **touches the Lower Band**: Indicates the price has reached the **lower end** of the recent volatility range, relatively weak / low.

However, it is crucial to emphasize an extremely important reminder: **Touching the Upper and Lower Bands is only a "positional reference," absolutely not a "reversal signal."** In a strong trend, the price is completely capable of **running continuously along the Upper Band (or Lower Band)** (commonly known as "running along the track") — at this time, touching the Upper Band does not mean it will fall, and touching the Lower Band does not mean it will rise. Furthermore, price distribution is not a perfect normal distribution, so "mostly running within the channel" is a **tendency, not an absolute rule**.
