# KDJ Indicator: Three Lines of the Stochastic Oscillator

KDJ, also known as the Stochastic Oscillator, measures the relative position of the current closing price within a recent price range. Composed of three lines—K, D, and J—it is capable of identifying overbought and oversold conditions as well as crossover signals, making it one of the most frequently used oscillators.

## How KDJ is Calculated

KDJ calculation involves three steps: first calculating RSV, then smoothing to get K and D, and finally amplifying to get J.

**Step 1: RSV (Raw Stochastic Value)**

`RSV = (C − Lₙ) / (Hₙ − Lₙ) × 100`

Where C is the current closing price, and Lₙ and Hₙ are the lowest and highest prices over the last n days (commonly n=9). RSV measures the position of the closing price within the high-low range of the last n days: the closer the closing price is to the top of the range, the higher the RSV (approaching 100); the closer to the bottom, the lower the RSV (approaching 0).

**Step 2: K and D**

- `K = SMA(RSV, 3)`: Smooths the RSV over a 3-period cycle, making it more stable than RSV.
- `D = SMA(K, 3)`: Smooths the K line again, making it slower and more stable than K.

**Step 3: J**

`J = 3K − 2D`

J amplifies the deviation between K and D. It is the most sensitive of the three lines and often turns first.

The common parameters are (9, 3, 3), meaning RSV is calculated over 9 days, and K and D are each smoothed over 3 periods.

## How to Read KDJ

### Overbought and Oversold

When K and D run above 80, it is typically considered the overbought zone; when below 20, it is considered the oversold zone.

But remember: **Overbought does not mean "sell now," and oversold does not mean "buy now."** This merely describes the price's position within the recent range and serves as a reference, not a reversal signal. See the "Stagnation" section below for the reason.

### Golden Cross and Death Cross

- **KDJ Golden Cross**: K line crosses above D line → Bullish reference.
- **KDJ Death Cross**: K line crosses below D line → Bearish reference.

Note that this refers to the crossover of the K and D lines. It is different from "MA Golden Cross" (two price MAs, such as MA5 crossing above MA20) or "MACD Golden Cross" (DIF crossing above DEA)—all three are called "Golden Cross," but they determine completely different lines. KDJ's golden and death crosses describe structure and are references, not trading orders.

### Characteristics of the J Value

J is the most sensitive and often leads K and D in turning. A point that often causes confusion is: **J values can exceed 100 or fall below 0**. K and D are constrained between 0~100, but J is calculated as `3K − 2D`, which can break out of this range—this is a normal phenomenon, not a data error.

## Two Common Pitfalls

### Stagnation

In strong trends, KDJ will run repeatedly in the overbought zone (during continuous rises) or the oversold zone (during continuous falls). This is called stagnation. At this time, "overbought" may persist for a long time while the price continues to rise, and "oversold" may persist for a long time while the price continues to fall.

**Stagnation does not imply an immediate reversal.** Treating overbought and oversold as mechanical reversal signals will lead to repeated losses in trending markets. This principle is the same as with RSI stagnation.

### Signals are References, Not Orders

KDJ's golden cross, death cross, overbought, and oversold conditions all describe price structure and are analytical references, not trading orders. No single indicator constitutes a complete basis for trading, and there is no "guaranteed win" method; indicators must be viewed in conjunction with trends, other tools, and risk control.

## Summary

KDJ uses RSV to measure the position of the closing price within the recent high-low range, obtains K and D through two smoothings (common parameters 9, 3, 3), and amplifies sensitivity with J. When reading it, remember three things:

- Overbought and oversold are positional references, not reversal signals;
- Stagnation occurs in strong trends; sticking to overbought/oversold zones does not mean an immediate reversal;
- J breaking out of 0~100 is normal.

Finally, don't forget: KDJ Golden Cross, MA Golden Cross, and MACD Golden Cross are three different "Golden Crosses," and the lines they determine are different, so do not confuse them.
