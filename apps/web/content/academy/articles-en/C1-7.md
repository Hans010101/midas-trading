# How to Identify Divergence More Precisely

> Midas Academy · Chan Theory Advanced · Practical Practice of Buying and Selling Points

[Divergence](/academy/glossary#Divergence) is the "engine" of [Chan Theory buying and selling point](/academy/glossary#ChanTheoryBuy/SellPoints) (especially 1 buy/1 sell) - without Divergence, there would be no trend turning. But "Divergence" is also the place that is most likely to be confused and misjudged. This article explains how to use **MACD yellow and white lines + red and green column areas** to judge Divergence relatively accurately, and what is the difference between **Trend Divergence and Consolidation Divergence**.

Put the most important sentence first:

> ⚠️ **Divergence ≠ Reversal immediately. ** Divergence describes "depletion of strength in the same direction" and is a **velocity reference**, not a switch that "turns around immediately after Divergence". Divergence may then reverse, or it may only have a slight correction, or even continue to "passivate". The method in this article is an **analysis framework** and does not make predictions or constitute buying or selling orders.

**Quick overview of this article**

- **Divergence = The strength of the same trend is exhausted** (the comparison is strength, not price; the price can reach new highs/new lows, but the strength is not enough).
- **MACD Divergence**: ①**The yellow and white lines (DIF/DEA) pull back** (the high point does not reach a new high, but pulls back toward the zero axis); ②**The area of ​​the red and green columns shrinks** (adjacent to the same direction, the area of ​​the latter column is significantly smaller than the previous section; if it rises, look at the red column, and if it falls, look at the green column). The area method is more reliable than column height.
- **Trend Divergence** (occurring in the trend) is of great significance, creating a type 1 buying and selling point, and may be a big turning point; **Consolidation Divergence** (occurring in the consolidation) is of little significance, and most likely only returns to the Central Hub - do not confuse the two.
- ⚠️ **Divergence ≠ Immediate reversal**, it is a reference for **strength failure**; it will be **passivated**; MACD is an approximate tool; it needs further confirmation of the buying and selling point structure, it is not a prediction, and it is not a buying and selling order.

## 1. What is Divergence: exhaustion of strength

**Divergence refers to the middle and later stages of a trend. The "strength" of the trend in the same direction is obviously weaker than that of the previous stage - it is not strong enough. **

Note that Divergence compares "**strength**", not "price". Prices can continue to make new highs (up) or new lows (down), but if the force driving it is weakening, that's divergence. It's like a person running and sprinting: still rushing forward (the price hits a new high), but the steps are getting weaker and weaker and obviously losing energy (the strength is exhausted) - this is a signal for "Divergence".

How to quantify "intensity"? In Chan Theory's actual combat, the most commonly used tool is **MACD**.

## 2. MACD judges Divergence: the yellow and white lines pull back + the area of the red and green columns shrinks

Use MACD to judge Divergence and look at two things (take the "top Divergence" at the end of the rise as an example, and the bottom Divergence mirror):

**1. The yellow and white lines (DIF, DEA) pull back. **
The price reached a new high, but the high point of MACD's yellow and white lines (DIF / DEA two lines) did not reach a new high simultaneously, but instead pulled back to the zero axis (or closer to the zero axis) - indicating that the "intrinsic strength" of this wave of rise has not kept up with the price, which is a sign of exhaustion.

**2. The area of ​​red and green columns is reduced (area method, key). **
This is a more subtle move. Compare the MACD pillars corresponding to the two adjacent periods of the same trend in the trend to the "**area**":

- Use **red bars** for increases (DIF is above DEA), and **green bars** for decreases;
- Top Divergence: The price hit a new high in the last period, but the corresponding ** red column area is significantly smaller than the previous period** - the intensity is shrinking, Divergence;
- Bottom Divergence: The price hit a new low in the last period, but the corresponding green column area was significantly smaller than the previous period - Divergence.

"Area" is more reliable than looking at column height alone, because it includes both the "intensity" and "duration" of force.

## 3. Trend Divergence vs Consolidation Divergence

This is the key analysis of this article. The same "strength failure" occurs in a **trend** or a **consolidation**, and the meaning is completely different:

**Trend Divergence**:

- Occurs in **trend** (≥2 Central Hubs in the same direction, see C1-4);
- What is compared is the strength of the segment **leaving the last Central Hub** in the trend and the segment **entering the last Central Hub** (or the adjacent segments in the same direction in the trend);
- Its meaning is **strong**: Trend Divergence will create **the first type of buying and selling points**, which often correspond to **larger level turning** (echoing the C1-3 theorem: trend Divergence → finally Central Hub level expansion → larger level consolidation or counter-trend).

**Divergence (also called Divergence)**:

- Occurs during **consolidation** (only 1 Central Hub);
- What is compared is the strength of two trends in the same direction, **entering the Central Hub** and **leaving the Central Hub**;
- Its meaning is **weak**: consolidation divergence usually only results in price **returning** to the Central Hub** (retracing within the range), and **not a reversal** at the trend level.

One sentence to distinguish: ** Trend Divergence occurs in "trend" and is of great significance (maybe a big turning point); Consolidation Divergence occurs in "consolidation" and has little significance (mostly just returning to the Central Hub). ** In the strict sense of Chan Theory, "Divergence" specifically refers to trend Divergence; when the strength of the consolidation is exhausted, it should be called "Consolidation Divergence". Don't confuse it with one thing, let alone use the reverse of the consolidation as a big turning point to move heavy positions.

## 4. Divergence’s easiest pitfalls

Let’s focus on several high-frequency errors:

- **Divergence ≠ Immediate reversal** (emphasized again): Divergence only means "power failure", and the reversal may come late or not at all. We need to wait for further confirmation of the buying and selling point structure (such as 1 buy followed by 2 buys) instead of "copying as soon as we see Divergence".
- **Passivation**: In a strong trend, MACD may continue to "seem to be Divergence but not Divergence" (passivation). If you judge Divergence too early, you will be repeatedly slapped in the face.
- **Select the right segments for comparison**: The area method must compare two segments that are "adjacent, in the same direction, and comparable". If you compare the wrong segments, the conclusion will be invalid.
- **MACD is only an auxiliary, the area method is an approximation**: The essence of Divergence is strength, MACD is just a commonly used and approximate measurement tool, not the only standard, nor absolutely accurate.

> ⚠️ The precise determination of Divergence (which two segments are compared, how to calculate the area, and how to correspond to different levels) has a lot of particularities and differences in Chan Theory. This article gives a commonly used caliber in actual combat, as an **analysis framework** for understanding; it is recommended to refer to the original text for details, do not apply mechanically, and do not regard "Divergence" as a reversal signal that guarantees a profit.
