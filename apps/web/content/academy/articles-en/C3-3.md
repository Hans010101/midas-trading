# Trend Following Strategy

> Midas Academy · C3 Strategy Topic Library

Moving Average and MACD are "tools", while trend following is a **complete strategic idea** - the skeleton of many successful trading systems is trend following. This article explains its logic, approach and boundaries.

**Quick overview of this article**

- Trend following = **Follow the formed trend, do not predict the top or bottom**; the essence is "**cut losses and let profits run**"; rely on **high profit-loss ratio** to win, not rely on high winning rate.
- **Applicable to unilateral trends, invalid for shocks**; lagging at turning points (highest without selling/lowest without buying).
- Method: **Identify the trend → Enter the market with the trend (callback/breakthrough) → Move Stop Loss to follow → Leave the market if the trend goes bad**.
- Advantages include being able to take advantage of the general trend/not guessing the top and bottom/good profit-loss ratio; Disadvantages** low winning rate (need to tolerate continuous small losses) + shock and wear + lag + no guarantee of profit**.

## 1. Strategy principles

The core belief of trend following is: Once a trend is formed, it often has inertia and will continue for a period of time; instead of predicting the top or bottom, it is better to follow the trend that has already formed. **

It has a widely circulated essence: **"Cut the loss and let the profit run."** - If you are wrong, quickly Stop Loss (cut the loss), if you are right, let the trend continue to run with profits (profit amplification). Trend following does not pursue a high winning rate, but relies on the profits from a few major trends to cover multiple small losses (high profit-loss ratio).

## 2. Applicable scenarios and failure scenarios

- **Applicable to: unilateral trend market. ** When there is a clear direction (continuous rise/fall), trend following can capture the "main rise/main fall period".
- **Invalidation: Shocking market conditions. ** There is no trend to follow, and the price goes back and forth in the range. Trend following will lead to repeated entry and Stop Loss (small losses again and again), and serious wear and tear (echoing C2-5 and F12).
- **Lagging at the turning point**: Trend following naturally leaves the market after the reversal, and **will not sell at the highest and buy at the lowest**.

## 3. How to use (signals and rules)

Common processes for trend tracking:

1. **Identify trend**: Use high and low point structure (high points and low points rise = rise), Moving Average direction/arrangement, etc. to determine the current trend (C3-1, A7);
2. **Enter with the trend**: Do not go against the trend, do not guess the top or bottom; there are two common types of entry - **Entry with callback** (the trend will stabilize after a correction and then enter with the trend) or **Enter with the breakthrough** (enter with the trend after breaking through the key position, C3-4);
3. **Move Stop Loss to follow**: After entering the market, move the Stop Loss** in the direction of the trend** (locking part of the floating profit and giving space to the trend);
4. **Exit when the trend goes bad**: When the trend structure turns bad (falls below the moving Stop Loss/Key Moving Average, the high point no longer rises) → exit.

The whole process is the specific implementation of "**cut off loss (Stop Loss) + let profits run (move Stop Loss to follow)**".

## 4. Advantages, Disadvantages and Risks

**Advantages**:

- Be able to capture the main profits of the general trend;
- **No need to predict the top or bottom** (follow the trend, less psychological pressure);
- **Good profit-loss ratio** (small loss, huge profit).

**Disadvantages and Risks**:

- ★**Low winning rate**: Trend following is often "right less often and wrong more often" (many small losses), relying on a few big profits to win - **You must be able to tolerate continuous small losses**, otherwise all efforts will be wasted if you give up midway;
- **Stop Loss repeatedly during shocks**: Continuous wear and tear when there is no trend;
- **Lag**: If the entry is later than the starting point and the exit is later than the top, the head and tail will inevitably be given up;
- **No guarantee of profit**: No matter how good the trend tracking is, it will encounter continuous losses in a volatile market.
