# Bollinger Bands Strategy

> Midas Academy · C3 Strategy Topic Library

Bollinger Bands is a "two-purpose chart" indicator - it can be used both for mean reversion in a volatile market and for breakthroughs when the market changes. This article explains its definition, two uses and applicable boundaries.

**Quick overview of this article**

- Bollinger Bands: **Middle band = moving average (20), upper/lower band = middle band ± K times the standard deviation (K=2, symmetrical)**; "breathe" with the fluctuations.
- Two usages: **mean reversion (shock market: hitting the upper track higher/touching the lower track lower, returning to the middle track)** and **breakthrough (opening breakthrough after closing, starting the trend)**; **judge the environment first and then choose the method**.
- ★The biggest pitfall: **Mean reversion will be ineffective in a strong trend** (the price will not return if it moves along the track); **Tracking the track is a position reference, not a reversal signal**.

## 1. Strategy principles

Bollinger Bands (BOLL) consist of three tracks (see B7, ★ definition must be accurate):

- **Middle track = Moving Average** (commonly used is 20 periods);
- **Upper rail/Lower rail = middle rail ± K times the standard deviation** (K is commonly used **2**), **symmetrically** distributed on both sides of the middle rail.

**Standard deviation reflects fluctuations**: If the fluctuations are large, the channel will be wide; if the fluctuations are small, the channel will be narrow - so Bollinger Bands will "**breathe**" (opening/closing, see B8) with the market. Prices move within channels most of the time (tendency, non-iron law).

The Bollinger Bands strategy uses "the position of the price relative to the channel" and "changes in the width of the channel" to assist decision-making. It has two almost opposite usages, corresponding to different environments.

## 2. Applicable scenarios and failure scenarios

The key to Bollinger Bands is to first judge the environment and then decide which usage to use:

- **Mean Reversion Usage → Applicable to shock markets**: In shocks, when the price hits the upper track, it is higher, and when it hits the lower track, it is lower, and there is a tendency to return to the middle track.
- **Breakthrough Usage → Applicable to market changes after closing**: After a long period of closing (fluctuation convergence), the price opens and the price breaks through the track, which may start a trend.
- ★** Failure (the biggest pitfall): Use "mean reversion" in strong trends. ** In a strong trend, the price will move all the way along the upper track (or lower track) and will not return to the middle track. At this time, if you "go short when it touches the upper track and go long when it touches the lower track", you will be repeatedly slapped in the face by the trend.

## 3. How to use (two ways to use)

**Usage 1·Mean Reversion (Shocking Market):**
- The price touches/is close to the **upper track** → a higher reference (it may fall back to the middle track during the shock);
- The price touches/is close to the **lower track** → a low reference (it may rise back to the middle track during the shock);
- ⚠️ **Tracking is a "position reference", not a "reversal signal"** - strong trends will follow the track, and must be coordinated with environmental judgment and Stop Loss.

**Usage 2·Breakthrough (change plate):**
- **Narrowing** (narrowing of the upper and lower rails) = fluctuations converge, often "preparing for changes";
- **Opening after closing + Price breaks through the track** → May start a trend (refer to the direction of the breakthrough, echoing C3-4);
- ⚠️ After closing, the **direction is unpredictable** - it prompts "to change the market", but does not prompt "which direction to change".

## 4. Advantages, Disadvantages and Risks

**Advantages**: **Intuitive** (the price relative to the channel is clear at a glance); **Adaptive fluctuations** (the channel breathes); **One picture for two purposes** (regression + breakthrough).

**Disadvantages and Risks**:

- ★**If you use the wrong environment, use reverse**: If you use mean reversion in a strong trend (Go Short), or use breakthroughs in shocks (chasing false breakthroughs), you will lose money - **The success or failure of the Bollinger Bands strategy lies in "judging the right environment"**;
- **Tracking ≠ Reversal**: The most common misuse is to regard "hitting the rails" as "must fall";
- **Profitability not guaranteed**.
