# Trading Contracts in Choppy Markets

> Midas Academy · C2 Contract Case Library (Virtual Suggestion Review)

The market is volatile most of the time. However, many people use the trend idea of ​​"chasing the rise, killing the fall, chasing breakthroughs" to engage in contract trading with volatile market conditions. As a result, they are repeatedly "slapped in the face", stop loss, and even doubt their life. This article uses a schematic case to see how the losses of "using the wrong strategy in the environment" accumulate.

> ⚠️ The entire process is virtual/indicative, no real targets are named, no predictions of real market conditions are made, and no recommendations are constituted.

## 1. Scene setting

Set a **indicative shock market** (digital fiction):

- A certain variety goes back and forth in the range of **95 ~ 105** for a long time (one box oscillates, corresponding to the consolidation of "only one Central Hub" in Chan Theory, see C1-16);
- Trader G **didn't realize that this was a shock**, applied the idea of "chasing after a breakthrough" to make contracts, and also used leverage to amplify;
- He repeatedly "chases breakthroughs" at the edge of the range.

## 2. Operation process (repeatedly harvested by false breakthroughs)

The operation of G repeats like a repeater:

- The price rushed above **105** a little bit, and G felt that "it is about to break through, chase the long position!" → The result was a **false breakthrough**, and the price rushed back to the range → G's long order **Stop Loss**;
- The price dropped a little bit below **95, and G thought "it fell below, chase the short!" → It was another **false breakthrough**, and the price went back up into the range after exploring it → G's short order** had another small Stop Loss**;
- ... This was repeated many times, and every time it was "I was beaten back as soon as I chased him in".

Looking at each stroke alone, the loss is not much (the stop loss is very small). But the problem is **more times + additional costs**:

- **Each Stop Loss**: One Stroke means a small loss;
- **Each opening**: There is a **handling fee**;
- **Holding a position overnight**: **Funding fees** may also be incurred;
- These **are accumulated** by StrokeStroke**, and then amplified** by **leverage** - G's account is in the repeated wear and tear of "small losses + handling fees + capital fees", **unknowingly shrunk by a large amount**.

The market was obviously fluctuating in the same place (95~105 did not change much), but G actually lost a lot of money.

## 3. Dismantling of key nodes

**Node 1: The mistake lies in "environmental judgment" - treating shocks as trends. **
G's most fundamental mistake is not the operation of a certain Stroke, but the failure to distinguish "whether it is a shock or a trend" first (echoing C1-16 and F17). "Chasing breakthroughs" is a strategy used in **trend/breakthrough market**; if used in **shock market**, every "breakthrough" is likely to be false, and Stop Loss will naturally occur repeatedly. **The strategy is not wrong, the mistake is that it is used in the wrong environment. **

**Node 2: "The more you move, the more you lose" in the shock. **
The characteristics of the volatile market are "back and forth and no direction". In it, **frequent openings and chasing** are equivalent to **actively sending handling fees and Stop Loss** to the market. In shocks, "moving less" often saves money than "being diligent" - many veterans simply wait and see with short positions in the shocks that they can't see clearly.

![C2-5 with picture 2](/academy-img/C2-5-配图2.png)

**Node 3: Leverage magnifies the cost of false breakouts. **
The Stop Loss of each false breakout was originally a small loss; but G used leverage, and **the small losses of each Stroke were magnified**. Multiplied by the number of repetitions, the cumulative total is considerable. **Leverage doubles the cost of "high-frequency trial and error in shocks"** - This is why frequent operations with high leverage should not be used in shocks.

**Node 4: Cost is the "invisible killer". **
G may think that "my stop loss is very small every time and I don't lose much", but ignores the accumulation of **handling fees and capital fees**. Under high-frequency operation, these hidden costs can eat up a considerable part of the principal. ** Frequent trading in a volatile environment is a losing battle of attrition with "cost". **

## 4. Key points of review

- **Distinguish the environment first, then choose a strategy**: Shock ≠ Trend (count Central Hub, look at high and low points, C1-4/C1-16); **Chasing breakthroughs is a trend play, and if used in shocks, false breakthroughs will be repeatedly harvested**;
- **"Move less" is the best in shocks**: Either **respond to the range** (light position, cautiously go long near the lower edge of the range, cautiously bearish near the upper edge, and still have to Stop Loss), or **simply wait and see with a short position** - don't send money diligently in a directionless market;
- **Beware of hidden costs**: **handling fees for frequent openings + capital fees for holding positions** will accumulate and be amplified by **leverage**, which is an important source of losses during shocks;
- **Leverage magnifies the cost of trial and error**: Frequent operations with high leverage should not be used during shocks;
- **There is no "one size fits all" playstyle** (echoing F17's No Holy Grail): The key is to match the strategy to the environment.

## 5. Main points of this article

- Illustrative case: The range **95~105**, G used the idea of "chasing breakthroughs" to repeatedly chase long/chasing shorts on the edge → **false breakthroughs in Stop Loss** again and again, plus **handling fees + capital fees** accumulated, amplified by **leverage**, the market did not move much, but the account shrank significantly.
- The fundamental mistake lies in **environmental judgment**: treating shocks as a trend; **chasing breakthroughs is a trend play, and using the wrong environment will be harvested repeatedly**.
- Response to shocks: **Range response (light position, still Stop Loss) or short position, less movement**; be wary of **hidden costs (handling fees + capital fees)** and **leverage amplification trial and error costs**.
- ⚠️ The whole process is virtual/signaled; there is no playing method suitable for all market conditions; it does not constitute suggestions, predictions, and frequent operations with high leverage are not encouraged.
