# Common Misunderstandings of Chan Theory Terminology

> Midas Academy · Advanced Chan Theory · Analysis of Difficulties

Chan Theory is difficult, and half of the difficulty lies in "misunderstanding of the concept." Many people talk about terminology, but their understanding is skewed - they think they understand it, but in fact they understand it fundamentally wrong, and the more they use it, the more confused they become. In this article, we will "clear the source" of some of the most commonly misunderstood core terms.

**Quick overview of this article**

- **Level ≠ Time Period**: The level is the size of the structure recursively, and the period is the scale of viewing the picture; it can be approximated without an equal sign (the same period chart can contain multiple levels of structures).
- **Divergence ≠ Reversal / Top and Bottom**: It is a reference for strength exhaustion, will be passivated, and has three evolutions (C1-18); it creates a buying and selling point structure and a non-reversal signal.
- **Buy and sell point ≠ Buy and sell order**: It is a structural position term and will fail; the operation is determined by the system and risk control.
- **Trend "Complete" ≠ Immediate reversal**: It is the end of a paragraph and must be followed by a reverse or consolidation; the completion of a trend is often accompanied by Divergence but does not mean an immediate violent reversal.
- **Central Hub ≠ Sideways**: It has three overlapping sub-levels with precise values ​​(ZG = minimum high point, ZD = maximum low point), and cannot be recognized based on "like sideways".
- **"Thousands of people and thousands of entanglements" ≠ Can doodle**: The differences are mostly in boundary cases, and the impact is limited; the basic skills are standard, and doodles are not allowed.
- ⚠️ Chan Theory is an **analysis framework**, which can only be used well if it is understood accurately; it does not predict, does not constitute buying and selling orders, and is not deified.

## 1. Deviation ①: level ≠ time period

**Misunderstanding**: "The 5-minute level is the 5-minute K-line chart."

**Correct answer**: **Level** is the "structure size" defined recursively by the trend structure (Central Hub/Trend); **Time period** is just the scale by which you **view the chart** (how long the K line is set to be). In practice, cycles can be used to **approximate** levels, but **the same period chart may have several levels of structure** (the daily chart includes both daily level trends and internal smaller-level Stroke/segment/Central Hub). So - **can be related, but not equal** (see C1-9 for details). Directly equating "level" with "the cycle of a certain K-line" is the number one source of confusion in Chan Theory.

## 2. Deviation ②: Divergence ≠ Reversal/Top and Bottom

**Misunderstanding**: "Divergence means peaking/bottoming and is about to reverse."

**Correct answer**: Divergence is the reference for "**same direction strength failure**" (C1-7), which **does not mean immediate reversal**. It will **passivate** (looking at Divergence in a strong trend but creating strength); Divergence may then reverse, may turn to consolidation, or even continue in the original direction (**three evolutions**, see C1-18). What Divergence creates is a certain level of buying and selling points (structure), not "a signal of inevitable reversal". Using Divergence as a reversal guarantee to take heavy positions against the trend is a common way to lose money.

## 3. Deviation ③: Buy and sell point ≠ Buy and sell order

**Misunderstanding**: "When a Type 1 Buy Point appears, it's time to buy."

**Correct answer**: Three Types of Buy and Sell Points are terms describing the **trend structure position** ("the structure has reached this position"), **not an operation instruction of "buy/sell when the point is reached"**. It **will fail** (see C1-19); whether to do it, where to put Go Long and Stop Loss is determined by your **complete system and risk control**. This is the red line that runs through the entire training camp—** buying and selling points are analytical concepts, not trading signals. **

## 4. Deviation ④: Trend Type "Complete" ≠ Immediately reverse

**Misunderstanding**: "When this trend is completed, it will turn around and fall / rise sharply."

**Correct answer**: The "completion" of a Trend Type is the end of a paragraph in the structure - after a trend of one level is completed, another Trend Type must follow (opposite trend or consolidation, see C1-4). The completion of a trend is often accompanied by Divergence, but "completion" does not mean an immediate and violent reversal in price: it may be followed by a period of consolidation (sideways) rather than a sharp decline. Don't read "complete" as "immediately reverse the trend."

## 5. Deviation ⑤: Central Hub ≠ Simple "sideways shock range"

**Misunderstanding**: "Central Hub is a sideways movement. If it looks like a sideways movement, it is Central Hub."

**Correct answer**: Central Hub is the **overlapping area** of at least three sub-level trends, with **accurate values** (upper edge ZG = the minimum value of the three-segment high points, and the lower edge ZD = the maximum value of the three-segment low points). It is a **structural concept**, and it does not count if "it looks like a sideways market to the naked eye" - some sideways do not constitute a Central Hub (the number of segments / overlap is not enough), and the price amplitude during some Central Hubs is not small. **If you use "does it look like a sideways market" to identify Central Hub, you will be mistaken. **

## 6. Deviation ⑥: "Thousands of people, thousands of entanglements" ≠ Chan Theory is useless / can be doodled

**Misreading**: "Anyway, there are thousands of people involved, so you can draw whatever you want. Chan Theory has no standards."

**Correct answer**: Thousands of people and thousands of entanglements mainly occur in **complex market conditions and boundary cases**, and the impact of these differences on operations is often limited (C1-2). On the contrary, Chan Theory uses a set of strict definitions to "reduce" differences - basic skills such as Stroke's conditions and Central Hub's values ​​have clear standards and cannot be messed with (see C1-11). Taking "thousands of people and thousands of entanglements" as an excuse for "no matter how you draw is right" is a complete misunderstanding of Chan Theory.
