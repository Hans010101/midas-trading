## 1. Concept introduction: The biggest enemy is often oneself

After learning analytical skills, many people still lose money. The problem is often not "not being able to analyze", but "knowing clearly what to do, but being led by emotions to do the opposite"**.

On the surface, trading is a game with the market, but in essence, a large part of it is a game with one's own emotions. In this article, we learn about some of the most common "emotional enemies" in trading and how they can make people make mistakes.

## 2. Four major emotional traps

The most common emotions in trading can be classified into four types:

**1. Fear. **
Afraid of losing money, afraid of missing out. It makes people dare not enter the market when the market should be entered, leave the market prematurely when the market is profitable (for fear of profit taking), and panic when the market plummets (cutting the flesh at the lowest point).

**2. Greed. **
I want to earn more and don’t want to miss out. It makes people **chasing the price** (chasing the price for fear of missing out), **not willing to leave when it's time to take profit** (wanting to make a little more, but ended up riding a roller coaster), **positions getting heavier as they go down**.

**3. Hope. **
When you lose money, you just "hope it goes back up". This is the root cause of ** carrying orders and not Stop Loss ** - it is obvious that you should admit your mistake and leave the market, but you hold on to your illusions and turn a small loss into a big loss.

**4. Regret. **
Regret over what has happened. It gave rise to **retaliatory trading** (eager to recover after losing money, increase leverage, and place heavy positions) and **chasing orders** (chasing higher after missing a wave unwillingly).

## 3. How emotions turn into wrong decisions

Putting the above together, it is the script of countless loss stories:

- Greed → **Chasing the rise and killing the fall**: Chasing at high prices and panic selling at low prices, falling as soon as you buy and rising as soon as you sell.
- Hope → **Carry the order**: If you don’t Stop Loss, you will fantasize about getting your money back, and the losses will get deeper.
- Fear → **Take Profit too early**: If you make a little profit, you will run away in a hurry, and you will not be able to take advantage of the big market; at the same time, you can hold on to the loss. "Cut off profits and let losses run" is exactly the opposite of the correct approach.
- Regret → **Retaliatory Trading**: After losing control of one stroke, he loses control of his emotions and makes large positions indiscriminately, resulting in more losses.

What these behaviors have in common is that temporary emotions replace prior rational judgment. **

## 4. Emotions cannot be eliminated, but they can be managed

One fact must be accepted: **emotions are human nature and cannot be eradicated. ** As long as you face the profit and loss of real money, fear and greed will emerge - this is not "poor psychological quality", but human nature.

So the goal is not to "eliminate emotions" (which is impossible), but to "manage emotions and not let them make decisions for you"**. Acknowledge the existence of emotions, and then use a set of external, objective things to restrain them - this leads to the most effective weapon against emotions: rules and discipline. **

## 5. Use rules and discipline to fight emotions

Fighting against emotions does not rely on the determination of "I will definitely be able to control it next time" (determination is often vulnerable in the face of emotions), but on **predetermined rules + strict execution**:

- **Make a plan in advance** (F2): Determine the entry, Stop Loss, Take Profit, and position before entering the market. When the time comes, you will only execute it and not make decisions on the spot - leaving no room for emotions to play out.
- **Mechanization and reduction of subjectivity**: The clearer the rules, the less room there is for emotions to influence the scene.
- **Execution by discipline** (F6): Turn "doing according to the rules" into a habit instead of confronting emotions head-on every time.
- **Use the trading log to supervise yourself**: Record every time "whether you are carried away by emotions" and make long-term corrections.

In a word: **You can't win against the positive willpower of emotions, but you can use "prior rules" to keep emotions out of decision-making. **
