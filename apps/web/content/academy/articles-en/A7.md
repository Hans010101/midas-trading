# What is Trend: The Trader's Best Friend

> **Midas Academy · Phase 2 · Module 5 Trends and Structure**

**Overview**

- **Trend = The directional movement of price**, determined by observing the movement of highs and lows.
- **Uptrend**: Higher highs + Higher lows (like climbing stairs).
- **Downtrend**: Lower highs + Lower lows (like descending stairs).
- **Consolidation/Range-bound**: Highs and lows remain roughly flat, price moves back and forth within a range with unclear direction.
- **Trading with the trend** is a concept to improve win rates, but trends can reverse and methods do not guarantee results; risk management must be applied.

## I. Concept Introduction: Price Doesn't Move Randomly, It Has "Direction"

If you stare at a set of candlesticks for a while, you will notice that although price goes up and down, it often presents an overall "sense of direction"—either the center of gravity shifts upward continuously, sinks downward continuously, or swings back and forth within a range. This **directional movement of price** is the **Trend**.

There is an old saying in the trading community: "The trend is the trader's best friend." This means: seeing the current direction is often more important than guessing whether the next candlestick will go up or down. In this section, we will clearly explain the three basic forms of trends; this is also the foundational understanding required for studying structure and **Chan Theory** later on.

## II. The Essence of a Trend: Observing How Highs and Lows Move

To determine a trend, the key is not to look at a single candlestick, but to observe how the **Highs** (swing tops) and **Lows** (swing bottoms) move sequentially over a period of time.

Price rarely moves in a straight line; it always fluctuates "two steps forward, one step back," forming a string of highs and a string of lows. The **direction of movement of these two sets of points determines the type of trend**.

## III. Uptrend: Higher Highs, Higher Lows

The structure of an **Uptrend** is: **Higher highs + Higher lows**.

Imagine climbing stairs: every time price surges, the new high is higher than the previous high; every time it pulls back, the pullback low is higher than the previous low. The overall center of gravity rises like steps.

This reflects supply and demand: buying pressure consistently dominates, and every pullback is taken over by someone at a higher price, raising the "floor" of the price.

## IV. Downtrend: Lower Highs, Lower Lows

A **Downtrend** is the opposite: **Lower highs + Lower lows**.

Imagine descending stairs: every time price bounces, the bounce high is lower than the previous high; every time it probes, the probe low is lower than the previous low, and the overall center of gravity sinks continuously. Selling pressure consistently dominates, and every bounce is suppressed to a lower level.

## V. Consolidation (Range-bound): Unclear Direction, Moving Within a Range

Besides rising and falling, there is a third state—**Consolidation** (also known as a sideways market or consolidation).

Its characteristics are: **Highs remain roughly at the same level, and Lows remain roughly at the same level**. Price fluctuates back and forth within a relatively fixed range without a clear direction. Above, there is a "ceiling" pressing down; below, there is a "floor" supporting it (which refers to the resistance and support levels mentioned earlier), and price fluctuates repeatedly between the two.

Consolidation often indicates a temporary balance between bulls and bears, with neither side gaining a significant advantage. Most of the time, the market actually spends in consolidation.

## VI. What Does "Trading with the Trend" Mean?

Once you understand the three types of trends, you can understand a concept repeatedly emphasized in trading—**Trading with the trend**: **Make your trading direction align as closely as possible with the current trend direction**, rather than fighting against the trend.

For example, in an uptrend, focus more on opportunities to go **Long**; in a downtrend, be more cautious or consider going **Short**; in consolidation, realize there is no clear direction and do not chase easily. The logic is simple: **Trading in the direction of the dominant force usually offers a higher probability of success and lower resistance.**

However, two points must be emphasized here to avoid misunderstanding:

1. **Trends can persist, but they can also reverse.** "Trading with the trend" does not mean "the trend will definitely continue." Any trend may end and reverse at some point—so even when trading with the trend, risk management such as stop-losses must be applied to leave room for being wrong.
2. **No method guarantees results.** Trading with the trend is a mindset to improve probability, not a guaranteed profit formula. Identifying trends and recognizing when a trend may shift is itself a skill that requires long-term practice.
