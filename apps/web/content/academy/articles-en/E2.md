# Funding Rate: The Anchor of Perpetual Contracts

> Midas Academy · Level 4 · Module 12 Contracts and Derivatives

## 1. Concept introduction: Without an expiration date, how can we get close to the spot?

The previous article left a question: Traditional futures rely on "convergence to the spot at maturity", but Perpetual Contract has no expiration date, so how does it rely on to prevent the price from deviating from the spot for a long time?

The answer is **Funding Rate (Funding Rate)**. It is like an invisible "anchor" that continuously pulls the price of the Perpetual Contract up toward the spot price by regularly charging fees between the long and short parties. This article will explain this "anchor" clearly.

## 2. What is Funding Rate: regular payment between long and short

**Funding Rate is a Stroke fee that traders holding long positions and short positions pay each other at regular intervals in the Perpetual Contract. **

There are two key points that must be emphasized first, otherwise it will be easy to misunderstand:

1. **The Stroke money is transferred between the long and short parties, not the handling fee paid to the platform. ** The platform is only responsible for transferring between long and short according to the rules, and does not charge the Stroke capital fee (the handling fee is another matter).
2. **Settlement is cyclical**: It is usually settled at regular intervals (for example, every 8 hours, **the specific period varies depending on the platform**), and the position will not be paid until the settlement time point.

## 3. Positive and negative rates: who pays whom

This is the core part of Funding Rate and the easiest thing to remember. Please be sure to remember it correctly:

- **Funding Rate is positive (positive rate)**: paid by **longs to shorts**. (long → short)
- **Funding Rate is negative (negative rate)**: paid by **shorts to longs**. (short → long)

Why is this? It is linked to the "premium/discount" of the perpetual price relative to the spot price:

- When the perpetual price is higher than the spot (premium), the rate is usually positive, allowing longs to pay and shorts to be charged - this will increase the holding cost of Go Long and encourage Go Short, thereby pulling the high perpetual price down and closer to the spot.
- When the perpetual price is lower than the spot (discount), the rate is usually negative, allowing shorts to pay and longs to be charged - pulling the low perpetual price up and closer to the spot.

This is the mechanism of "anchoring": by making the deviating party pay, the perpetual price is continuously pulled back to the spot. **

## 4. Rate as emotional reference

The level, positive and negative of Funding Rate are often used to observe market sentiment from the side:

- The rate is **obviously positive and on the high side**: It shows that the demand for Go Long is strong and people are willing to pay for Go Long, which often reflects the **longish sentiment**.
- The rate is **obviously negative**: It shows that the demand for Go Short is strong, which often reflects **bearish sentiment**.

But two points must be emphasized:

1. **It is a sentiment reference, not a buying and selling signal, let alone a price prediction. ** High rates do not mean they will continue to rise - sometimes extreme rates mean overcrowding on one side (which is also a risk in itself).
2. **The capital fee paid is a holding cost. ** If you hold a direction that requires payment for a long time, the Stroke fee will continue to erode your income and is one of the costs that need to be included in the contract position.

## 5. Let’s look at the settlement cycle again

Funding Rate is settled according to a fixed period (such as once every 8 hours, which varies by platform). A detail that is often overlooked is: **Usually only when you hold a position at the settlement time, you need to collect and pay funding fees**; many times, whether and in which direction you hold a position across the settlement point will affect the actual fees you pay or receive. Specific rules are subject to each platform.

![E2 with picture 4](/academy-img/E2-配图4.png)

## 6. Main points of this article

- **Funding Rate** is the **regular payment** fee between long and short parties in the Perpetual Contract, which is used to anchor the perpetual price to the spot**.
- It is a **transfer between long and short**, **not a handling fee** paid to the platform; it is settled on a periodic basis (e.g. every 8 hours, varies by platform).
- **Positive rate = longs pay to shorts; negative rate = shorts pay to longs** (the direction must not be reversed).
- The rate can be used as a **emotion reference**, but **does not predict the price**; the capital fee paid is a **holding cost**.
