# Three Evolutions After Divergence

> Midas Academy · Chan Theory Advanced · Advanced Special Topics

"Divergence, is it going to be reversed?" - This is the most frequently asked question in Chan Theory and the easiest to get wrong. The answer is clear: **Not necessarily. ** This article clearly explains the **three possible evolutions** after Divergence, completely breaking the obsession of "Divergence = reversal". This is an extension of C1-7 and an important lesson in Chan Theory’s probabilistic thinking (C1-20).

**Quick overview of this article**

- **Divergence is a reference for strength exhaustion, which does not mean immediate reversal**; there are **three evolutions** after Divergence.
- **Evolution 1·Reversal**: The price really turns (type 1 buying and selling point is realized) - it is just one of three, not inevitable.
- **Evolution 2·Consolidation**: The price does not reverse much and moves into consolidation/sideways (corresponding to "Trend Divergence→Last Central Hub Level Expansion→Large Level Consolidation"), which is most easily ignored.
- **Evolution 3·Continuation**: Divergence is passivated/failed, the price stops for a while and then moves in the original direction (common in strong trends) - entering the market against the trend will be slapped in the face.
- Reason: Divergence only describes "the strength of this period is exhausted", and the next move depends on the greater level of strength, etc., it cannot determine it.
- Practical combat: **Improper reversals ensure heavy positions against the trend, wait for further structural confirmation, and always cooperate with Stop Loss**.
- ⚠️ Divergence is a **probability reference/analysis framework** and does not predict or constitute buying or selling orders.

## 1. Reiterate: Divergence is a strength reference, not a reversal guarantee

Let’s restate the foundation first (red line):

> **Divergence = the exhaustion of the strength of the same direction** (judged by the MACD yellow and white lines pulling back + the area of red and green columns shrinking, see C1-7). It is a "strength reference" and does not mean that the price will reverse immediately.

Divergence only tells you "**This section is not strong enough**", but it **cannot determine** how the price will move next - this is why there will be more than one way to move after Divergence.

## 2. Evolution 1: Reversal

After **Divergence, the price really turned** - it turned to increase after falling Divergence, and then turned to fall after rising Divergence, creating a **turning** at this level (the corresponding type 1 buying and selling point was realized).

This is the situation that everyone is most looking forward to, and it is also the source of the misunderstanding "Divergence = reversal". But please note: **Reversal is only one of three evolutions** and is not an inevitable result. Treating it as the only possibility is the beginning of losses.

## 3. Evolution 2: Consolidation (Transfer to Central Hub/Consolidation)

After **Divergence, the price did not reverse significantly, but entered a period of consolidation** - it stayed sideways.

This corresponds to the theorem C1-3: **Divergence of a certain level of trend will cause the level of the last Central Hub of the trend to expand** and evolve into **a larger level of consolidation** (or a higher level counter-trend). The specific manifestation is: the price does not turn around and fall / rise sharply after Divergence, but enters a range and goes back and forth (forming a new Central Hub, or an expansion and upgrade of the original Central Hub, see C1-2).

This situation is very common, but it is most easily ignored - many people think that "Divergence either reverses or continues", forgetting that "sideways consolidation" is the most likely intermediate state**.

## 4. Evolution 3: Continue (Divergence passivation/failure)

**After Divergence, the price just pauses for a moment and then continues in the original direction** - that is, "**Divergence passivation/Divergence failure**".

It is especially common in **strong trends**: MACD seems to be divergent (the column area shrinks, the yellow and white lines pull back), but the price increases again and continues to hit new highs/new lows. At this time, if you enter the market against the trend because you "see Divergence", you will be severely slapped in the face by the continuing trend.

**Divergence will be passivated** - this point has been repeatedly emphasized in C1-7 and C1-13, and it is nailed down again: **Don't take the "appearance of Divergence" as "the inevitable end of the trend". **

## 5. Why are there three types?

Fundamental reason: **Divergence only describes "this period of strength exhaustion", but how the market goes next depends on greater levels of power, new funds, the overall environment, etc. - these are not determined by "this period of Divergence". **

Therefore, what Divergence provides is a "signal light of exhaustion" rather than a script of "what will happen next". The correct usage is to understand it as a probabilistic reference (C1-20), rather than a deterministic switch.

## 6. Practical meaning

These three evolutions give three direct reminders for actual combat:

1. **Don’t use Divergence as a "reversal guarantee" to take heavy positions against the trend**: If it is a "continuation", taking heavy positions against the trend will be very miserable;
2. After **Divergence, you must wait for further structural confirmation** before deciding which evolution to take - for example, waiting for "1 buy followed by 2 buys" to confirm a reversal, or waiting for "Central Hub formation" to confirm consolidation, rather than acting as soon as you see Divergence;
3. **Always cooperate with Stop Loss**: Because you don’t know in advance which one of the three it will be, Stop Loss is the only insurance against "wrong judgment (especially 'continue')" (echoing C1-19, Group F discipline).
