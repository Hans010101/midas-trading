## 1. Concept introduction: There is no best system, only the one that suits you

After learning this, you may want to ask: Which is better, trend trading or swing trading? Which set of indicators is the strongest?

The answer may surprise you: There is no "best" trading system, only a trading system that "suits you". ** The same method can be executed stably in the hands of one person, but it is awkward in the hands of another person. This article is the end of the first batch of the fifth level. It talks about how to create a system that is **your own** and how to test and iterate it.

## 2. What should you consider when "suiting you"?

Whether a system is suitable for you mainly depends on whether it matches your practical conditions:

- **Personality**: Are you a calm person who can wait for big trends, or do you like to get in and out quickly? Those who are impatient and insist on long-term trends often fail to hold on; those who are chronic and insist on high-frequency trends often fail to keep up.
- **Available time**: Can you keep an eye on the market all day long, or do you only have sporadic time? This directly determines whether you are suitable for a large cycle or a small cycle.
- **Fund status**: Fund size and the fluctuations you can withstand affect your position and product selection.
- **Risk Tolerance**: What is the maximum loss you can sleep on? The risk level of the system must be within your tolerance.

**The system that suits you is one that you can "implement in a long-term, stable, and non-awkward manner"** - No matter how powerful the method is, it is useless if it cannot be implemented.

## 3. From imitation to originality

Building your own system usually goes through a natural process:

1. **Imitation**: First learn and borrow mature methods (trends, shocks, usage of various indicators), follow them and understand their logic.
2. **Screening and Adjustment**: Discover what suits you and what doesn’t during practice (especially virtual practice), keep the ones that are suitable and get rid of the awkward ones.
3. **Integration and Forming**: Integrate the parts that suit you into a set of rules that have your own style and can be stably executed by you.
4. **Be original**: In the end, it bears your imprint - matching your personality, time, and risk appetite.

**No one has a perfect system when they come up**. They all start from imitation and slowly develop their own things through testing.

## 4. The system must be tested: backtesting and real (virtual) verification

After thinking clearly about a system, you cannot directly gamble with real money on whether it is useful or not, but you must first test it:

- **Backtest**: Use historical market data to test the past performance of this set of rules. (Note: **The fact that the backtest is effective does not necessarily mean that it will be effective in the future** - history will not simply repeat itself. Overfitting, survivor bias, etc. will make the backtest too optimistic.)
- **Simulation/Virtual Real Market Verification**: In an environment that does not bear real financial risks, actually run according to the rules for a period of time to see how it performs under "close to real" conditions and whether you can strictly implement it.

## 5. The system is not static: continuous optimization

Finally, it must be understood that the ** system is not fixed once and for all, it requires continuous optimization and iteration. **

- Market conditions will change, and methods that once worked may gradually become ineffective.
- Through continuous **review** (F7), you will find areas that need adjustment in the system.
- Turn the conclusions of the review into improvements to the rules, then test and then implement them - let the system grow with you and the market.

**A good system is "living": it is constantly polished through inspection and review, and becomes more and more suitable for you. **
