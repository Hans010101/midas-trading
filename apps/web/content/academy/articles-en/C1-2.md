# Expansion and Extension of the Central Hub

> Midas Academy · Chan Theory Advanced · Structure Advanced

In the introduction, Central Hub is "the interval where at least three sub-level trends overlap." But in actual combat, after a Central Hub is formed, the trend often does not end obediently - it will continue to toss. This kind of "tossing" is divided into several different ways by Chan Theory. The most commonly confused ones are **extension** and **expansion** (there is also a close relative called **expansion**).

These words look alike and are related to "Central Hub enlarging/upgrading". They are the hardest hit area of ​​"thousands of people and thousands of entanglements" in Chan Theory. In this article, we will explain clearly the differences in their definitions, how to distinguish them, and what each means.

**Quick overview of this article**

- First distinguish the two sets of boundaries: **Central Hub interval ZG~ZD** (ZG=minimum value of three-segment high points, ZD=maximum value of three-segment low points, core overlap area) and **oscillation interval GG~DD** (GG=highest, DD=lowest, fluctuation frame).
- **Extension**: **Not out** Three Types of Buy and Sell Points, the trend repeats around the Central Hub interval, the number of segments increases; **9 segments are upgraded** (5/7 segments are just extensions but not upgraded); the range is mainly determined by the first three segments.
- **Extension**: **The **th Three Types of Buy and Sell Points are out. After leaving, **return to the Central Hub interval ZG~ZD**. The two Central Hubs overlap → upgrade (see ZG/ZD, not GG/DD).
- **Expansion (semi-expansion)**: After three transactions, the pullback only reaches the **shock range GG~DD**, and does not enter ZG~ZD; the same upgrade, but in different forms; it can be expanded first and then expanded, and cannot be reversed.
- At first glance: first check if there are **Three Types of Buy and Sell Points** (none = extension), and if there are **return to ZG~ZD (extension) or only to GG~DD (extension)**.
- ⚠️ This set of definitions has "thousands of people and thousands of entanglements" and has limited impact on operations. It can be understood according to the framework; Three Types of Buy and Sell Points are **structural terms and non-operational instructions**.

## 1. First, nail down the two sets of boundaries of Central Hub (this is the basis for distinction)

To clearly explain extension and expansion, we must first distinguish the **two groups** of key price points of Central Hub. Many people get confused because they don’t make the distinction here:

- **Central Hub range (ZG ~ ZD)**: This is the "strict sense" range of Central Hub.
  - **ZG (Central Hub upper edge) = the "minimum value" of the high point of each segment in the first three segments that constitute the Central Hub**;
  - **ZD (Central Hub lower edge) = the "maximum value" of the low point of each segment in the first three segments**.
  - (That is, take the lowest of the three high points as the upper edge, and the highest of the three low points as the lower edge - this is the area where the three periods truly "overlap", be sure not to invert it.)
- **Swing Range (GG ~ DD)**: This is the "maximum envelope" of price fluctuations during Central Hub.
  - **GG = the maximum value of all high points in Central Hub**;
  - **DD = the minimum value of all lows in the Central Hub**.

**ZG~ZD is the "core overlap area" (small), GG~DD is the "fluctuation outer frame" (large)**. The difference between extension, expansion, and expansion lies precisely in "which set of intervals you return to."

## 2. Central Hub extension: Three Types of Buy and Sell Points are not released, and the Central Hub is repeated.

**Extension means that after the Central Hub is formed, the trend does not appear, but continues to move back and forth around the Central Hub range. **

The characteristics are:

- **Premise**: **No** Three Types of Buy and Sell Points appear (the trend does not really "leave and stand firm" Central Hub);
- **Performance**: The sub-level trend shuttles back and forth in the Central Hub range segment by segment, and the "number of segments" of the Central Hub continues to increase - segment 3 is the initial composition, and it may be segment 5, segment 7, segment 9... in the future;
- **Scope**: During the extension process, the Central Hub range** is still mainly determined by the initial first three segments** (ZG/ZD remains unchanged);
- **Upgrade point**: When extended to **Segment 9**, this Central Hub will be **upgraded** to a higher level Central Hub (in Chan Theory, the Central Hub level is pushed forward by the power of 3; Segments 5 and 7 are just extensions, not upgraded, and will not be upgraded until Segment 9).

One sentence to remember: **Extension = the same Central Hub, no three transactions, repeated on the spot, 9 stages of upgrades. **

## 3. Central Hub Extension: Three Types of Buy and Sell Points are out, but "back"

**Expansion means that after the formation of Central Hub, the trend went out of the (sub-level) Three Types of Buy and Sell Points. The original Central Hub was once considered to be over, but then the trend of leaving "returned to the original Central Hub range (ZG ~ ZD)". **

Looking at the extension, there are two key differences:

- **Different premise**: The extension is **appeared** Three Types of Buy and Sell Points (the extension is not out). The Three Types of Buy and Sell Points means that "Central Hub was once settled and the trend tried to leave";
- **Extent of return**: After leaving, the trend** returned to the core Central Hub range of ZG~ZD** (note: the expansion looks at ZG and ZD, not GG and DD).

The result is: you will see that the two Central Hub intervals overlap/entangle, so they are "expanded" and merged, which brings about the upgrade of this level of Central Hub (to a higher level Central Hub).

One sentence to remember: **Expansion = Three transactions occurred, the transaction returned to the original Central Hub range (ZG~ZD), and the two Central Hubs were upgraded overlappingly. **

## 4. Let’s clarify “expansion (semi-expansion)” by the way: only return to the shock range

There is also a close relative that is often confused with expansion - **expansion (also called semi-expansion)**:

**Expansion = After the Three Types of Buy and Sell Points appear and the trend leaves, the pullback only returns to the original Central Hub's "oscillating range (GG ~ DD)", but does not return to the "Central Hub range (ZG ~ ZD)". **

So to distinguish between expansion and expansion, just one sentence: **Which range does the pullback after the Three Types of Buy and Sell Points return to? **

- Returned to the **Central Hub interval ZG~ZD** → **Extended**;
- Only returned to the **shock range GG~DD** and did not enter ZG~ZD → **expansion (semi-expansion)**.

Two additional points: Expansion and expansion will both cause the Central Hub to be upgraded (to a higher-level Central Hub), but in different forms; and in practice, you can "expand first and then expand", but you cannot "expand first and then expand".

## 5. How can "extension" and "expansion" be distinguished at a glance?

Condensate the core judgments into a decision-making logic, and ask yourself this in actual combat:

1. **Does Three Types of Buy and Sell Points appear? **
   - **No** → The trend repeats in the Central Hub → **Extend** (upgrade at 9 levels);
   - **Yes** → Go to question 2.
2. Where did the pullback after **Three Types of Buy and Sell Points return? **
   - Return to **ZG~ZD (Central Hub interval)** → **Extended**;
   - Only returns to **GG~DD (oscillatory range)** → **Expansion (semi-expansion)**.

> ⚠️ An honest reminder: Extension / Expansion / Expansion / Rebirth This "Central Hub growth method" is one of the most divergent parts of Chan Theory and the most inconsistent opinions among different schools (a typical "thousands of people and thousands of entanglements"). The good news is that most Chan Theory practitioners also admit: **Whether it is called extension, expansion or expansion, the impact on actual operations is actually limited**. They are more to "give a unified view on the Central Hub level and whether to merge, so that everyone will not understand it differently." So please regard this article as an **analysis framework to clarify the concepts**, and just remember the core judgments (whether there are three transactions, and which range to return to); there is no need to dwell on whether a certain boundary case is expanding or expanding, let alone taking it as a buying and selling signal. The Three Types of Buy and Sell Points themselves are also **structural terms**, which describe the structural position of the trend, not the operation instructions of "buy/sell here".
