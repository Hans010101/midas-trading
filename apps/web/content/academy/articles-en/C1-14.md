# A Complete Chan Theory Walkthrough: Uptrend

> Midas Academy · Chan Theory Advanced · Complete Review

I have learned a lot of concepts, and this article will string them together and use them again - using a period of **symbolized rising market**, from the bottom K-line, all the way to Stroke, Central Hub, and buying and selling points, to complete the complete process of Chan Theory analysis.

First put the red line in front:

> ⚠️ This article uses a **teaching, fictitious/indicative rising market**, **does not name any real targets, and does not make a judgment on "what to buy or sell"**. All buy and sell points are marked as **structural positions** and are not buy and sell orders. What we demonstrate is the "**analysis process**", not prediction of rise or fall.

**Quick overview of this article**

- Chan Theory's complete derivation process (bottom-up): **K line includes processing → Find Fractal → Connect Stroke (≥3 K lines between Fractals, not shared) → Segment (≥3 Stroke overlap, Feature Sequence is judged to be destroyed) → Central Hub (labeled ZG = minimum high point, ZD = maximum low value) → Judge Trend Type (number Central Hub) → Mark buying and selling points (structural position) → Look at the end Divergence**.
- Trend Type depends on the number of Central Hubs: 1 Central Hub = consolidation, ≥2 Central Hubs increasing in sequence = upward trend.
- The target of the buying and selling point (1/2/3 buy) is the **structural position**, **not a buying and selling order**; the terminal trend Divergence indicates that the rise may be completed, but **Divergence ≠ will reverse immediately** (C1-18).
- ⚠️ Full ** indication / fictitious market conditions, non-real targets, not trading recommendations, and no predictions**; Chan Theory gives structures and probabilities, not definite answers.

## 1. The first step: K-line inclusion processing → Find Fractal → Connect to Stroke

When you get a trend that indicates an upward trend, don’t rush to find buying and selling points first, start from the bottom:

1. **K line inclusion processing**: Perform non-inclusion processing on adjacent K lines that have an inclusion relationship (take high and high in the upward direction, merge low and low in the downward direction) to obtain a clean K line sequence;
2. **Find Fractal**: Find Top Fractal and Bottom Fractal on the processed K line;
3. **Connect Stroke**: According to the Stroke standard - **at least 3 K lines between the top and Bottom Fractal (new standard, excluding Fractal sharing), Fractal does not share K lines** - connect the Fractal into one StrokeStroke.

This step is the foundation. If you get it wrong, all the rest will be wrong (see C1-11).

## 2. Step 2: Stroke → Segment

Connect Strokes into Segments according to Segment rules: **At least three Strokes, the first Stroke and the third Stroke overlap**. At the possible end of the Segment, use **Feature Sequence** (take the Stroke in the opposite direction to the Segment) to determine whether the Segment has been damaged (the first/second type of damage, see C1-1).

Segment is a higher-level "skeleton" than Stroke. It allows us to see clearly which segments make up this rise.

## 3. Step 3: Find Central Hub

During this period of rise, find the overlapping area of at least three sub-level trends, draw the Central Hub, and mark the precise value:

- **Central Hub upper edge ZG = the minimum value of these three high points**;
- **Central Hub lower edge ZD = the maximum value of these three lows**.

(Be sure to take this "overlapping area" and don't reverse it.) In a healthy rise, you can often see one or even multiple Central Hubs.

![C1-14 with picture 2](/academy-img/C1-14-配图2.png)

## 4. Step 4: Determine Trend Type

Count Central Hub and look at the direction (C1-4):

- If there is only one Central Hub during this period of rise → it is a period of consolidation (even if it rises a lot);
- If there are **two or more Central Hubs that rise** in sequence → it is the real **uptrend** (relay of Central Hubs).

This step determines your judgment on the "nature of this market" - whether it is a trend or just a wave in a large box.

## 5. Step 5: Mark the buying and selling points (structural position)

After the structure is clear, mark the buying and selling points in this market (**The whole process is the structural position, not the buying and selling instructions**):

- **1 buy**: the end of the downtrend before this rise** and the turning point after the **trend Divergence** (if this rise was started by a 1 buy);
- **2 buy**: After 1 buy, the price rebounds and then pulls back, and the **callback does not break the 1 buy low** point;
- **3 Buy**: After Central Hub is **upwardly broken**, the price pulls back to the point where **does not return to Central Hub (does not break ZG)**.

What they mark is "the structure has reached a position worthy of attention", not "buy here"**.

## 6. Step 6: Use Divergence to see the "healthiness" of this increase

Finally, use **MACD** (the yellow and white lines pull back + the red column **area** shrinks, see C1-7) to see if there is a trend Divergence** at the end of this rise:

- If there is a trend Divergence → it means that the strength of the rise is fading, and this rise may be "completed";
- But please remember (continued from C1-18): **Divergence ≠ Immediate reversal** - after completion, it may reverse, may turn into consolidation, or may continue. Divergence is just a "dynamic reference".

## 7. Summary of review

After walking through this, you will find: The complete deduction of Chan Theory is a set of "**bottom-up structural analysis**" - **K line → Fractal → Stroke → Segment → Central Hub → Trend Type → Buy and sell points → Divergence**. It helps you objectively understand "how this rise was formed and what step it has taken", rather than predicting how much it will rise next.

Let me emphasize again: ** indicates market conditions, is not a real target, is not a trading recommendation, and is not a prediction. ** Chan Theory gives structure and probability (see C1-20), not a definite answer.
