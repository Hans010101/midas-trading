# Grid Trading Strategy

> Midas Academy · C3 Strategy Topic Library

Grid trading is very popular because it "automatically sells high and buys low, and it looks like making money lying down". But it has a fatal weakness - fear of unilateral market conditions. This article explains clearly the logic, parameters and risks of the grid, and exposes the illusion of "laying down and making money".

**Quick overview of this article**

- Grid = **Layered orders within the range, mechanical selling high and buying low (buy every time it falls/sell every time it rises)**, and earn the small price difference between the ranges.
- **Applicable to continuous shocks/clear ranges, invalid (fatal) in unilateral market conditions**: unilateral declines lead to deeper losses, unilateral rises lead to shortfalls, **profit and loss asymmetry (small profits and big losses)**.
- Parameters: upper and lower bounds of the range, grid distance, position per grid + total position upper limit, **Stop Loss** when breaking through the range.
- ★**Afraid of unilateral or misjudgment of the range, and unilateral liquidation may occur if the contract is added with leverage, and it is definitely not a matter of lying around to make money**.
- ⚠️ Midas virtual disk can be tried/backtested (focus on unilateral losses), AI signals are for reference only; high leverage, prediction, and guaranteed profit are not encouraged.

## 1. Strategy principles

Grid trading is to place buy and sell orders in a layered manner at fixed intervals within a ** price range ** and mechanically ** sell high and buy low **: ** buy one share every time it falls and sell one share ** every time it goes up ** (see D Dictionary, C2-5).

Its source of profit is the small price difference accumulated one by one when the price fluctuates back and forth within the range. In a continuously volatile market, with prices going up and down, the grid keeps "buying low and selling high", looking like an "automatic money-making machine." **But this only holds true under the premise of "continuous shock" - once the premise is broken, trouble will come.

## 2. Applicable scenarios and failure scenarios

- **Applicable: Markets that continue to fluctuate and have clear ranges. ** The price repeatedly moves back and forth in the range, and the grid can continue to make transactions and accumulate small price differences.
- ★**Failure/fatal risk: unilateral market. **
  - **Unilateral decline**: The price is still falling after falling out of the lower bound of the range. The grid **continues to buy, deeper and deeper** until **funds are exhausted** (even contract Liquidation);
  - **Unilateral rise**: The price rises beyond the upper bound of the range, the grid **sells out its positions early**, and **shorts** the subsequent surge.
  - The result is **extremely asymmetric profit and loss**: What you make in the shock is a "small price difference", and what you lose in a unilateral way is a "big hole" - **make small profits and lose big**.

## 3. How to use (parameters and rules)

Several key parameters of the grid strategy:

1. **Upper and lower bounds of the interval**: the price range of the grid operation (if the interval is wrongly judged, the entire grid will be affected);
2. **Grid spacing (grid spacing)**: The price interval between each grid (small grid spacing, frequent transactions but small single price difference; large grid spacing, vice versa);
3. **Position per grid + total position limit**: how much to buy and sell per grid, and **maximum overall investment** (to control total risk);
4. ★**Stop Loss** when breaking through the range: When the price effectively breaks through the range (especially downward), there must be an **Exit/Stop Loss** mechanism, instead of allowing the grid to take infinite orders on one side.

## 4. Advantages, Disadvantages and Risks

**Advantages**: It can **automatically sell high and buy low** during shocks, without having to watch the market, and is executed in a disciplined manner (mechanically, without emotions).

**Disadvantages and Risks**:

- ★**Afraid of unilateralism (the biggest flaw)**: The deeper the unilateral decline, the deeper the unilateral rise, and the shortfall - **asymmetric profits and losses, small profits and big losses**;
- **If the interval judgment is wrong, you will be trapped**: The success or failure of the grid depends on "whether the interval judgment is correct or not";
- ★**Leverage is added to the contract to run the grid, and the unilateral market may be liquidated directly**: Leverage amplifies the risk of "deeper and deeper" when unilateral declines (echoing the C2 leverage red line);
- ★**It is by no means a "making money" tool**: On the surface, you can make a small amount of money steadily during the fluctuations, but in fact, you may lose all the money you made before, or even lose money in one move.
