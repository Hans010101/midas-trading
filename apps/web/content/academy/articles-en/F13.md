# Breakout Trading Strategy: Enter on Key Levels

> Midas Academy · Level 5 · Module 16 Trading Strategy Types

**Quick overview of this article**

- **Breakout trading = Enter the market with the trend when the price breaks through the key level**; the key level includes the previous high and low points, range boundaries, form necklines, and intensive trading areas; the essence is to follow the trend/right side of the trade.
- **The biggest enemy is a false breakthrough** (a short-term breakthrough and then retraction); you can use **higher volume, a firm closing price, and a retreat after a breakthrough without breaking through** to improve confirmation - but **there is no way to 100% eliminate false breakthroughs**.
- After entering the market, **Stop Loss is set on the other side of the key level** (the point where the breakthrough is falsified), and the price retracts to strictly Stop Loss; the breakthrough strategy especially relies on strict Stop Loss.
- Suitable for trend start/market changes, not suitable for continuous shocks (frequent false breakthroughs); ⚠️ The risk of chasing breakthroughs with leverage is extremely high (amplification of losses, possible Liquidation).
- ⚠️ Red line: Breakthrough** does not guarantee successful continuation, does not guarantee profit, and needs to cooperate with risk management**; entry and exit are logical diagrams, not precise trading signals; what was valid in the past does not mean it will be valid in the future.

## 1. Concept introduction: When chasing breakthroughs, why do you always get trapped?

When I talked about trend following in the previous article, I mentioned that there are two opportunities to enter the market following the trend: **pullback entry** and **breakthrough entry**. In this article, we will specifically talk about "breakthrough entry" - **enter the market with the trend when the price breaks through the key level and may "change the market"**.

It and the callback entry are a pair of complementary strategies: the callback entry is "waiting for the price to fall and buy a bargain", and the breakthrough entry is "waiting for the price to rush up and chasing a confirmation".

Many people have a love-hate relationship with breakthroughs:

- **Love**: Breakthroughs are often accompanied by a wave of rapid market trends, and it’s great to catch the right one;
- **Hate**: When chasing breakthroughs, I always get "trapped on the top of the mountain" - as soon as I catch up, the price turns around and shrinks, Stop Loss is out, and looking back, it is a "false breakthrough".

This article explains the breakthrough strategy thoroughly: what is a breakthrough, how to filter out false breakthroughs as much as possible, how to enter the market and how to set up Stop Loss.

## 2. What is breakout trading?

**Breakout trading is a strategy of entering the market in the direction of the breakthrough when the price crosses an important "key level". **

Its logic is: the key position is the "line of defense" that the long and short parties repeatedly compete for. Once this line of defense is broken, it often means that one party has the upper hand and the original balance is broken, which may trigger a trending market - breakthrough trading is to **follow this new direction** after it is broken.

There are several types of common "key bits":

- **Previous high/low**: The price breaks through the previous high (new high) or falls below the previous low (new low).
- **Boundary of a swing range**: The price breaks through the upper edge (resistance) of a sideways range or falls below the lower edge (support).
- **Pattern Neckline**: Some classic patterns (such as double bottom, head and shoulders bottom, etc.) have a "neckline", and breaking through the neckline is often regarded as a signal for the completion of the pattern.
- **Intense transaction area**: An area where prices have been entrenched for a long time and transactions are intensive. Breaking through it means getting rid of the entanglement of hold-up/profit order.

From a classification point of view, the essence of breakthrough trading is to play with the trend/right side - it does not predict whether a breakthrough will occur, but waits for the breakthrough to actually occur (confirmed) before following the direction to enter the market.

## 3. Why breakthroughs deserve attention

The reason why the key position is important is that it is the "dividing line" between the long and short forces:

- A resistance level that has been tested many times and failed to be broken is backed by intensive selling pressure; once the price breaks through it with heavy volume, it means that the power of the buyers has finally overwhelmed the sellers, and the **balance is broken**.
- In the same way, a break below an important support indicates that sellers have the upper hand.

Breakthroughs are often the turning point when the market moves from "balance" to "imbalance" and from "shock" to "trend". This is why breakout trading is often used as a means to capture the start of a trend - to jump on the trend at the moment the market changes.

## 4. The biggest enemy of breakthrough strategy: false breakthrough

But the ideal is very full, and the reality is very skinny. The biggest enemy of breakout trading is - **False Breakout**.

**False breakthrough means that the price briefly breaks through the key level, but fails to stand firm and quickly shrinks back to the key level. ** People chasing breakthroughs enter the market at the moment of breakthroughs, but as soon as the price retracts, they are trapped and swept away by Stop Loss.

False breakthroughs are extremely common, especially in **shock market** (this is the risk of "false breakthroughs" mentioned by F9) - the boundaries of the shock range are often "tentatively" poked by the price and then retracted, specifically to "wash" away those chasing breakthroughs.

Therefore, distinguishing between "true breakthroughs" and "false breakthroughs" is the core problem of breakthrough trading:

- **True Breakthrough**: After the price breaks through the key level, it stands firm and continues to move in the direction of the breakthrough, evolving into a trend.
- **False breakthrough**: After the price breaks through, it **fails to stand and then retracts within** the key level. The breakthrough "fails".

The cruel thing is: **At the moment a breakthrough occurs, you cannot be 100% sure whether it is true or false** - true and false often cannot be distinguished until later. What the breakthrough strategy needs to do is not to "eliminate" false breakthroughs (which cannot be done), but to use some methods to increase the odds of winning and filter out some false breakthroughs**.

## 5. How to filter false breakthroughs (improve confirmation)

Although false breakthroughs cannot be eradicated, there are several common "confirmation" methods that can help you improve the reliability of breakthroughs:

**1. Look at trading volume: True breakthroughs are often accompanied by heavy volume. **
A truly powerful breakthrough is usually backed by the actual participation of ** funds, which is manifested in a significant increase in trading volume (high-volume breakthrough). If the trading volume is very small (shrinkage) during the breakthrough, it means that the power of participation is insufficient, and the reliability of this breakthrough will be questioned.

**2. Look at the closing price: use closing confirmation instead of intraday touch. **
It is common for the price to briefly "insert" the key level during the session (insert the pin during the session), but it is more meaningful to determine whether the closing position can be outside the key level. A common approach is: **wait for the closing price of one (or several) K lines to stand firmly outside the key level**, and then determine that the breakthrough is established - this can filter out many intraday false moves.

**3. Watch and step back: After a breakthrough, step back and then advance if the price does not break. **
A more stable (but may miss part of the market) approach: after the price breaks through the key level, **wait for it to step back** - if it steps back near the original key level and **does not fall back** (the original resistance becomes support), it means that the breakthrough has been confirmed, and then enter the market with the trend.

> ⚠️ It must be emphasized: **All the above confirmation methods can only increase the probability, and none of them can 100% eliminate false breakthroughs. ** The stricter the confirmation (such as waiting to step back), the fewer false breakthroughs, but at the cost of entering the market later and possibly missing part of the market; the looser the confirmation, the earlier the market entry, but the more false breakthroughs. It's a trade-off and there's no perfect answer.

## 6. Entry and Stop Loss

The entry and exit logic of the breakout strategy:

- **Entry**: When the breakthrough is confirmed (according to your rules), enter the market in the direction of the breakthrough.
- **Stop Loss (Key)**: Stop Loss is usually set on the other side of the **key level** - that is, the position where the "breakthrough is falsified". For example, after an upward breakthrough, the Stop Loss is set a little below the breakthrough level; **Once the price shrinks back to the key level (the breakthrough fails and becomes a false breakthrough), the Stop Loss is strictly enforced**.

![F13 with picture 3](/academy-img/F13-配图3.png)

The logic of this Stop Loss is very natural: the premise for your entry is that "the breakthrough is established", then "the breakthrough is falsified (the price retracts)" is a signal that you should admit your mistake. **Breakthrough strategies especially rely on strict Stop Loss** - Because false breakthroughs are frequent, without Stop Loss, losses will accumulate quickly if you are trapped again and again.

(Remind again: The entry points and Stop Loss levels mentioned above are **logical diagrams/areas**, not signals of "buy at this precise price and sell at that precise price". In practice, it must be combined with your own rules, product characteristics and risk management.)

## 7. Breakthrough vs. Retracement: Complementarity of two trend-oriented entries

Comparing breakthrough entry (this article) and callback entry (mentioned on F8/F12) together, they are two complementary opportunities to enter the market following the trend:

| | Breakout entry | Pullback entry |
|---|---|---|
| Timing | Chase when the price breaks through the key level | Buy when the price pulls back to support/Moving Average |
| Nature | Right side, follow-up confirmation | Cheaper than left side, etc. |
| Advantages | Higher certainty, less likely to miss the start of the trend | Better cost, potentially better profit-loss ratio |
| Disadvantages | Poor cost, easy to encounter false breakthroughs | May not wait for the correction, difficult to judge during the correction |

There is no absolute advantage or disadvantage between the two. Many mature trend playing methods will be used in combination - using different entry methods in different situations.

## 8. Application and risks of breakout trading

**Suitable market**: **The moment when the trend starts and the market changes**. When the market does turn from shock to trend and key levels are effectively broken through, the breakthrough strategy can capture this market situation earlier.

**Unsuitable market/biggest risk**: **Continuous volatile market**. False breakthroughs are frequent in range oscillations, and the breakthrough strategy will suffer repeated false breakthroughs and Stop Loss, which is the environment where it suffers the most.

**Other Risks**:

- **Leverage amplifies risk**: Use leverage to chase breakthroughs on contracts. Once there is a false breakthrough and the price reverses quickly, ⚠️ leverage will **equally amplify losses and even trigger Liquidation** - Never use high leverage just because "the breakthrough looks fierce".
- **Emotions of chasing highs/selling lows**: When a breakthrough occurs, the market is fast and can easily trigger the impulse of "fear of missing out", leading to blind pursuit without following the rules.
