# Discipline and Execution: The Gap Between Knowing and Doing

> Midas Academy · Level 5 · Module 13 Trading System and Practical Combat

**Quick overview of this article**

- There is a gap between "knowing" and "doing". Crossing it requires **discipline**; what hinders execution is **emotion, luck, and laziness**.
- **The Essence of Discipline**: Follow the rules set in advance, and do not change your mind temporarily based on emotions on the spot (do it firmly when you need to do it, and stop when you need to stop).
- Cultivate discipline: **Mechanization of rules, planning in advance and executing during the event, supervision of transaction logs, and practice from small steps**.
- **Excessive trading** is a typical example of a loss of discipline (cost erosion, emotional fatigue, deviation from the system); not trading when there is no opportunity is also a correct operation. (This article emphasizes execution and does not promise returns.)

## 1. Concept introduction: I understand the principles, but I just can’t do it.

"Follow the trend", "Stop Loss", "Don't take heavy positions", "Don't chase highs" - these principles are understood by almost every trader. But as soon as the real offer comes, we will still chase the high, still carry the orders, and still have full positions.

**There is a huge gap between "knowing" and "doing". ** The bridge across this chasm is **discipline**. This article talks about: Why you know but can’t do it, and how to make yourself truly “do it”.

## 2. Why "know" but "can't do it"

There are three main things between "knowing" and "doing":

1. **Emotions** (see F5): Fear and greed hijack your judgment at critical moments - the truth is in your head, but your hands are not in control.
2. **Lucky**: I know I should stop loss, but I think "what if I can come back this time"; I know I shouldn't chase, but I think "just this time". Luck mentality makes people find excuses for breaking the rules.
3. **Lazy**: It’s too troublesome to plan, too troublesome to write a log, and too boring to wait for signals, so I place orders hastily based on my feelings.

The superposition of these three things makes "I understand it all" an empty statement. **What truly distinguishes the level of a trader is often not how much they understand, but how much they can execute. **

## 3. The essence of discipline: execution according to established rules

So what is discipline? **The essence of discipline is to "implement according to the rules set in advance and not change your mind temporarily based on emotions on the spot." **

It contains two layers:

- **What should be done, do it resolutely**: execute the signal if it complies with the rules, and don’t miss it out of fear.
- **If you should stop, stop resolutely**: Leave the market when Stop Loss is reached, and don't hold on to it just because of luck; if there is no opportunity that meets the rules, just wait for a short position, and don't do anything recklessly.

Discipline is not "stronger willpower", but **respect for "the one who sets the rules when he is calm"**. You are rational in the planning stage, and what you have to do in the execution stage is not to betray that rational decision.

## 4. How to develop discipline

Discipline is not innate, it can be deliberately trained. Several effective methods:

1. **Mechanize the rules and reduce subjectivity**: Make the rules as clear and measurable as possible ("leave if it falls below a certain line" rather than "leave if it feels wrong"). The more objective the rules are, the less room there is for emotion and luck to take advantage of the situation.
2. **Plan in advance and only execute during the process** (F2): Make decisions in advance until you are calm, and only do "according to the rules" during execution.
3. **Use transaction log to supervise**: For each stroke, record "whether the rules were followed and where the precepts were broken." Records in black and white are the most honest mirrors that can continuously expose and correct your implementation problems.
4. **Start small and accumulate positive feedback**: First practice "strict execution" into muscle memory in a low-risk (or even virtual) environment, so that the benefits of observing discipline will slowly outweigh the urge to break discipline.

## 5. Over-trading: a typical example of loss of discipline

One of the most common manifestations of a loss of discipline is **over-trading** - frequent and uncontrolled buying and selling. Its dangers are very real:

- **Cost Erosion**: Each Stroke has a handling fee (contract and funding fee). The more frequent the transaction, the more cost erosion it will cause.
- **Emotion and Fatigue**: People who are addicted to watching the market and operate frequently will become tired, their judgment will be distorted, and they will be more likely to make mistakes.
- **Deviation from the system**: Good opportunities that truly comply with the rules are limited; trading too frequently often means that you are making a large number of impulse orders "outside the rules".

Restraining over-trading is itself an important part of discipline: only do opportunities that comply with the rules. When there are no opportunities, "not trading" is also a correct operation. **
