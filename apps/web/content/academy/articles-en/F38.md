## 2. Trading is a long-term continuous practice

First establish a basic understanding: ** trading is a long-term matter. **

- It is not "win or lose with one or two Strokes" - the result of a single Stroke is full of luck (recall F7's right result); it is a **long-term, probability game** that compares with your overall results after enough transactions.
- Like any profound skill - doctors, craftsmen, athletes - it is not something that can be achieved in a few days, but relies on long-term accumulation and continuous polishing to become stronger bit by bit.

By lengthening the time scale, you can stop being emotionally influenced by the winning or losing of a single stroke and focus on "getting every stroke right and letting your abilities grow slowly."

## 3. What can we rely on to achieve long-term success: Unify the core of the training camp into one

This entire training camp, in the final analysis, is to teach you what you need to survive in the market for the long term. Twist the core into four sentences:

**1. Survive by the system (continue from F1). **
Don't rely on inspiration, luck, or some magic indicator, but rely on a system that is systematic, repeatable, and risk-controllable. The system allows your transactions to be followed, tested, and improved - this is the foundation for long-term stability.

**2. Rely on discipline (continue from F6). **
No matter how good the system is, if it cannot do it, it will be zero. **Discipline is the bridge that truly puts the "right method" into practice** - follow the rules, don't be carried away by emotions, stop loss when it's time to stop loss, and go short if there is no chance.

**3. Make progress through review (then F7). **
**Learn from every Stroke transaction (especially losses) and continue to iterate** - This is the engine for your ability growth. If you don't review the market, you will stand still and make mistakes again and again. Only by persisting in reviewing the market can you move forward more steadily.

**4. Rely on risk control to save your life (continue from F4, E9). **
**Controlling risks and surviving are always the first priority. ** Control the risk of single stroke, do not take heavy positions, and do not gamble on your life - because only by living can you have a future and have the opportunity to let your ability and compound interest slowly come into play.

## 4. Long-termism vs. short-term gambling

Putting the two roads side by side, the difference is clear at a glance:

- **Short-term gambling**: Pursue getting rich overnight, take heavy positions, high leverage, and gamble on one direction - **High probability of rapid loss, Liquidation will be out** (Review E8, F24). It's about luck, and luck is unsustainable.
- **Long-termism**: Do every stroke well, develop your abilities solidly, and control risks - **Get better slowly and steadily**. It compares to system, discipline and patience, which can be accumulated and compounded.

**Choose long-termism, not because it is faster - on the contrary, it is slower; but because it is more likely to allow you to "survive to the end." ** In the game of trading, living long is the greatest advantage in itself.
