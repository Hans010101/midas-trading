# No Holy Grail: Matching Strategy with Market Environment

> Midas Academy · Level 5 · Module 16 Trading Strategy Types

**Quick overview of this article**

- **There is no "Holy Grail"** - There is no strategy that can consistently make money in all markets, at all times; this is just logically impossible.
- Reason 1: **Each strategy has its applicable boundaries** (trend strategy needs trend, shock strategy needs shock), and the two types of profit logic are contradictory and cannot be effective at the same time.
- Reason two: **Strategy effectiveness will decay** (the market will change, public methods will be used); **What was effective in the past does not mean it will be effective in the future**, and history will not simply repeat itself.
- Real core capabilities: **Identify the market environment + Use the right strategy in the right environment + Accept that "no strategy is effective around the clock" + Use a complete system to make up for the limitations of a single strategy**; and admit that "identifying the environment will make mistakes".
- ⚠️ Red line: There is no holy grail strategy that guarantees profit; the effectiveness of strategies declines with the market; what worked in the past does not guarantee the future; any strategy does not guarantee profitability and needs to be coordinated with risk management.

## 1. Concept introduction: Stop looking for the "strongest strategy"

After learning a circle of strategies - trend following, breakthroughs, mean reversion, grids, left and right sides - many people will have an idea in their minds:

**"Then...which strategy is the most powerful? I just learn the strongest one, right?"**

This article is dedicated to breaking this idea. It tells you a perhaps less pleasant, but extremely important fact: There is no Holy Grail. ** There is no "strongest strategy that can guarantee profits in any market at any time".

Pain points are very common: some people are looking for "sure-win systems", "strongest indicators" and "guaranteed profit strategies" everywhere; some people immediately change one strategy to another after losing money, always chasing new magic skills and always in the cycle of losing money. This article helps you break out of this cycle.

## 2. What is the "Holy Grail" fantasy?

The "Holy Grail", borrowing the image of the legendary "universal artifact", refers to what many people dream of - a trading strategy that can make money stably in all markets, all quotes, and at all times. **

Such a thing **doesn't exist**. It's not that "it hasn't been discovered yet", it's that it's logically impossible to exist. Why? This starts with "every strategy has applicable boundaries".

## 3. Core reason 1: Each strategy has its applicable boundaries

Review what we have learned (taking over from F8 and F9):

- **Trend Strategy** (Trend Tracking, Breakthrough) **Needs Trending Markets** - During the shock, it will repeatedly make false breakthroughs, Stop Loss, and be slapped in the face.
- **Shock strategy** (mean reversion, grid) **needs to oscillate the market** - in the trend, it will be crushed in the opposite direction (mean reversion against the trend, grid unilaterally getting deeper and deeper, or even Liquidation).

The key is: **The profit logic of these two types of strategies is contradictory**——

- Trend strategy bets on "the deviation will continue";
- The shock strategy bets on "the deviation will return".

One bets on continuation and the other bets on regression, **they cannot be correct at the same time**. Therefore, there is no strategy that can perform well in both trends and shocks at the same time - this logically excludes the possibility of being "effective all day long".

## 4. Core reason 2: The effectiveness of the strategy will decay

Even in the market where it is suitable, a strategy cannot always be effective. Because:

**1. The market is dynamic. **
The market's participant structure, liquidity, regulatory policies, technology (algorithmic trading)... are all constantly changing. The market characteristics on which a strategy relies may disappear as circumstances change.

**2. Methods that are widely used will gradually become ineffective. **
This is a profound phenomenon: when a set of public and mechanical ** methods are known and used by more and more people, its effectiveness is often "used" - everyone does it, and this "edge" is smoothed out. The turtle rule mentioned in F12 is an example: it was extremely effective back then, but as the method became public and the market changed, the parameters back then were no longer as effective as they were back then.

**3. "What was valid in the past does not mean it will always be valid in the future."**
This red line throughout the training camp is especially important here. **History will not simply repeat**. Any strategy that has performed well in the past (including backtesting) is not guaranteed to "continue to be effective in the future" (this is the core warning of the F18 backtesting part).

## 5. What is the real core competency?

If there is no Holy Grail, what is the difference between masters and novices? **It’s not about “finding the strongest strategy”, it’s about the following abilities:**

**1. Identify the current market environment. **
"Is the market trending now or is it a volatile market? Which type of market is it?" - This is the **prerequisite** for using the right strategy. Only when you judge the environment correctly can you use the right strategy.

**2. Use the right strategy in the right environment. **
Use trend strategies for trending markets and shock strategies for oscillating markets; in markets that are not suitable for your own strategy, lower your expectations, reduce operations, or even wait for a short position. "Doing less or not doing anything in unsuitable market conditions" is itself an advanced ability (echoing F6: Not trading when there is no opportunity is also a correct operation).

**3. Accept the fact that "no strategy works 24/7." **
Don’t insist on making money in every market, and don’t panic and completely deny the strategy just because it fails in a certain period of time. **Accepting strategies has its "harvest season" and "bad harvest season"**. In the bad harvest season, you will be less injured, survive it, and wait for your own season.

**4. Use the integrity of the "system" to make up for the limitations of a single strategy. **
Back to F1: strategy is only part of the system. No matter how good the strategy is, it will sometimes fail, but a complete system combined with **risk management (F4), discipline (F6), and review and iteration (F7, F10)** can prevent you from collapse when a single strategy fails and continue to adjust and adapt. **Survive by relying on the system, not relying on a single strategy. **

> ⚠️ An honest supplement: **"Recognizing the market environment" itself is also difficult, there is a lag, and judgments may be wrong** - What you think is a shock may be the beginning of a trend. So even "identifying the environment" is not the new Holy Grail. It also requires risk management and trial and error. **It is a mature mentality to admit that "you will make mistakes in judgment" and to be prepared for the risks of making mistakes. **
