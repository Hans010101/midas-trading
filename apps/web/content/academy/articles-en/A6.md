# What are Support and Resistance Levels

> Midas Academy · Phase 2 · Module 5 Trends and Structure (Basics)

**Quick Overview**

- **Support**: A buying cluster below the price; price tends to stop falling and bounce here ("the floor").
- **Resistance**: A selling cluster above the price; price tends to be blocked and fall back here ("the ceiling").
- **Mutual Conversion**: Resistance often becomes support after a successful upward breakout; support often becomes resistance after a successful downward breakdown (roles swap with the breakout direction).
- **Identification Clues**: Previous highs and lows, dense trading zones, and repeatedly tested levels.
- They are **probabilistic reference zones, not ironclad rules**; price may bounce or break through, so combine with other information and prepare for failure.

## I. Concept Introduction: Price Has a "Floor" and a "Ceiling"

Price does not move aimlessly on a chart. It often gets "stuck" at certain levels—when it falls near a certain price, it stops dropping, like stepping on a **floor**; when it rises near a certain price, it stops climbing, like hitting a **ceiling**.

These two positions are among the most fundamental and important concepts in trading:

- The "floor" below the price is called **Support**.
- The "ceiling" above the price is called **Resistance**.

Understanding them is the starting point for reading price structure and learning trends.

## II. Support: The Buying Cluster Below the Price

**Support** is a **cluster of buying power** located below the price.

Why does price tend to "stop falling" when it reaches here? Because near this price level, more people are willing to buy—some think, "This price is cheap enough to buy." Buying power strengthens while selling power weakens, offsetting the downward momentum of the price, so it is easy for the price to **stop falling and even bounce** here.

You can understand it as: **Support is the area where "buyers are willing to step in to support the price."**

## III. Resistance: The Selling Cluster Above the Price

**Resistance** is the exact opposite: a **cluster of selling power** located above the price.

Price tends to "stop rising" when it reaches here because near this price level, more people are willing to sell—some think, "I should sell now that it has risen to this price," while others are trapped at highs and finally want to exit to break even. Selling power strengthens, suppressing the upward momentum of the price, so it is easy for the price to **be blocked and even fall back** here.

You can understand it as: **Resistance is the area where "sellers tend to exit and suppress the price."**

## IV. Key Point: Support and Resistance Convert to Each Other

This is the most exciting and practical feature of support and resistance: **Once the price effectively breaks through, their roles swap.**

- When price **breaks upward** through a resistance level, this former "ceiling" often becomes a new "floor"—that is, **Resistance becomes Support**. The logic is: the selling pressure that existed here has been digested; once the price stands above it, this position becomes support during pullbacks.
- Conversely, when price **breaks downward** through a support level, this former "floor" often becomes a new "ceiling"—that is, **Support becomes Resistance**.

Remember this picture: **Before the breakout, it is a top (Resistance); after the breakout, it becomes a bottom (Support). Before the breakdown, it is a bottom (Support); after the breakdown, it becomes a top (Resistance).** Roles swap with the breakout direction.

## V. How to Identify Support and Resistance Levels

Support and resistance are not a precise line, but a **zone**. Common identification clues include:

- **Previous Highs and Lows**: Previous highs where price rose to the top and fell back are often subsequent resistance; previous lows where price fell to the bottom and bounced are often subsequent support.
- **Dense Trading Zones**: Areas where the price has been consolidating for a long time with high volume mean a large amount of chips have changed hands here, often forming strong support or resistance.
- **Repeatedly Tested Levels**: If a price level is touched and reversed multiple times by the price, it indicates that its support/resistance function has been repeatedly verified.

## VI. Important Reminder: It is "Probability," Not "Ironclad Law"

Finally, this must be emphasized, as it is the most easily misunderstood part for beginners:

**Support and resistance levels are "probabilistic reference zones," not guarantees that "price will definitely bounce/resist here."**

When price reaches a support level, it may bounce, or it may break through directly; when it reaches a resistance level, it may fall back, or it may break through on high volume. Support and resistance tell you that "**historically, the balance of buying and selling power changed here, so it is worth paying close attention**," not that "it will definitely happen this way."

The correct usage is to treat it as a **reference coordinate for observation and decision-making**, combined with trends, volume, and other signals for judgment, and always be prepared for "it failing (being broken through)"—rather than treating it as a foolproof entry or exit point.

## VII. Key Takeaways

- **Support**: A buying cluster below the price; price tends to stop falling and bounce here ("the floor").
- **Resistance**: A selling cluster above the price; price tends to be blocked and fall back here ("the ceiling").
- **Mutual Conversion**: Resistance often becomes support after a successful upward breakout; support often becomes resistance after a successful downward breakdown (roles swap with the breakout direction).
- **Identification Clues**: Previous highs and lows, dense trading zones, and repeatedly tested levels.
- They are **probabilistic reference zones, not ironclad rules**; price may bounce or break through, so combine with other information and prepare for failure.
