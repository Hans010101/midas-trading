# Review: Learning from Every Trade

> Midas Academy · Level 5 · Module 13 Trading System and Practical Combat

**Quick overview of this article**

- **Review is the core engine of progress**: Turn each Stroke transaction into a learning sample to avoid repeated mistakes.
- The review should distinguish between **wrong judgment (change methods) and wrong execution (change discipline)**, and examine the impact of **emotions**.
- **Doing the right thing but not the result** (key point): Look at the right or wrong process rather than a single profit or loss - the most dangerous thing is "making money while doing it wrong" (relying on luck, reinforcing bad habits), while "losing money while doing it right" is acceptable (the probability fluctuates, continue to persevere).

## 1. Concept introduction: progress comes from looking back

Why do some people trade for many years and still stand still, while others become more and more mature? The difference often lies in one thing: whether ** has carefully reviewed the transaction. **

For transactions without review, each stroke is isolated and forgotten after being done, and mistakes will be repeated again and again. However, review turns each stroke transaction into a "learning sample", allowing you to truly develop abilities from experience. **Review is the core engine for traders' progress. ** This article talks about how to review and the most important principle in review.

## 2. What should be reviewed in the review?

The review is not to simply look at "whether Stroke made a profit or a loss", but to ask a few questions in depth:

- **Why it’s right or wrong**: Was this Stroke trade a good one? What’s good and what’s the problem?
- **Is it a wrong judgment or wrong execution? ** This is a crucial point——
  - **Wrong judgment**: There is a problem with the analysis and rules themselves (such as looking at the wrong trend).
  - **Execution Error**: The rules are correct, but you did not follow them (for example, you did not stop loss as planned, ran ahead of schedule, and added positions randomly).
  - The improvement directions of the two are completely different: if the judgment is wrong, the method must be changed, and if the execution is wrong, the discipline must be changed.
- **How ​​emotions affected this Stroke transaction**: Have you been led away by fear, greed, or luck?

Only by thinking these things through can review be valuable - only then will you know what to change next time.

## 3. The most important principle: Don’t focus on the matter, not the result.

This is the most important and counter-intuitive point in review: To evaluate a Stroke transaction, you must look at "whether the process was correct", rather than just "whether you made money this time". **

Why? Because the result of a single Stroke transaction **contains a lot of luck** - if the process is correct, you may lose money due to probability, and if the process is wrong, you may make money due to luck. If you only judge by "profit/loss", you will come to a completely wrong conclusion.

Combining "right or wrong process" and "profit and loss results", there are four situations (this is the famous "four quadrants"):

| | Make money | Lose money |
|---|---|---|
| **The process is right** | ✅ Ideal: Right and profitable | 🟡 Acceptable: The process is right, losses are probabilities, continue to persevere |
| **The process is wrong** | ⚠️ Danger: Earning money by luck will reinforce bad habits | ❌ Must change: The process and results are both wrong |

What you need to be most vigilant about is the "make money by doing something wrong" - it is the most dangerous, because luck will make you mistakenly think that the wrong approach is effective, so you will continue to repeat it, and sooner or later it will be taken back by the market. As for "losing money if you do it right", it is acceptable: as long as the process complies with the system, a single loss is just a normal fluctuation of probability, and you should continue to persist.

**Conclusion: The correct process is more important than a single result. ** The goal of review is to continuously polish the "process" rather than worrying about the gains and losses of a single win or loss.

## 4. How to review: a cycle

Review is a cyclical process that can be fixed:

1. **Record**: Write down the plan and execution when trading (continue from F2’s transaction log).
2. **Review**: Looking back afterwards - what you thought at the time, what you did, and what the results were.
3. **Summary**: Whether the judgment process is right or wrong, whether it is a judgment problem or an execution problem, and what role emotions play.
4. **Improvement**: Turn conclusions into specific adjustments to rules or disciplines.
5. → Enter the next Stroke with improvements, record again, review again...and so on.

This cycle of "recording → reviewing → summarizing → improving" continues to rotate, and your system and execution capabilities become stronger in rounds of iterations.
