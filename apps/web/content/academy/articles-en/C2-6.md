# Trading Contracts in Trending Markets

> Midas Academy · C2 Contract Case Library (Virtual Suggestion Review)

The previous articles are mostly "negative teaching materials". This article changes the flavor and looks at a **relatively smooth trend trading case** - in a clear upward trend, how **following the trend, low leverage, moving Stop Loss, and batched Take Profit** cooperate. But please note: **Smooth ≠ Inevitable, this is just a demonstration of "how to do it", not "you will definitely make money if you do this". **

> ⚠️ The entire process is virtual/indicative, no real targets are named, no predictions of real market conditions are made, and no recommendations are constituted.

## 1. Scene setting

Set up a period of **indicative of an upward trend** (digital fiction):

- A certain variety has embarked on a clear upward trend, rising from **100 to 130** (the highs and lows are constantly rising, corresponding to the upward trend of C1-4);
- Trader H **judges that this is an upward trend and decides to go Long**;
- The key is - he **used low leverage**: **3 times** (theoretical Liquidation price of long orders ≈ 100×(1−1/3) ≈ **67**, far below, leaving enough room for error in judgment, compare to C2-1).

## 2. Operation process (Follow the trend + Low leverage + Mobile Stop Loss + Take Profit in batches)

The whole process of H was very "restrained":

- **Entry**: He does not chase the highest point, but waits for the price to stabilize after a correction (such as a correction to stand firm near **105**) and go long with the trend, and determines the initial Stop Loss before entering the market (for example, if it falls below the correction low and leaves the market);
- **Hold + Move Stop Loss**: The price continues to rise, H **Move the Stop Loss up stepwise** with the trend (for example, move the Stop Loss to 108 when it rises to 115, and move it to 118 when it rises to 125) - not only **lock in the existing floating profits**, but also **give the trend room to continue to run**;
- **Take Profit** in batches**: When the price reaches **120**, H **first close part of the position and pocket part of the profit**, **continue to hold the remaining position**, and follow the mobile Stop Loss;
- **Exit**: The price started to weaken after reaching around **130**, **falling below the moving Stop Loss** (for example, falling below 122) → H **Close the remaining positions according to the rules** and leave the market.

In the end, H made a profit for a period of time in this trend - neither selling at the highest point of 130, nor being washed out in the midway correction.

## 3. Dismantling of key nodes

**Node 1: Low leverage - leaving room for error in judgment. **
H uses 3 times leverage, Liquidation price ≈ 67, far below. This means that a **normal callback** in the middle of the trend (such as a callback from 115 to 108) **cannot touch the Liquidation line** at all, and H can hold it with peace of mind. Comparing with B of C2-1 (50 times, Liquidation≈98), we also look at the trend, **low leverage can be held, but high leverage has long been washed out**. **In the trend market, low leverage is not "timid", but "to hold on". **

**Node 2: Mobile Stop Loss - let profits run while protecting results. **
H does not preset "all must be sold when the price reaches a certain price", but follows with **moving Stop Loss**: Stop Loss moves up with the price, **not only locks in floating profits, but does not limit the space for the trend to continue to rise**. This is the essence of trend trading - **cut losses and let profits run** (echoing Group F trend play).

![C2-6 with picture 2](/academy-img/C2-6-配图2.png)

**Node 3: Take Profit in batches - balance "falling into the bag" and "running". **
By 120, H had partly leveled off - this solved the most tangled contradiction among traders: "wanting to be safe in the pocket" and "fear of missing out on selling too soon". **Take Profit in batches allows you to take care of both ends**: the profit is locked in for the flat part, and the remaining part continues to enjoy the trend. It does not pursue "selling at the highest point" (that is unrealistic), but **divides profits into segments and secures a portion**.

**Node 4: Not sold at the highest point - normal. **
H exited at 122 and did not sell at the high of 130. **This is the normal cost of trend following** - moving Stop Loss must involve the period of "falling back from the highest point to Stop Loss" (echoing F26). It is an unrealistic fantasy to "sell at the highest point"; **According to the rules, leaving the market after the trend goes bad and getting most of the middle price is already a successful execution. **

## 4. Key points of review

- **The "four-piece set" for making contracts in the trend**: **Follow the trend** (don't go against the trend) + **Low leverage** (leave room for error in judgment and be able to hold) + **Mobile Stop Loss** (lock profits and give room for profit) + **Take Profit** in batches (take into account both pocketing and running);
- **Low leverage is for "holding"**: There is a normal callback in the middle of the trend, so low leverage will not be liquidated/washed out during the callback (compare C2-1);
- **Move Stop Loss = Cut off losses and let profits run**: Moving Stop Loss up with the trend is the core technique of trend trading;
- **It is normal not to sell at the highest point**: Moving Stop Loss will inevitably give up the top section. It is good to leave the market according to the rules and get most of the middle part;
- ★**But smooth ≠ inevitable**: This is a demonstration of "how to do it", **does not mean that you will definitely make money** by doing this - the trend may end early, and may falsely break through the Stop Loss as soon as you enter the market; trend following has a low winning rate, and you must rely on the profit-loss ratio to win (F12). **No guarantee of profit or prediction. **

One sentence to end: **Making money in the trending market does not rely on "catching at the lowest and selling at the highest", but "following the trend, holding on with low leverage, and letting the mobile Stop Loss and batched Take Profit help you collect the profits." ** But always remember - no matter how good the method is, there is no guarantee that you will make money every time.
