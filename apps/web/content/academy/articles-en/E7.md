# Basis and Spread: The Price Relationship Between Perpetual and Spot

> Midas Academy · Level 4 · Module 12 Contracts and Derivatives

## 1. Concept introduction: Perpetual price and spot price are not always equal.

We say that Perpetual Contract "anchors" the spot by Funding Rate, but "anchoring" does not mean "completely equal". At any time, there is often a small gap between the price of the Perpetual Contract and the spot price. This gap is the **basis** (also often called the spread). Observing it can help us understand whether sustainability is "more expensive" or "cheaper" than spot prices.

## 2. What is the basis: perpetual price minus spot price

In order to unify the caliber, this article defines it as follows:

**Basis = Perpetual Contract price − Spot (index) price. **

- The basis ** is positive **: the perpetual price ** is higher than ** spot - the perpetual relative spot ** premium ** (more expensive).
- The basis **is negative**: the perpetual price** is lower than** the spot price - the perpetual price is at a discount** (cheaper) relative to the spot price.
- Basis **close to 0**: the perpetual price and the spot price are basically consistent.

## 3. The meaning of positive and negative basis

The positive and negative of the basis reflects the strength and weakness of the contract market relative to the spot:

- **Positive Basis (Premium)**: Perpetual is bought more expensively than spot, which often reflects the relatively stronger willingness to **Go Long** in the contract market.
- **Negative basis (discount)**: Perpetual is cheaper than spot, which often reflects the relatively stronger willingness to **Go Short** in the contract market, or a strong risk aversion sentiment.

But it should also be emphasized: this is a reference perspective, not a prediction. A positive basis does not mean it will definitely rise, and a negative basis does not mean it will definitely fall.

![E7 with picture 2](/academy-img/E7-配图2.png)

## 4. The relationship between basis and Funding Rate

Basis and **Funding Rate** discussed in the previous article are actually two sides of the same thing, and are highly related in direction:

- When the perpetual **contango** (positive basis), the Funding Rate is usually **positive** (longs pay to shorts) - by making the longs pay, the higher perpetual price is pulled down and closer to the spot.
- When the perpetual **discount** (negative basis), the Funding Rate is usually **negative** (shorts pay to longs) - pulling up the low perpetual price.

It can be understood this way: **basis describes "how much the perpetual deviation deviates from the spot and in which direction", and the Funding Rate is the "hand" that pulls it back. ** Together, they maintain a permanent anchor to the spot.

![E7 with picture 3](/academy-img/E7-配图3.png)

## 5. Basis as a reference

Basis is a useful perspective to observe the "contract vs spot" price relationship: a sustained, large premium or discount can help you sense the emotional tendency of the contract market relative to the spot. However, like Funding Rate, OI, and the long-short ratio, it is a reference data for sentiment/structure. It does not predict prices and does not constitute a basis for buying and selling. It needs to be viewed comprehensively in conjunction with other information.

## 6. Main points of this article

- **Basis = Perpetual price − Spot price**; Positive basis = Perpetual premium (more expensive), Negative basis = Perpetual discount (cheaper).
- The positive and negative sides of the basis reflect the strength and weakness of the contract relative to the spot (premium is long, discount is short), but it is a reference and not a prediction**.
- The basis is highly correlated with the direction of **Funding Rate**: premiums are often accompanied by positive rates, and discounts are often accompanied by negative rates - Funding Rate is the "hand" that pulls the perpetual price back to the spot.
- The basis is one of the **reference data** and needs to be combined with other information and does not constitute operational advice.
