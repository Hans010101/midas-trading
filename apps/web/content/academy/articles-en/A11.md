## I. Concept Introduction: Two Completely Different Ways to Buy Coins

In the crypto market, there are mainly two ways to "participate in a coin's trend": **Spot Trading** or **Futures Trading**. Although both are linked to price fluctuations, the underlying mechanisms, risks, and capabilities differ significantly.

This section first clarifies Spot and Futures, then addresses a product-level question: **Why does Midas Crypto focus primarily on Futures analysis with Spot as a supplement?** (Note: Midas is a virtual trading analysis terminal; it uses virtual funds throughout and involves no real trading.)

## II. Spot: Buying and Truly Holding the Asset

**Spot Trading** means using funds to **buy and truly hold** a specific crypto asset.

Its logic is similar to buying stocks or physical goods:

- After you buy, the asset **belongs to you** and is stored in your account.
- Your profit or loss comes from **price difference**: you sell for a profit after the price rises, or realize a floating loss if it falls.
- **Generally only Long**: You must hold first to sell after the price rises. Shorting (selling without holding) usually requires borrowing, which is restricted.
- **No leverage by default, so no "Liquidation"**: A price drop is just a paper loss; as long as you don't sell, the asset remains. You won't be forced to close your position because a margin deposit is wiped out.

Simply put, Spot is a relatively "simple" method: **holding the real thing and profiting (or losing) from its price movements.**

## III. Futures (Perpetual): A Leveraged, Two-Way "Price Agreement"

**Futures** (here referring primarily to the most common **Perpetual Futures** in the crypto market) are completely different. It doesn't make you hold the coin itself, but rather a **protocol revolving around price movements**. It has several key characteristics:

- **No physical asset held**: You are trading "price fluctuations," not the coin itself.
- **Leverage**: Using a smaller margin to control a larger position, amplifying profit and loss (see the Leverage section).
- **Can Long or Short**: It is convenient to open Long when bullish or Short when bearish; both directions offer participation opportunities.
- **Funding Rate**: This is a mechanism unique to Perpetual Futures.
- **Risk of Liquidation**: Because of leverage, if the price moves inversely to a certain extent, the margin may be wiped out, and the position will be forcibly closed.

Regarding **Funding Rate**, a brief explanation: Perpetual Futures have no expiration date. To prevent the contract price from deviating from the spot price for a long time, a "Funding Rate" is introduced—**between Longs and Shorts, a fee is paid to each other at regular intervals (specific cycle varies by platform)**. Note that this money is **transferred between Longs and Shorts**, not a fee paid to the platform.

> ⚠️ Must be clear: Futures carry leverage, and the risk is significantly higher than Spot. While it amplifies profits, it equally amplifies losses, and there is a possibility of Liquidation (losing the principal). Beginners must understand the risks first; see the relevant sections on Leverage and Futures Risk Management.

## IV. Spot vs. Futures: A Table to See the Differences

| Dimension | Spot | Futures (Perpetual) |
|---|---|---|
| Hold Physical Asset | Yes, truly hold | No, it is a price agreement |
| Leverage | No leverage by default | Has leverage |
| Direction | Generally only Long | Can Long, Can Short |
| Funding Rate | None | Yes (paid periodically between Longs and Shorts) |
| Liquidation Risk | None (unrealized loss until sold) | Yes (leverage amplifies, possible forced closure) |
| Risk Level | Relatively low | **Significantly higher** |

## V. Why Midas Crypto Focuses on "Futures Analysis"

Now that you understand both, you can understand Midas' product positioning. **Midas Crypto uses Perpetual Futures as the primary data source and analysis object, with Spot as a supplement.** The reason is not to "encourage you to take high-risk Futures," but because **Futures markets can provide richer analytical dimensions**:

1. **Open Interest (OI)**: The total size of open contracts in the market, reflecting changes in capital participation volume.
2. **Long/Short Ratio**: Metrics such as the Top-Trader Long/Short Ratio and Global Long/Short Account Ratio, which reflect current positioning across market participants.
3. **Funding Rate**: As mentioned earlier, it is paid between Longs and Shorts—and its level, positive or negative, is often used to **observe market sentiment indirectly** (for example, when Long sentiment is strong, it is often manifested as Longs paying Shorts).

These are "sentiment and structure" data that the Spot market struggles to provide but Futures markets possess naturally. Midas uses them to perform functions like **Long/Short Structure Analysis, Contract Dimension Charts, and AI Sandbox Diagnosis**, helping users understand the market more comprehensively.

## VI. Key Takeaways of This Article

- **Spot**: Buy and truly hold the asset, profit from price difference, generally only Long, no leverage or Liquidation by default, relatively low risk.
- **Futures (Perpetual)**: No physical asset, has leverage, can Long or Short, has Funding Rate, has Liquidation risk, **significantly higher risk**.
- **Funding Rate** is a unique mechanism of Perpetual Futures, paid periodically between Longs and Shorts, often used to observe sentiment indirectly.
- Midas Crypto **focuses on Futures analysis** because Futures provide richer analytical dimensions like OI, Long/Short Ratio, and Funding Rate; however, **it is fully virtual, AI analysis is for reference only, and does not constitute investment advice**.
