# Open Interest (OI): Tracking Participation in Derivatives

> Midas Academy · Level 4 · Module 12 Contracts and Derivatives

## 1. Concept introduction: "How much money is bet" in the market

The price tells you "how much money is now", the trading volume tells you "how much has been traded", and the position (OI, Open Interest) tells you a different thing: how many contracts are still in the market at this moment that have not been closed and are still being "betted". ** It is like a "fund thermometer", reflecting the volume of participation in the contract market.

## 2. What is open interest: the total amount of open positions

**Open interest (OI) refers to the total number of contracts currently in the market that have not yet been closed. **

Note the difference between it and trading volume:

- **Trading volume**: How many transactions were completed within a period of time (it is "traffic", which is recalculated every cycle).
- **Position**: How many positions are currently open (it is "stock", the total amount of open positions accumulated continuously).

One Stroke to open a new position will increase OI, and one Stroke to close a position will decrease OI.

## 3. The meaning of OI changes: increasing and decreasing positions

The increase or decrease in OI reflects whether funds are "entering" or "leaving":

- **OI increase (increased position)**: New positions are being established, which usually means **new funds entering the market** and increased participation.
- **OI reduction (position reduction)**: There are positions being closed or liquidated, which means **funds are leaving the market** and participation is declining.

OI itself is not good or bad, it just tells you "whether the volume of market participation is getting larger or smaller."

![E5 with picture 2](/academy-img/E5-配图2.png)

## 4. Look at the combination of OI and price (volume-price relationship)

Putting the changes in OI and the rise and fall of prices together is a common **reference** observation angle. The following are several commonly discussed combinations (please note: these are **reference interpretations, not predictions, not buying and selling signals**):

- **Price rise + OI increase**: The rise is accompanied by the entry of new funds, which is often interpreted as the participation of new forces in the rise.
- **Price increase + OI decrease**: The increase may come more from the liquidation of existing short positions (covering) than from new funds.
- **Price drop + OI increase**: The drop is accompanied by the establishment of new positions, which is often interpreted as new forces participating in the drop.
- **Price drop + OI reduction**: The drop may come more from the exit of the original long positions.

![E5 with picture 3](/academy-img/E5-配图3.png)

These combinations just help you gain an additional observation dimension and understand "whether new funds are entering the market or old positions are being closed" behind the price changes. They** have limitations and exceptions, and must not be used as predictions or operating basis** for rise or fall. They must be comprehensively judged in conjunction with price and other information.

## 5. OI is reference data, not a signal

It must be made clear: **Position volume is a reference data for analysis, not a buying and selling signal, let alone a price prediction. ** An increase in OI does not mean "should buy", and a decrease does not mean "should sell" - it just describes changes in the volume of market participation. It is a useful observation dimension in contract analysis (Midas's contract dimension chart will also display data such as OI, which is used for analysis reference and does not constitute investment advice).

## 6. Main points of this article

- **Open Interest (OI) = The total number of contracts that have not yet been closed in the market**, reflecting the volume of participants in the contract market ("stock", which is different from the "flow" of trading volume).
- **OI increase = increase the position (new funds enter the market); OI decrease = reduce the position (the funds leave the market)**.
- The combination of OI and price can be used for **reference interpretation** (new funds entering the market vs. closing of old positions), but there are limitations and exceptions. **It is not a prediction and it is not a signal**.
- It is a **reference dimension** for contract analysis and does not constitute any operational advice.
