# The Most Common Chan Theory Misreads

> Midas Academy · Advanced Chan Theory · Analysis of Difficulties

You understand the introductory concepts, and you have also learned the first batch of structural advancements in C1. But when it comes to drawing it on the picture - why is the Stroke I draw different from others? Why do I classify the same market differently this time than last time? Many so-called "thousands of people and thousands of entanglements" disagreements are actually rooted in misjudgments of basic skills. This article focuses on analyzing the most common misjudgments at the three levels of **Stroke, Segment, and Central Hub** to help you lay a solid foundation.

**Quick overview of this article**

- **Misjudgment of Stroke**: The number of K lines is not enough to connect the Stroke (over-division, requiring ≥ 3 K lines between Fractal / 5 K lines in total under the old standard), Fractal shares K lines, no inclusion processing is done, and the unconfirmed Stroke is regarded as established.
- **Segment misjudgment**: not enough three strokes/no overlap, **Feature Sequence is taken as the same direction Stroke (reverse Stroke should be taken)**, the first/second type destroys confusion, and Feature Sequence inclusion processing is not done.
- **Central Hub misjudgment**: ★**The upper and lower edges are inverted (ZG= the minimum value of the three-segment high point, ZD= the maximum value of the three-segment low point)**, ZG~ZD is confused with GG~DD, the number of segments is not enough, the level is wrong, and the extension/expansion is misjudged.
- Common root causes: **Insufficient basic skills + unclear levels + eagerness for success**; avoid relying on "according to the definition layer by layer, taking the value by heart, and comparing it with the original text".
- ⚠️ Chan Theory is an **analysis framework** with standard basic functions and cannot be scrawled; however, it does not predict and does not constitute a buying or selling order.

## 1. Stroke’s common misjudgments

**Misjudgment 1: If the number of K lines is not enough, Stroke is required. **
Most common. Forget the hard conditions of Stroke - **Between Top Fractal and Bottom Fractal, the new standard requires at least 3 K lines (excluding those shared by Fractal), and the old standard requires at least 1 independent K line, a total of 5**. If you see two high and low points at random and connect them into one stroke, it is a typical "over-division", which will completely mess up the subsequent Segments and Central Hubs.

**Misjudgement 2: Fractal shares the K line. **
Top Fractal and Bottom Fractal** cannot share the K line**. Some people count a K line into both the Top Fractal and the adjacent Bottom Fractal. This is wrong - the two Fractals must be independent.

**Misjudgement 3: Contact Fractal without performing K-line inclusion processing. **
Before looking for Fractal, you must first perform non-inclusion processing on the K-lines that have an **inclusive relationship** (take high and high upwards, and merge low and low downwards). If you skip this step and go directly to Fractal, Fractal will be looking for the wrong person, and Stroke will naturally be wrong too.

**Misjudgment 4: Treating "Unconfirmed Stroke" as established. **
Stroke requires Fractal confirmation to be established. It is easy to be overturned by the subsequent trend if Fractal believes that "this is a Stroke" before it comes out.

## 2. Common misjudgments of Segment

**Misjudgment 1: If there are not enough three Strokes, or there is no overlap, treat it as a Segment. **
The hard condition of Segment is **at least three Strokes, and the first Stroke and the third Stroke overlap**. As long as there is no overlapping interval between two Strokes or three Strokes, they do not constitute a Segment.

**Misjudgement 2: The direction of the Feature Sequence is reversed. **
This is what C1-1 repeatedly emphasizes - **Feature Sequence is composed of Strokes that are "opposite to the direction of the Segment": upward Segment takes **Downward Stroke**, and downward Segment takes **Upward Stroke**. If someone takes a Stroke in the same direction, the entire judgment of Segment destruction will be invalidated.

**Misjudgement 3: The first type of damage is confused with the second type of damage. **
**No gap** (the 1st and 2nd elements of Feature SequenceFractal overlap) → The Fractal is destroyed immediately after it is established; **There is a gap** → **Reverse Feature Sequence starting from the extreme point and then Fractal** need to be confirmed (see C1-1). It is a common mistake to directly confirm that there is a gap as if there is no gap.

**Misjudgment 4: Feature Sequence is not included. **
Feature Sequence must also be processed non-inclusively like K-line (to obtain standard Feature Sequence), and only on the same side of Fractal. If you skip this step, Fractal judgment will be wrong.

## 3. Common misjudgments of Central Hub (★including the most fatal low-level mistakes)

**Misjudgment 1 (the most fatal): Negate the upper and lower edges. **
The lowest but most common flaw in Chan Theory - **The upper edge of Central Hub ZG = the "minimum value" of three high points, and the lower edge of Central Hub ZD = the "maximum value" of three low points**. Many people mistakenly remember that "the upper edge is the highest and the lower edge is the lowest". As a result, the entire Central Hub range and the buying and selling point judgments that rely on ZG/ZD (such as 3 buying "without breaking ZG") are completely wrong. **This value must be set to death. **

**Misjudgment 2: Confusing ZG~ZD (Central Hub interval) and GG~DD (shock interval). **
ZG~ZD is the "core area" (small) of the three truly overlapping periods, and GG~DD (GG= the maximum value of the high points of the three periods, DD= the minimum value of the low points of the three periods) is the "outer frame" (large) of the fluctuations. The two have different uses (for example, see ZG~ZD for expansion, GG~DD for expansion, see C1-2), and errors will occur if they are mixed.

**Misjudgment 3: The number of Central Hub segments is insufficient. **
The Central Hub must be composed of at least three overlapping sub-level trends. If only two segments overlap, or the overlap zone is not established, it is not a Central Hub.

**Wrong judgment 4: The "level" that constitutes the Central Hub is wrong. **
What constitutes this level of Central Hub is the trend of the **sub-level**. If you surround the Central Hub by mistaking things of the same level or smaller level as sub-levels, the levels will be messed up (continue from C1-9).

**Misjudgement 5: Central Hub’s extension/expansion/new misjudgment. **
Without first looking at "Whether Three Types of Buy and Sell Points appears", you can judge whether it is an extension or an expansion (see C1-2). The order of judgment: first check whether there are Three Types of Buy and Sell Points (none = extension), and if there are, check back to ZG~ZD (expansion) or only GG~DD (expansion).

## 4. The common root causes of these miscarriages of justice

Putting the above misjudgments together, there are three root causes:

1. **Basic skills are not solid**: Skip the "troublesome but necessary" steps such as K-line inclusion and Feature Sequence processing;
2. **Unclear Level**: Unable to distinguish between the current level and sub-levels, resulting in mismatching of Stroke/Segment/Central Hub levels (continued from C1-9);
3. **Eager for success**: Drawing conclusions before confirming, or making hasty decisions in order to "quickly see the buying and selling points".

The accuracy of Chan Theory is precisely based on the strict definition of each layer - the K-line contains → Fractal → Stroke → Segment → Central Hub, and if one layer is wrong, the other will be wrong**.

## 5. How to avoid

- **Slow, follow the definition layer by layer**: include processing first, then Fractal, then Stroke, then Segment, then Central Hub, don’t skip steps;
- **Carve the value to death**: Especially Central Hub ZG/ZD (high point minimum value / low point maximum value), if it is wrong, it will be a flaw;
- **Please refer to the original text**: For complex and borderline cases, return to the original text of Chan Theory to figure it out;
- **Accept "there are differences in boundaries"**: The "thousands of people and thousands of entanglements" in boundary cases often have limited impact on operations (C1-2), so don't get too horny for a certain boundary; but **basic skills (values, Stroke conditions) are standardized and cannot be scribbled**.
