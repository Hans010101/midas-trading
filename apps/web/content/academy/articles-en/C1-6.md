# Understanding Buy/Sell Point Timeframes

> Midas Academy · Chan Theory Advanced · Practical Practice of Buying and Selling Points

Many people have learned to find Three Types of Buy and Sell Points, but still cannot do it well. A high-frequency reason is: **got the "level" of the buying and selling point wrong. ** Also called "Type 1 Buy Point", one appears on the daily line and the other appears on the 5-minute line. Their weights are very different. This article explains clearly: Why buying and selling points have levels, what are the differences in strength between different levels, and how to correspond to your own operating level. **

**Quick overview of this article**

- **The level of the buying and selling point = the level of the Divergence that produced it** (Theorem: Any Divergence creates a buying and selling point of a certain level, and any buying and selling point originates from a certain level of Divergence).
- **The larger the level, the stronger the buying and selling points, the larger the corresponding amplitude, and the more reliable, but the lower the frequency and the slower the confirmation**; small-level buying and selling points are frequent but have more noise and small amplitude.
- **Operating level** = the level you intend to operate/hold; it **must match** the level of the buying and selling point used (not higher or lower, but it must match).
- **Level mismatch** is a common source of losses: use small-level buying and selling points to make large-level expectations/positions, or vice versa; the correct approach is to "Go Long and use corresponding level buying and selling points for large levels."
- ⚠️ Buying and selling points are **structural terms/analytical frameworks, not operational instructions**; level judgment does not predict prices and needs to be included in the complete system.

## 1. The level of buying and selling points, from the level of Divergence

First answer "How do the levels of buying and selling points come from". Chan Theory has a very essential theorem:

> **Any Divergence must create a certain level of buying and selling points; any level of buying and selling points must originate from a certain level of trend Divergence. **

The translation is: **The level of the buying and selling point = the level of the trend (Divergence) that generated it. ** A 1 buy caused by daily level trend Divergence is a "daily level 1 buy"; a 1 buy caused by a 5-minute trend divergence is a "5-minute level 1 buy". They are both called "1 buy" in structure, but the levels are different and the weight is different**.

## 2. Differences in strength of buying and selling points at different levels

The higher the level of buying and selling points, the stronger the ** intensity, the larger the corresponding market range, and the more reliable it is**:

- **Large-level buying and selling point** (such as daily line 1 buy): Behind it is the exhaustion of the strength of the large-level trend. Once realized, it often corresponds to a market with a larger amplitude and longer duration; its "gold content" is high, but the frequency of occurrence is low and the confirmation is slower**.
- **Small-level buying and selling point** (such as 1 buy in 5 minutes): Behind it is a small-level trend, and the corresponding market is **small in amplitude and short-term**; it **occurs frequently**, but **is noisy and has relatively low reliability**, and it is easily a small fluctuation in a large-level trend.

To use an analogy: a large-level trading point is like a "traffic light on a main road", and a small-level trading point is like a "traffic light in a small alley" - both are called traffic lights, but one is in charge of the traffic flow on the main road, and the other is only in charge of a few bicycles. **Don't use the lights in the alleys to direct the cars on the main roads. **

## 3. What is "operation level"

To use the buying and selling points correctly, you must first clarify a concept: **operation level - at which level you plan to operate and hold this Stroke transaction. **

- If you want to make a big market move and hold it for a long time, your operation level is the highest (for example, the daily level is the operation level);
- If you want to do **short-term, fast in and fast out**, your operation level should be **small** (such as 5-minute level).

**There is no distinction between high and low operating level, but it must be "matched" with the level of buying and selling points you use. ** This is the most practical sentence in this article.

## 4. Level mismatch: common source of losses

Mismatching the buying and selling point level and the operating level is a frequent cause of losses for novices. Two typical mismatches:

**Mismatch 1: Use small-level buying and selling points to build large-level positions/expectations. **
When you see a 5-minute buy point of 1, you buy heavily and expect it to rise a lot - but the 5-minute buy point corresponds to only small fluctuations, and there is a high probability that it will go back after a small rise. **Using the green light in the alley, he drove onto the main road** and the result was predictable.

**Mismatch 2: Use the "patience" of large-level buying and selling points to do small-level fast in and fast out. **
On the other hand, if you hold a small-level position but use the logic of buying points on the daily line, you will easily be tortured repeatedly by the normal fluctuations of the small-level.

**Correct Correspondence**:** If you want to make a large-level transaction, use the buying and selling points of the corresponding level (or greater level) to make decisions. ** If the operation level is the daily line, use the daily buying and selling points as the main basis; if the operating level is 5 minutes, use the 5-minute buying and selling points, and accept its corresponding small and strict Stop Loss.

## 5. Relationship with "Union of Large and Small Levels" (Preview C1-9)

The "level correspondence" discussed in this article is the basis for the next group of "multi-level association" (C1-9). To put it simply: the mature approach is often - **use large-level buying and selling points to determine the direction and frame the range, and then use small-level buying and selling points to find more precise entry points**. But the premise is that you must first understand that "buying and selling points have levels, different levels have different weights, and they must be consistent with the operation level" - this is the foundation of this article.
