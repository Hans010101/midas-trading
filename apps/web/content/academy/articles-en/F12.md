# Trend Following Strategy: Let Profits Run

> Midas Academy · Level 5 · Module 16 Trading Strategy Types

**Quick overview of this article**

- Trend following is a **specific strategy**: **only follow the trend and do not predict tops and bottoms; enter the market on breakthroughs or pullbacks; follow with moving Stop Loss; let profits run**.
- Its profit and loss characteristics are very counter-intuitive: the winning rate is often not high, profits are concentrated in a few major trends, and a large number of small Stop Loss have to be endured during the period - the difficulty lies in execution and mentality, not analysis.
- **The Turtle Trading Rule** is a classic case of regularized trend tracking (breakthrough entry, volatility management, pyramid addition, mechanical Stop Loss); however, the **market has changed, and the rules of the year are not guaranteed to be effective now. They are only for understanding the ideas and cannot be copied**.
- Suitable for market trends with big trends, not suitable for shocks; risks include suffering from low winning rate, taking back top profits, and leverage amplification (⚠️ Amplifying profits also amplifies losses, possibly Liquidation).
- ⚠️ Red line: Trend following** does not guarantee profitability, and needs to be coordinated with risk management. What was effective in the past does not mean it will be effective in the future**; entry and exit are logical and non-exact trading signals.

## 1. Introduction of concepts: I know that I have to "follow the trend", but I can't grasp the trend.

In F8 "Trend Trading System", we talked about the overall framework of following the trend: identifying the trend, following the direction, entering the market on a pullback, and exiting if it falls below. That's "broad category methodology."

But many people understand the framework, but they still can’t do the actual market trend:

- I obviously took advantage of the trend and entered a Stroke. I quickly sold it after the price rose a little. As a result, I missed all the subsequent trends;
- Or you can’t hold on to your life or death, and you will be scared out by a normal correction in the trend;
- Or simply don't know how to turn "follow the trend" into **executable rules**.

In this article, we move from the framework** to the implementation level of the specific strategy of "trend following"**: how it sets the rules, which classic historical cases there are, and - most importantly - what its real profits and losses look like, and why it is so difficult to stick to it.

(Note: F8 talks about the overall framework and logic of "following the trend". This article talks about the regular implementation and profit and loss characteristics of trend following as a **specific strategy**. The two are inherited but not repeated.)

## 2. Core rules of trend following

Trend following, as a strategy, can be distilled into a few clear rules:

**1. Only follow the trend and never predict the top or bottom. **
The belief in trend following is "following", not "predicting". It **never tries to guess the highest point or lowest point**, but waits for the trend to be formed and the direction is clear, and then follows the direction. It admits "I don't know how far the trend will go", so it chooses "If the trend is there, I will be there; if the trend is gone, I will leave."

**2. Enter the market on breakthrough or pullback. **
There are usually two opportunities to enter the market following the trend:

- **Breakthrough entry**: Enter the market when the price **breaks through a certain key level** (such as the previous high point, the upper edge of the range), thinking that the breakthrough represents the continuation or start of the trend (this is the theme of the next F13 article).
- **Calibration entry**: In an upward trend, **wait for the price to correct** to a relatively low level (such as a moving average, a certain support) and then buy with the trend (mentioned by F8), pursuing better cost and profit-loss ratio.

**3. Mobile Stop Loss follows. **
This is the key technique for trend tracking to "hold the trend". After entering the market, **move the Stop Loss in a favorable direction as the trend advances** (the Stop Loss gradually moves upward when it rises), which not only locks in part of the existing floating profit, but also gives the trend room to continue running - it is not until the trend truly reverses and the price falls back to touch the moving Stop Loss that you leave the market.

**4. Let the profits run. **
Trend tracking does not preset a hard Take Profit target of "you must sell if it rises to this point", but relies on moving Stop Loss to passively leave the market - the purpose is not to sell out easily and to allow profitable orders to follow the trend as far as possible**. This is exactly the second half of the classic mantra: cut off losses and let profits run. **

## 3. Profit and loss characteristics of trend following (the key and most difficult part)

This section is the most important because it reveals the **counterintuitive truth** of trend following - many people give up on trend following because they haven't thought about this clearly.

**1. The winning rate is often not high (maybe only 30% to 40%). **
It sounds surprising: a classic strategy, but the winning rate may be less than half? The reason is – **Clear megatrends don’t appear often**. Trend following will try to enter the market again and again according to the rules, and the largest number of attempts will leave the market with small losses amid shocks and false breakthroughs**. Therefore, its winning rate is naturally low, and "misreading" is common.

**2. Profits come from a few major trends. **
So how does it make money? It relies on the profit-loss ratio** brought about by "letting profits run": Although the number of wins is small, once a big trend is caught, the profit of a single Stroke can be very large** - large enough to cover many previous small losses and make a net profit. In other words, all the profits from trend tracking are often concentrated in a few strokes with "big wins", and the rest are mostly small losses and small profits.

**3. During this period, you have to endure a lot of small Stop Loss or even continuous losses. **
This is the most difficult part of trend tracking** - before you catch the big trend, you may have to Stop Loss many times in a row, and your account will continue to retreat slightly, which will cause you psychological torture. After "losing money N times in a row", many people **abandoned the rules** on the eve of the big trend, thus missing out on the market wave that could recover their capital and make a profit.

Therefore, the real test of trend following is three things: **strict implementation** (enter the market according to the rules every time, stop loss, and not be shaken by consecutive losses), **psychological endurance** (can withstand consecutive small losses), **not miss the big trend** (always be present and participate in a regular manner). Its "difficulty" lies not in analysis, but in execution and mentality.

## 4. Classic Case: Turtle Trading Rules (idea introduction, with historical warnings)

When talking about trend following, we cannot avoid a famous historical case - **Turtle Trading**. It is a model of "regularized trend following".

**background. ** In the 1980s, American trader Richard Dennis and his partner William Eckhardt made a bet: Is trading ability innate, or can it be taught? In order to verify, Dennis recruited a group of ordinary people with no experience and gave them a set of **mechanized trend following rules** for training. This group of students was called "Turtles". As a result, many Turtles achieved very good results - this became a famous example that "trading can be systematized and taught".

**Core Idea** (understood as an example of regularized trend tracking):

- **Breakthrough Entry**: Go Long if the price breaks through the highest price in the previous period (such as the 20th or 55th), and Go Short if it falls below the lowest price (the so-called "Donchian Channel Breakthrough"). Everything is based on **regular signals** and does not involve subjective predictions.
- **Use volatility to manage risks (ATR / "N")**: Use the market's **true fluctuation range (ATR, called N by turtles)** to determine how much to buy for each variety and how wide the Stop Loss should be - buy less for volatile varieties and relax the Stop Loss, in order to **standardize the risks of different markets**.
- **Pyramid Adding Positions**: When the trend continues to advance in a favorable direction, **Add positions in batches** (increase positions in the profitable direction), but there is an upper limit for adding positions, and there will be no limit to adding positions.
- **Regulated Stop Loss**: Use a fixed volatility multiple (such as 2 times N) as Stop Loss, **mechanical execution, no hesitation**.
- **Reverse Breakthrough Exit**: When the price reversely breaks through the extreme value of the previous period (such as the previous 10 days/20 days), exit the market.

> ★ A historical warning that must be made clear: The Turtle Rule is a **famous and very successful method in history**, but please pay attention to a few points——
> 1. **The market environment has undergone tremendous changes. ** The structure of market participants, liquidity, information dissemination speed, and the popularity of algorithmic trading back then were completely different from today.
> 2. **The specific parameters and rules of that year are not guaranteed to be still valid now. ** A set of breakthrough parameters that worked in the 1980s may not work today (many widely publicized mechanical rules will lose effectiveness as more and more people use them).
> 3. **Introducing it is to let you understand the idea of ​​"regularized trend following"** (breakthrough entry, volatility management, pyramid increase, mechanical Stop Loss), **not to let you copy this set of rules for real trading. ** Any method needs to be combined with the current market, your own situation, and tested (F18).

## 5. Application and risks of trend following

**Suitable market**: A market or stage with a **clear and sustained general trend**. The bigger and smoother the trend, the more powerful trend tracking can be in "let profits run".

**Unsuitable market**: **Shocking market**. The back and forth between the ranges without direction will cause the trend-following entry signals to **frequently fail** and fall into repeated Stop Loss of "being beaten back as soon as you enter the market" (this is also the weakness of the trend strategy mentioned by F8).

**Main Risks**:

- **Psychological suffering of low winning rate**: Continuous small losses are very common, and people who can't bear it will give up midway.
- **Taking part of the floating profit when the trend reverses**: Moving Stop Loss naturally means that you **cannot sell at the highest point**. When the trend reverses, you must always give up the profit from the top to the Stop Loss level. This is the "necessary price" of trend following and must be accepted.
- **Leverage amplifies risk**: If leverage is used for trend tracking on a contract, continuous Stop Loss will consume margin **faster**; ⚠️ Leverage amplifies profits while **equally amplifying losses and possibly Liquidation**. You must not take heavy positions with high leverage just because "the trend looks smooth".
