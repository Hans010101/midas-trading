# MACD Divergence: Signal of Trend Exhaustion

> Midas Academy · Phase 2 · Module 6 Classic Technical Indicators

**Quick Overview**

- **Divergence = Price and indicator move in opposite directions**, often signaling a depletion of trend momentum.
- **Bullish Divergence**: Price makes a new high, but MACD makes a lower high → Upward momentum is exhausted.
- **Bearish Divergence**: Price makes a new low, but MACD makes a higher low → Downward momentum is exhausted. (The directional relationship must never be reversed.)
- **Divergence is not a precise timing tool**: It can become stale, persist, or fail; it signals a "potential reversal," not a specific timing or magnitude. It must be combined with context and risk management.

## I. Concept Introduction: When "Price" and "Momentum" Start Speaking Differently

Under normal circumstances, when price makes a new high, the indicator reflecting momentum also makes a new high—the two move in the same direction, and the trend is "healthy." However, sometimes a fascinating situation occurs: **Price continues to make new highs, but the indicator quietly moves lower.** This phenomenon of "price and indicator moving in opposite directions" is called **Divergence**.

Divergence is important because it is often a signal that **trend momentum is beginning to exhaust**. This section covers the two types of MACD divergence: Bullish Divergence and Bearish Divergence. (Divergence is generally observed on the MACD DIF line, but can also be seen via the high and low points of the red and green bars.)

## II. Bullish Divergence: Price Makes New Highs, MACD Highs Actually Lower

**Bullish Divergence** appears during an uptrend with the following characteristics:

- **Price**: The subsequent high is **higher** than the previous high (Price continues to **make new highs**, highs are rising).
- **MACD**: The corresponding subsequent high, however, is **lower** than the previous high (Indicator highs **decrease**).

Price is going up while the indicator is going down; they are **moving in opposite directions**. Its meaning is: **Although price has made a new high again, the underlying upward momentum is actually weakening**—the force pushing the price up cannot keep pace. Therefore, Bullish Divergence is often viewed as a warning signal of **exhausted upward momentum and a potential peak or weakening of the uptrend**.

## III. Bearish Divergence: Price Makes New Lows, MACD Lows Actually Rise

**Bearish Divergence** appears during a downtrend and is the mirror image of Bullish Divergence:

- **Price**: The subsequent low is **lower** than the previous low (Price continues to **make new lows**, lows are falling).
- **MACD**: The corresponding subsequent low, however, is **higher** than the previous low (Indicator lows **increase**).

Price is going down while the indicator is going up; they are **moving in opposite directions**. Its meaning is: **Although price has made a new low again, the downward momentum is actually weakening**. Therefore, Bearish Divergence is often viewed as a signal of **exhausted downward momentum and a potential bottom or strengthening of the downtrend**.

Memorize the comparison carefully; **the directional relationship must never be reversed**:

- **Bullish Divergence**: Price highs **rise** ↔ MACD highs **fall** (Momentum cannot keep up with new highs).
- **Bearish Divergence**: Price lows **fall** ↔ MACD lows **rise** (Momentum cannot keep up with new lows).

## IV. Meaning of Divergence: Momentum No Longer Supports Price

Why is Divergence worth watching? Because it reveals a contradiction: **The "surface" of price continues to persist (new high / new low), but the "momentum" driving it has begun to reverse.** When momentum no longer supports price continuing its path, the trend has the possibility of **exhaustion or even reversal**.

Think of it this way: A car is still sliding forward (price making new highs), but the gas pedal has been released and the engine's strength is weakening (momentum divergence). The car is still moving, but you will start to doubt how far it can go.

## V. Key Reminder: Divergence is Not a Precise Timing Tool

This point must be emphasized strongly; otherwise, it is easily misleading—**Divergence can signal "momentum is exhausting," but it is absolutely not a "precise buy or sell signal."** There are several reasons:

1. **Possible Stalemate / Persistent Divergence.** Price may **continue to make new highs (or lows) for a long time** after divergence appears, with divergence recurring but the reversal delayed. This is especially common in strong trends.
2. **Possible Direct Failure.** Divergence appears, but the trend does not reverse; instead, it continues in the original direction—this situation of divergence failing completely exists.
3. **It Says "Possibly," Not "Definitely."** Divergence raises the alert that "the trend may turn," but it cannot be used to infer exactly when or to what magnitude the reversal will occur.

Therefore, the mature usage is to treat Divergence as a **reference clue worth raising vigilance for**, combined with trend, position, and other information for judgment, and always accompanied by risk management—**rather than concluding a reversal upon seeing divergence, or using it to predict price.** This section does not predict any price action and does not constitute any trading advice.

## VI. Key Takeaways

- **Divergence = Price and indicator move in opposite directions**, often signaling trend momentum exhaustion.
- **Bullish Divergence**: Price highs rise (new highs), but MACD highs fall → Upward momentum exhausted.
- **Bearish Divergence**: Price lows fall (new lows), but MACD lows rise → Downward momentum exhausted. (The directional relationship must never be reversed.)
- **Divergence is not a precise timing tool**: It can become stale, persist, or fail; it signals a "potential reversal," not a specific timing or magnitude. It must be combined with context and risk management.
