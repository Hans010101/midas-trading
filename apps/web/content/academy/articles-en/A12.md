## I. Concept Introduction: Practice in the "Simulator" First, Then Hit the Road

People learning to drive almost always practice on driving school grounds or simulators; no one gets on the highway on day one. Trading is the same—**Virtual trading is the "simulator" in the trading world.**

Virtual trading (also known as simulated trading or paper trading) means: **placing orders, holding positions, and closing trades using simulated, non-real funds to experience the complete trading process, but without any real financial profit or loss involved throughout.** Gains are just increases in virtual numbers, and losses are just decreases in virtual numbers; your real money remains untouched.

## II. Why Practice with Virtual Trading First

For beginners, jumping straight into the market with real money is often the most expensive way to learn. The value of virtual trading lies in bringing "learning costs" down to near zero:

**1. Zero Real Risk for Trial and Error.**
There are many things in trading that understanding them does not equal being able to do them. How to place an order, how to set a stop loss, how you react when you see price fluctuations... these all require practice. Virtual trading allows you to **experience all "firsts" and "inevitable mistakes" in an environment without real losses**—this is its greatest value.

**2. Hone Judgment, Master the Method First.**
Real money amplifies emotions: panic during unrealized losses, greed during unrealized profits, often leading to decisions one wouldn't normally make. The virtual environment lacks the pressure of real money, allowing you to **focus first on whether the judgment itself is correct**—solidifying the method, process, and discipline first.

**3. Extremely Low Cost to Verify Ideas.**
You have a trading idea and want to know "what happens if I do this method." Virtual trading allows you to test, observe, and adjust at low cost without paying tuition with your principal.

## III. Correct Usage of Virtual Trading: Learn → Practice → Review

How well you use virtual trading makes a big difference. The most common mistake is "since it's fake money, just click around"—you learn almost nothing this way. The correct usage is to treat it as a **serious learning loop**:

**Step 1: Learn.** First, understand a specific knowledge point or method (such as trends, stop loss, and indicators in this bootcamp).

**Step 2: Practice.** Execute seriously in the virtual environment with this method: set stop losses when required, follow the plan when required. **Key Mindset—Treat it like a real account.** How you treat this virtual capital largely predicts how you will treat real capital in the future.

**Step 3: Review.** This step is most easily overlooked but is the most important. Look back periodically: what did you do right, what did you do wrong, was it a judgment error or an execution error (e.g., lack of discipline)? **Bring the conclusions of the review back into the next round of practice.**

Learn → Practice → Review, forming a cycle, is when virtual trading truly takes effect.
