# Range Trading System: Buy Low, Sell High

> Midas Academy · Level 5 · Module 13 Trading System and Practical Combat

**Quick overview of this article**

- **Swing trading = Sell high and buy low within the range**: buy near support, sell near resistance, and make profits back and forth within the range; the premise is that the market is indeed oscillating.
- To identify shocks, rely on **Support and Resistance + Bollinger Bands + Central Hub**; entry and exit are **Buy near support, sell/leave near resistance, and exit with Stop Loss** if there is a breakthrough.
- ⚠️ Two major risks: **False breakthrough** (losses are swept by short-term breakthroughs), **Trend start trap** (reverse direction at the boundary but encounters a real breakthrough); the direction of the breakthrough is **unpredictable**.
- Swing trading **extremely relies on strict Stop Loss** and **does not guarantee profit or predict**; it and trend trading are applicable to different market conditions and require a combination of judgment and risk management.

## 1. Concept introduction: What to do when there is no trend?

The trend trading in the previous article will be beaten repeatedly in volatile market conditions. But the reality is - the market fluctuates most of the time without a clear direction. Is there any corresponding idea in this kind of market?

Yes, it is **swing trading**: **sell high and buy low** within a range. This article integrates support, resistance, Bollinger Bands, Central Hub and other knowledge into a shock trading framework. But let’s put the red line first: The risk of shock trading is not small, especially "false breakthroughs" and "trend starts", which also do not guarantee profits. **

## 2. Core Concept: Sell high and buy low within the range

The core of shock trading: **When the price fluctuates back and forth within a range, buy close to the lower edge of the range (support) and sell close to the upper edge of the range (resistance) to earn money from the fluctuations within the range. **

Its logic is exactly the opposite of trend trading: trend trading "follows the breakthrough and follows the direction", while swing trading "reverses at both ends of the range and bets that the price will return to the range." **The premise is that the market is indeed in a shock, not a trend. **

## 3. How to identify the shock range

To determine "whether it is a shock now and where the range is", you can use a combination of:

- **Support and Resistance** (A6): The price repeats between a clear support and a clear resistance, and does not break the resistance when it rises and the support when it falls → range oscillation.
- **Bollinger Bands** (Group B): During the shock, the price often goes back and forth between the upper and lower rails of Bollinger Bands; but be aware that "the market may change after closing, and the direction is unpredictable."
- **Chan TheoryCentral Hub** (Group C): The Central Hub itself is an oscillation area of ​​long and short balance, corresponding to the oscillation structure.

## 4. Entry and exit

The entry and exit logic of shock trading:

- **Entry: buy near support and sell near resistance. ** When the price falls near the lower edge of the range (support), consider Go Long (buy low), and when the price rises near the upper edge of the range (resistance), consider Go Short or leave the market (sell high).
- **Take Profit: Go to the other end of the interval. ** Buy low and then cash out when it rises to resistance; sell high and then cash out when it falls near support - what you earn is going back and forth within the range.
- **Stop Loss (key): Leave the market when it breaks through the range. ** Once the price ** effectively breaks through the range ** (breaks the resistance upward or breaks the support downward), it means that the shock may end and the trend may start. ** Stop Loss** must be decisively exited and must not continue to "buy low and sell high" outside the range.

## 5. Risks of shock trading (must be emphasized)

There are two **fatal risks** in swing trading, which must be explained clearly:

**1. False breakthrough. **
The price briefly breaks through the range, sweeps away your Stop Loss, and then shrinks back into the range - you are eliminated by the Stop Loss when you least should (commonly known as "washed out by a false breakthrough"). The boundary of a swing range is never an exact line, and false breakouts are common.

**2. Get trapped when the trend starts. **
This is the most dangerous. If you go short on the "upper edge of the range", but the price does not fall back, but actually breaks upward and the trend starts, you are standing in the opposite direction of the trend - if you do not strictly stop loss at this time, you will be trapped deeper and deeper by the trend market. "Go Short at the Resistance Level" and encounter a "Trend Breakout" is a typical way to lose a lot of money in volatile trading.

The root of these two risks is the same thing: **You can never determine in advance whether after the price hits the boundary, it will "return to the range as usual" or "this time it will really break through." The direction of the breakthrough is even more unpredictable. **So swing trading** relies heavily on strict Stop Loss discipline** - once a breakthrough occurs, admit your mistake and leave the market immediately.
