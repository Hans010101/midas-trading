# Golden Cross and Death Cross of Moving Averages

> **Midas Academy · Phase 2 · Module 6 Classic Technical Indicators**

**Quick Overview**

- **Golden Cross = Short-term MA crosses above Long-term MA**, often viewed as a bullish signal.
- **Death Cross = Short-term MA crosses below Long-term MA**, often viewed as a bearish signal. (Never get the direction reversed.)
- Golden Cross and Death Cross are **lagging signals**: by the time they appear, the price has often already moved; false signals are especially common in ranging markets.
- **Do not trade solely based on Golden Cross or Death Cross**. Combine them with trends, other information, and risk management; they are references, not predictions, and certainly not buy/sell orders.

## I. Concept Introduction: What Does the "Crossing" of Two MAs Indicate?

In the previous article, we learned that MAs of different periods have different personalities: short-term MAs are sensitive, while long-term MAs are stable. When we place a short-term MA and a long-term MA together, they will sometimes get close and sometimes cross. **This crossing forms the two most widely known signals in trading—Golden Cross and Death Cross.**

They are watched because a crossing often implies "a change in the comparison between short-term and long-term strength." But please remember one sentence first, which will be emphasized repeatedly: **They are reference signals, not buy/sell orders, and certainly not predictions.**

## II. Golden Cross: Short-term MA Crosses Above Long-term MA

The definition of **Golden Cross** is very clear: **Short-term MA crosses from below to above Long-term MA.**

Breaking down this process:

- **Before the cross**: Short-term MA is **below** the Long-term MA.
- **At the time of the cross**: Short-term MA **crosses from below to above** the Long-term MA.
- **After the cross**: Short-term MA runs **above** the Long-term MA.

Its meaning is: Short-term average cost begins to surpass long-term average cost, indicating that buying pressure in the near term is strengthening. Therefore, **Golden Cross is often viewed as a bullish (upward-leaning) signal.**

## III. Death Cross: Short-term MA Crosses Below Long-term MA

**Death Cross** is exactly the opposite: **Short-term MA crosses from above to below Long-term MA.**

- **Before the cross**: Short-term MA is **above** the Long-term MA.
- **At the time of the cross**: Short-term MA **crosses from above to below** the Long-term MA.
- **After the cross**: Short-term MA runs **below** the Long-term MA.

Its meaning is: Short-term average cost breaks below long-term average cost, indicating that selling pressure in the near term is strengthening. Therefore, **Death Cross is often viewed as a bearish (downward-leaning) signal.**

![B2 Figure 2](/academy-img/B2-配图2.png)

Remember this symmetric relationship, and **never get it reversed**:

- **Golden Cross = Short-term MA "crosses above" Long-term MA** (Short line goes from bottom to top) → Bullish.
- **Death Cross = Short-term MA "crosses below" Long-term MA** (Short line goes from top to bottom) → Bearish.

## IV. Key Insight 1: Golden Cross and Death Cross are "Lagging" Signals

This is the most important cognitive framework for beginners. Remember what was said in the previous article? **MAs are calculated from past prices and are naturally lagging behind the price itself.** Therefore, Golden Cross and Death Cross generated by MA crossovers are naturally **lagging signals**.

This means: **When the Golden Cross actually appears, the price has often already risen for a stretch; when the Death Cross appears, the price has often already fallen for a bit.** The signal is a confirmation of "changes that have already occurred," not a prophecy of the "future."

Lagging nature brings a direct consequence: in **ranging markets**, prices repeatedly move up and down, MAs cross frequently, generating a large number of **false signals** such as "rebound after a Golden Cross" or "pullback after a Death Cross." This is why you cannot mechanically "Long on a Golden Cross and Short on a Death Cross."

## V. Key Insight 2: Do Not Trade Based Solely on Golden Cross or Death Cross

Based on this lagging nature, it must be emphasized (this concerns both the red line and your capital safety):

**Golden Cross and Death Cross are auxiliary references, never a standalone basis for trading.** The mature approach is to view them within a larger context:

- **Combine with Trend**: A Golden Cross appearing in a clear uptrend usually carries more significance than one appearing in chaotic ranging markets.
- **Combine with Other Information**: Price position (support/resistance), other indicators, volume, etc., can all be used for cross-confirmation.
- **Always Combine with Risk Management**: No matter how "beautiful" the signal looks, always prepare a stop-loss for the possibility that "it is a false signal / I was wrong."

This article does not predict any price movement, nor does it tell you "what to buy or sell." We simply clarify: what Golden Cross and Death Cross are, how they form, and what their limitations are. **How to use them, or whether to use them, requires you to combine your own judgment and discipline.**
