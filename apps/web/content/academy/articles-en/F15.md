> Midas Academy · Level 5 · Module 16 Trading Strategy Types

- ⚠️ **The Achilles heel is the unilateral market**: During the unilateral decline, **continue to buy and get deeper and deeper** (running out of funds); during the unilateral rise, **sell out prematurely and miss the big market**; the profit and loss are extremely asymmetrical (small profits and big losses).
- ⚠️ **Add leverage to run the grid, and unilateral market conditions may be liquidated directly**; "amplifying gains" will inevitably "amplify losses and may be liquidated" at the same time.

- ⚠️ Red line: Grid is not a sure profit/lay profit tool**; it is relatively controllable only when it continues to fluctuate + strictly controls the total position + no/extremely low leverage + sets a breakthrough Stop Loss; **No guarantee of profit, risk management is required**, what worked in the past does not mean it will work in the future.

## 1. Concept introduction: a grid that has been touted as a "magic tool for making money"

Among various trading promotions, "grid trading" is probably the most attractive one - "set it and forget about it, automatically buy low and sell high", "make money while lying down", "shock arbitrage artifact"... it sounds like a money printing machine.

Is it really that good?

In this article, on the one hand, we will clearly explain the principles of the grid, and on the other hand - and most importantly - we will explain its fatal risks to the end. Let me say the most important thing in advance:

> ⚠️ **Grid trading is by no means a tool for "guaranteed profit" or "easy profit". ** It can make a small amount of money during continuous fluctuations, but once it encounters a unilateral market, it may cause you huge losses or even Liquidation (under leverage). This article will explain these risks thoroughly. Please be sure to read the risk section before understanding this strategy.

## 2. Principles of grid trading

**Grid trading is to place buy and sell orders in layers according to fixed price intervals (like a "grid") within a price range, and mechanically sell high and buy low. **

How it works:

- You first define a price range (for example, you think the price will fluctuate between a certain upper and lower limit).
- Within the range, set a series of price points at fixed intervals to form a "grid".
- **Every time it falls by one bar, a buy order is placed (buy low); every time it rises by one bar, a sell order is placed (sell high). **
- When the price fluctuates back and forth within the range, these buy and sell orders are constantly triggered - automatically buying at low levels and selling at high levels, repeatedly earning small price differences. **

In the **ideal continuous oscillation** market, the price shuttles up and down, and the grid can indeed continue to close transactions and accumulate small profits bit by bit - it looks like an "automatic selling high and buying low machine". **This is the alluring part of the grid and the source of the illusion of "making money while lying down". **

## 3. Why "seemingly making money automatically" is an illusion

Grid's "automatic money making" is based on a **dangerous assumption**: **prices will always fluctuate within a range. **

But the market will not fluctuate forever. Sooner or later, the shock will end, and sooner or later the market will move out of the **unilateral market** (continue to move in one direction) - and the unilateral market is the "Achilles heel" of the grid. If you think about this clearly, the illusion of "making money while lying down" will be exposed.

## 4. ★Fatal Weakness: Unilateral Market

This section is the core of the entire article. The most fatal risk of the grid comes from unilateral market conditions, and both rising and falling unilateral trends will hurt it:

**1. Unilateral decline: keep buying, going deeper and deeper until funds are exhausted. **

This is a typical way for grids to lose a lot of money. When the price does not fluctuate, but **unilaterally declines all the way**:

- The grid will continue to trigger buy orders one by one on the way down - the lower the price, the more it buys.
- The result is: you have accumulated more and more positions bought at high prices, and the floating losses continue to expand, and the positions get deeper and deeper**.
- If the price falls below the lower boundary of the grid, either the funds are bought out and there are no orders to hang, or you hold a lot of deeply locked positions.

Figuratively speaking, when the grid is in a unilateral decline, it is "continuously catching falling flying knives, and more and more" -- this is its most dangerous scenario.

**2. Unilateral rise: selling out prematurely and shorting the market. **

Conversely, when the price ** rises unilaterally **, the grid will not be happy either:

- The grid will gradually sell out the positions ** one by one in the ** early stage ** of the rise**.
- After that, the price continued to rise sharply, but you were **out of stock** - you only made a small profit at the beginning, but you **missed out on the entire subsequent big market**.

**3. Summarize the profit and loss characteristics of the grid in one sentence: make "limited small money" in the shock, and lose "possibly a lot of money" in the unilateral situation (or miss the big market) - the profit and loss are extremely asymmetric. **

This asymmetry of "small profits and big losses" is exactly the truth that needs to be seen clearly in the grid. It's not that it can't be used, but it's definitely not as "stable" as advertised.

## 5. ★Add leverage to run the grid: possible direct Liquidation

If the unlevered grid is "getting deeper and deeper" in a unilateral decline, then if you use leverage to run the grid in a crypto contract, you may liquidate directly and the principal will return to zero**.

The reason is very straightforward:

- During the unilateral decline, Grid continued to buy and floating losses continued to expand;
- And leverage will **equally amplify these floating losses** - originally it was just a deep position, but after adding leverage, the floating losses may quickly eat up the margin, **triggering Forced Liquidation (Liquidation)**.

> ⚠️ This is **extremely high risk** play. Never just because "the grid looks very stable and can automatically make money", you can safely add leverage and run - leverage + grid + unilateral market is a combination that may make you out of the game instantly. The other side of "magnifying profits" is always "equally magnifying losses, possibly Liquidation".

## 6. ★About "Martinale": an extreme variant more dangerous than the grid (strong warning)

Speaking of the grid, we must incidentally warn about an extreme variant that is more dangerous than the grid - Martingale.

Its idea is: **After each loss, double the position to cover** - trying to use larger and larger positions to spread the low cost. As long as the price rebounds a little, the cost can be recovered or even made a profit. It sounds like "you can always get it back."

But it has a fatal, mathematical knot:

- It **theoretically requires "unlimited funds"** to withstand continuous adverse market conditions;
- In reality, funds are limited** - as long as you encounter a unilateral market trend that is long enough, doubling and doubling your position will quickly expand to the point where you cannot bear it, leading to a devastating retracement and the elimination of Liquidation.
- In the long run, using Martingale, ** will almost certainly encounter disaster in a certain continuous loss**.

> ⚠️ **Martingale is strongly not recommended for anyone to use. It is by no means a usable "strategy", but a bomb that will explode sooner or later. ** This article only serves as a warning and does not elaborate on its specific methods - because it is not worth learning as a trading method. If you see someone advocating "double your losses and ensure your profits are guaranteed," please be extremely vigilant.

## 7. Conditions for "relatively controllable" grids (if they must be understood)

After talking about so many risks, is the grid completely untouchable? Not absolutely. But it is **relatively controllable** and needs to meet several demanding conditions at the same time:

- **It is indeed in a market that continues to fluctuate** (and this is precisely the most difficult thing to confirm in advance - you never know when the fluctuation will become one-sided);
- **Strictly control the total position**, leaving enough capital buffer for unilateral market conditions, and not using up all the ammunition at once;
- **No leverage, or only very low leverage** - Never overlay high leverage on the grid;
- **Set Stop Loss** outside the range boundary - Once the price effectively breaks through the range (unilateral market may start), **admit defeat and leave**, instead of letting the grid sink deeper and deeper in the unilateral direction.

Even if these conditions are met, please remember: ** Grid is still not a guaranteed tool for making money, and the risk of unilateral market conditions always exists. ** These conditions only make it "relatively controllable", not "safe".
