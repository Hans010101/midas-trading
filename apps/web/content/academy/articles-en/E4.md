# How Liquidation Price is Calculated: Forced Liquidation Mechanism

> Midas Academy · Level 4 · Module 12 Contracts and Derivatives

## 1. Concept introduction: Each leverage position has a "red line"

In A5 we met "Liquidation". This article goes one step further: At what price does **Liquidation occur? What determines this price? ** Understanding this "red line" is the basic skill of contract risk management - you must know your position, and it will be forced to close wherever the price goes.

> Let me start with this: **Liquidation is the most important and fatal risk in contract trading. ** This article explains its mechanism clearly. The purpose is to let you fully understand the risks and learn to take precautions, rather than to encourage you to take risks.

## 2. What is Liquidation price (strong liquidation price)?

**Liquidation price, also called forced liquidation price, refers to the price at which your position will be Forced Liquidation by the system when the price reaches this position in reverse. **

When the price moves in an unfavorable direction and your loss is so large that it approaches the critical point of your margin, the platform will forcefully close your position to prevent your loss from exceeding your margin. The price that triggers liquidation is the Liquidation price.

## 3. Key: The Liquidation price for long orders is at the bottom, and the Liquidation price for short orders is at the top.

The **direction** of the Liquidation price relative to the opening price must be recorded accurately:

- **Go Long**: You are afraid of the price falling, so the **Liquidation price is [below] the opening price** - the price **drops** to the Liquidation price and is liquidated.
- **Go Short**: You are afraid of the price rising, so the **Liquidation price is [above] the opening price** - if the price **rises** to the Liquidation price, it will be liquidated.

The reason is very straightforward: long orders are losing money, short orders are losing money, and liquidation prices are naturally in the direction of losses.

## 4. What affects Liquidation price: leverage, margin, direction

How far the Liquidation price is from the position price (that is, how much reverse fluctuation it can withstand) is mainly determined by these factors:

- **Leverage**: The higher the leverage, the closer the Liquidation price** is to the position price** - a little reverse fluctuation will trigger it. This is the most critical factor.
- **Margin**: Under the same position, the more the margin, the greater the reverse fluctuation that can be tolerated, and the farther the Liquidation price is.
- **Direction**: Determines whether the Liquidation price is above or below (long orders at the bottom, short orders at the top).

![E4 with picture 2](/academy-img/E4-配图2.png)

Recall the approximate relationship of A5: under N times leverage, a reverse fluctuation of approximately (100÷N)% may result in loss of margin. For example, with 10 times leverage, if the price reverses by about 10%, it is close to Liquidation; with 100 times leverage, if the price reverses by about 1%, it may be Liquidation. **The higher the leverage, the closer this red line is to you. **

## 5. Maintenance margin rate: why actual Liquidation is often earlier than the "theoretical price"

Here is a point that novices often overlook, but it is very important.

Liquidation does not wait until "the margin is lost to nothing (100% loss)" to occur. Instead, it has a **Maintenance Margin Ratio**: **When your account equity (margin + floating profit and loss) drops to the maintenance margin level, a liquidation will be triggered** - this level is before "loss".

Therefore, **actual Liquidation often arrives earlier than the theoretical price you calculated using "100% loss"**. The main reasons are:

1. **Existence of maintenance margin**: The trigger point for liquidation is before "loss of money", not after.
2. **Handling fees, Funding Rate**: These costs are also constantly consuming your margin.
3. **Slippage during strong periods**: Under extreme market conditions, the actual closing price may be worse.

In other words: **Don't think that "it's safe until you lose 100%." ** The true liquidation red line is usually closer than your naive calculation.

## 6. Emphasize risks again

> ⚠️ Liquidation means that the margin will suffer a large loss or even return to zero, which is the most serious consequence of contract trading. **The higher the leverage, the closer the Liquidation price is and the easier it is to be triggered by an ordinary fluctuation. ** Knowing your own Liquidation price and keeping a sufficient safe distance from it is the most basic self-protection when making a contract. Using high leverage without understanding the Liquidation mechanism is equivalent to walking a tightrope while blindfolded.

## 7. Main points of this article

- **Liquidation price (forced liquidation price)** = the price at which the position is Forced Liquidated when the price reaches the reverse direction.
- Direction: **The Liquidation price for long orders is [below] the opening price, and the liquidation price for short orders is [above]** (Never record reverse).
- Influencing factors: **Leverage** (the higher the Liquidation price, the closer it is, the most critical), **Margin** (the more, the further away), direction.
- **Maintain the margin rate** to allow liquidation to be triggered before "loss"; plus handling fees, funding fees, and slippage, **actual Liquidation is often earlier than the theoretical price**.
- ⚠️ Liquidation is the biggest risk in a contract, and it is especially dangerous under high leverage - be sure to fully understand it and keep a safe distance.
