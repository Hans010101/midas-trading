## 1. Concept introduction: turning "following the trend" into an executable framework

Earlier we learned about trends, Moving Average, Chan Theory, and also talked about the concept of "following the trend". This article integrates these scattered knowledge** into a complete trend trading framework** - from "how to judge the trend" to "how to enter, how to exit, and how to manage risks".

Let me state the red line first: **This is just to clarify the logic of trend trading, not a set of profit-making strategies. ** Any system has its suitable and unsuitable market conditions, and no profit is guaranteed.

## 2. Core Concept: Trade in the direction of the trend

The core of trend trading is just one sentence: **Trade in the direction of the confirmed trend, rather than guessing the top or bottom. **

- In an upward trend, **only consider Go Long** (or wait and see), and do not easily go against the trend and go Short.
- In a downward trend, **only consider Go Short/Avoid** and do not easily buy the bottom.

Its underlying logic is to "go with the trend" (see entry): following the direction of the dominant force, the odds of winning are usually relatively higher and the resistance is smaller. But it does not predict how far the trend will go, it just "follows" the trend that has already occurred.

## 3. How to identify trends

To judge trends, you can use several tools learned previously (mutual confirmation is more reliable than using one tool alone):

- **High and Low Point Structure** (A7): Uptrend = high points continue to rise and lows continue to rise; Downtrend = high points and low points continue to fall.
- **Moving Average** (Group B): The price is above the Moving Average, the Moving Average is upward, and the trend is Bullish Alignment → bullish; on the contrary, it is bearish.
- **Chan Theory Structure** (Group C): Use Segment and trend structure to determine the level and direction of the trend.

## 4. Entry and exit

There is a common set of logic for entry and exit of trend trading:

- **Entry: Enter the market during a pullback. ** In an upward trend, instead of chasing the highest point, wait for the price to fall back to a relatively low level (such as a moving average, a support, or a structural level) and then go long with the trend. This way, the risk is smaller and the profit-loss ratio is better.
- **Exit (Stop Loss): Leave the market when the trend is destroyed. ** When the price ** falls below the key support of the trend ** (such as falling below the key Moving Average, falling below the previous low, or the Chan Theory structure deteriorates), it means that the trend may end, and you should leave the market according to discipline.
- **Take Profit: Follow the trend and hold on to the trend. ** Trend trading often "cuts off losses and lets profits run" - follow the trend by using methods such as moving Stop Loss until the trend truly reverses.

(The specific entry and exit points are logical indications, not a signal to "buy here, sell there"; the actual entry and exit points must be combined with your own rules and risk management.)

## 5. Advantages and Disadvantages of Trend Trading

Any system has applicable boundaries, and trend trading is no exception:

- **Advantages**: Once you hit a major trend, "let profits run" can bring you a high profit-loss ratio; you have clear logic, follow the trend, and have a relatively calm mentality.
- **Disadvantages**: It is very disadvantageous in the **shock market** - the price has no direction, fluctuates back and forth, trend signals frequently fail, and it is easy to **stop loss** repeatedly (commonly known as "repeatedly slapped in the face"). Profits from trend trading often come from a few major trends, during which many small losses must be endured.
