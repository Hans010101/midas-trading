## 1. Concept introduction: What determines how far you go is your mentality.

The previous article (F36) said that in addition to knowledge, execution, discipline, risk control, and reverence are the long-term keys. In this article, we will talk about the foundation behind these-**mentality**.

Technology and system determine whether you can do it; mentality determines how far you can go and whether you can live to see compound interest. This article talks about three things: **How ​​to view losses correctly, how to make real progress, and how to remain in awe of the market** - and clearly oppose the "quick wealth" mentality.

## 2. View losses correctly (then F7)

One of the biggest mental obstacles for novices is their inability to face losses correctly - they collapse as soon as they lose, have self-doubt, or rush to make a comeback. To establish a healthy mentality, we must first re-understand losses:

**1. Loss is part of trading, not "failure". **
**No matter how good a trader is, he will lose money. ** We have said that the winning rate of trend following may be only 30% to 40% (F12) - that is to say, a large number of losses are **normal and built into trading**. It is a wrong understanding to equate "losing a stroke" with "I failed". Loss is not failure, **Loss is one of the costs of this business**.

**2. Loss is "tuition" and "feedback". **
The key is not "whether there was a loss", but "whether you learned anything from it after the loss" (continued from F7 review). A stroke of loss is a stroke of valuable tuition if you review it, find problems, and make improvements; if you just get upset and forget about it, then it is really a waste of money. **Treat every Stroke loss as feedback from the market. **

**3. Distinguish between "loss caused by the right process" and "loss caused by the wrong process" (continue from F7). **
This is extremely important (echoing the result of F7 being right):

- **Process Pair + Losing Money**: This is **acceptable** - you did the right thing according to the system, but this time the odds were not on your side. **Don’t deny yourself, overturn the system, or change the rules just because of this normal loss. **
- **Process Wrong + Losing Money**: This is what **must be corrected** - it means there is a problem with the method or execution.

**Don't confuse "normal losses when the process is right" with "losses when the process is wrong and should be corrected". ** The former requires persistence, while the latter requires correction.

## 3. Base progress on review and iteration (continue F7)

The second mentality point: ** Figure out where "progress" comes from. **

Many people think that progress depends on "finding stronger strategies/more accurate indicators/more awesome skills" - so they keep changing strategies and chasing new tools, always "looking for the Holy Grail" (F17), but never making progress.

The truth is: **Progress relies on continuous review and the process of improving oneself bit by bit** (continues from F7 and F34).

- Not by "changing to a stronger method", but by "polishing the existing method through review to make it more suitable for you and implement it better";
- **"Slow is fast"**: Iterating and accumulating steadily is far more reliable than chasing magic skills. Those who have one strategy today and one indicator tomorrow often stand still; but those who insist on review and continuous fine-tuning are slowly becoming stronger.

## 4. ★Always remain in awe of the market

The third and most important mentality is: awe. **

**The market is always full of uncertainty and always more complex than you think. ** No matter how perfect the system is, it may fail (there is no Holy Grail, F17), and no matter how confident the judgment is, it may be wrong.

What is respect for the market? It is embodied in three "forevers":

- **Always assume that you may be wrong**: Do not regard any judgment as "must be right", and always prepare a retreat for "I am wrong" (Stop Loss);
- **Always do risk management** (F4): Always control the risk of single stroke and never gamble on your life with heavy positions;
- **Never All in**: Never put all your wealth on a "sure-win" opportunity - because **there is no "sure-win"**.

The essence of awe is to admit your own insignificance and the uncontrollability of the market, and then use risk management to protect yourself. ** In one sentence: ** Survive (F4) first, then you can be qualified to talk about progress and profit. ** Living is the prerequisite for everything.

## 5. ★Oppose the "get rich quick" mentality

Finally, we must take a clear-cut stand against a toxic mentality - **quick riches**:

- **There is no "quick fix" in trading, nor is there any "guaranteed instant wealth". **Anyone who advocates "doubling in a few days", "guaranteed profit without losing money" or "getting rich by following me" is either a scam or a survivor bias** (most people who only let you see the profit and don't let you see the loss).
- Those **leverage/contract "get rich myths"** are behind the people who **lots of Liquidation went out** (review E8, F24). You see the story of one person who became rich through high leverage, but behind it are hundreds of people who are also highly leveraged but have already been eliminated - they will not speak out.
- A **healthy mentality** is: Treat trading as a skill that requires long-term practice**, and pursue **long-term, stable, and survival** instead of treating it as gambling or pursuing getting rich overnight**.

> ⚠️ Let me solemnly say this: This training camp **does not promise any income or exaggerate about getting rich**. We repeatedly emphasize risk management, survival, and respect for the market, precisely because-** trading is risky, and the vast majority of people who want to "get rich quickly" end up losing money quickly. ** Please approach trading with a healthy and long-term mentality, and do not take risks like a gambler.
