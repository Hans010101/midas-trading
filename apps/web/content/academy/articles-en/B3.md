# Multi-MA Systems: Bullish and Bearish Alignment

> Midas Academy · Phase 2 · Module 6 Classic Technical Indicators

**Quick Overview**

- **Multi-MA System**: Simultaneously using short / medium / long MAs to judge market strength based on their arrangement order.
- **Bullish Arrangement**: From top to bottom = Price > Short-term > Medium-term > Long-term (Short on top, Price on top), representing a strong uptrend structure.
- **Bearish Arrangement**: From top to bottom = Long-term > Medium-term > Short-term > Price (Short on bottom, Price on bottom), representing a weak downtrend structure.
- **Arrangement Conversion** (reversal after entanglement) hints that the trend may be changing, but does not equal a definite reversal. It is lagging and for reference only.

## I. Concept Introduction: Not Just One MA

Previously, we used one or two MAs. In practice, many traders **simultaneously display short-term, medium-term, and long-term MAs**—this is the "Multi-MA System."

Why do this? Because viewing multiple MAs together presents the "strength state across different time dimensions" simultaneously. Their **arrangement order** can intuitively reflect whether the current market is strong or weak. This section covers the two most typical arrangements: Bullish and Bearish.

## II. Bullish Arrangement: MAs "Short-Medium-Long" from Top to Bottom, Price on Top

**Bullish Arrangement** describes a typical **strong (uptrend) structure**. Its characteristic is that from the chart, arranged from top to bottom:

**Price (Top) → Short-term MA → Medium-term MA → Long-term MA (Bottom)**

In other words: the short-term MA is on top, the long-term MA is on the bottom. The MAs fan upwards, while the price runs **above all MAs**.

Why does this represent strength? Recall that "MA = Average Cost":

- Short-term > Medium-term > Long-term indicates that **recent buyers have higher average costs**—people are willing to keep buying at higher prices.
- Price above all MAs indicates that **holders across all time periods are generally in profit**.

This is exactly what a healthy uptrend looks like: buyers are dominant, and the center of gravity is rising steadily.

## III. Bearish Arrangement: MAs "Long-Medium-Short" from Top to Bottom, Price on Bottom

**Bearish Arrangement** is the mirror image of the Bullish Arrangement, describing a typical **weak (downtrend) structure**. From the chart, arranged from top to bottom:

**Long-term MA (Top) → Medium-term MA → Short-term MA → Price (Bottom)**

In other words: the long-term MA is on top, the short-term MA is on the bottom. The MAs fan downwards, while the price runs **below all MAs**.

The meaning can also be understood via average cost: Short-term < Medium-term < Long-term indicates that **recent buyers have lower average costs**—prices are falling; while price is below all MAs, indicating **holders are generally in loss**. This is a typical pattern of a weak downtrend.

![B3 Figure 2](/academy-img/B3-配图2.png)

Compare and memorize the two:

- **Bullish Arrangement**: Short on top, Long on bottom, Price on top → Strong uptrend structure.
- **Bearish Arrangement**: Short on bottom, Long on top, Price on bottom → Weak downtrend structure.

## IV. Arrangement Conversion: Trend May Be Changing

MA arrangements are not static. When the power of a trend begins to exhaust and may turn, the originally neat arrangement will first become **entangled and sticky** (MAs squeeze together and cross each other), then **rearrange into the opposite order**.

For example: From Bullish Arrangement → MAs entangle and stick → gradually turning into Bearish Arrangement, often **hinting that the original uptrend may be weakening or changing**.

However, please note two points:

1. **"Hinting at possible change" does not equal "Definite reversal."** After entanglement, the arrangement could turn, or it could just oscillate mid-way and return to the original arrangement. It is an observation clue, not a prediction conclusion.
2. **It also has lagging characteristics.** Like Golden Cross and Death Cross, the Multi-MA System is based on historical prices, so changes in arrangement also lag behind the price itself.

Therefore, the Multi-MA arrangement is suitable for **describing and confirming the current market state (Strong / Weak / Converting)**, rather than predicting the future or serving as a standalone basis for trading.

## V. Key Takeaways

- **Multi-MA System**: Simultaneously using short / medium / long MAs to judge market strength based on arrangement order.
- **Bullish Arrangement**: From top to bottom = Price > Short-term > Medium-term > Long-term (Short on top, Price on top), representing a strong uptrend structure.
- **Bearish Arrangement**: From top to bottom = Long-term > Medium-term > Short-term > Price (Short on bottom, Price on bottom), representing a weak downtrend structure.
- **Arrangement Conversion** (reversal after entanglement) hints that the trend may be changing, but does not equal a definite reversal, and is lagging, for reference only.
