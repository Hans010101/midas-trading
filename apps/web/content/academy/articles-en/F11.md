## 1. Concept introduction: know the "system", but also know the "playing method"

In the previous module (F1–F10), we established the overall concept of "trading system": a complete set of repeatable rules, including six modules: analysis methods, entry and exit rules, risk management, fund management, psychological discipline, and review. We also learned the two most basic categories of frameworks-trend trading (F8) and swing trading (F9).

However, after many people learn the system, they still get stuck when it comes to the game: **"I understand the principles, but for this Stroke, what method should I use to do it?"**

- Some people use the same trick no matter what the market situation is. The results are fine when the trend is trending, but they are repeatedly slapped in the face when the market fluctuates;
- Some people chase breakthroughs with one stroke, buy the bottom with the next stroke, and hit the grid with the next stroke. Their playing methods change every day, and they can’t even tell what they are doing;
- Some people hear that a certain "strategy" is very powerful and copy it, but they don't know that it only works in certain market conditions and will fail in other circumstances.

The root cause of these problems is the lack of understanding of "strategy". In this article, we will draw a **"playing map"**: what exactly are trading strategies, what are their classifications, and what their respective positions are - paving the way for a thorough explanation of each strategy later.

## 2. What is a trading strategy?

Let’s first clarify the relationship between “strategy” and “system”. These two words are often used interchangeably.

**Trading strategy is the specific implementation of the two components of "analysis method + entry and exit rules" in the trading system. ** In other words, the strategy focuses on answering two core questions:

- **How should I judge the market and look for opportunities? **(Analytical method)
- **What conditions must be met for me to enter the market, and what conditions must be met for me to exit the market? **(entry and exit rules)

And the **system** is bigger than the strategy - in addition to the strategy (how to analyze, how to enter and exit), the system also includes risk management, fund/position management, psychological discipline, review and other parts of "how to execute this style of play safely and stably".

For example:

- **System** is like the entirety of a team - tactical play + physical training + logistical support + psychological construction.
- **Strategy** is the specific **tactical play** - whether to play fast break, positional warfare, or focus on defensive counterattack.

An important corollary: **A mature system can contain more than one strategy. ** For example, use trend following strategy in trending market, and switch to shock strategy in oscillating market - just like a team will switch different tactics according to the opponent and situation. Strategy is the "tool set" in the hands of the system, not the entire system.

## 3. Why should we understand different strategies?

Now that we have two major frameworks: trend and shock, why do we need to subdivide so many specific strategies? Because:

**1. Different market conditions require different playing methods. **
The market will not always be in the same state - sometimes it is a unilateral trend, sometimes it is range-bound, and sometimes it breaks through violently. One style of play cannot work in all market conditions. Trend strategies will repeatedly Stop Loss during shocks, and oscillation strategies will be crushed in the opposite direction during trends. **If you use the wrong market, no matter how good the strategy is, you will lose money. **

**2. No single strategy can be effective "around the clock". **
This is the core concept that F17 will talk about later. Any strategy has its "comfort zone" (suitable market conditions) and "minefield" (unsuitable market conditions). Understanding multiple strategies is to have options and switch strategies in different environments, rather than to take over the world with one move.

**3. You must first have a "map" before you can find your seat. **
Only by first knowing "what are the roughly playing methods on the market and what scenarios are they suitable for" can you judge "which type of playing method should be used in this situation" when facing specific market conditions. This map is what this article is for you.

## 4. Classification Dimensions of Strategies

Trading strategies can be classified from many perspectives. We use the three most practical dimensions to help you build a framework.

### Dimension 1: According to "suitable market" - trend type vs. shock type

This is the most important dimension, which directly corresponds to the two basic states of the market:

- **Trend Strategy**: Suitable for market conditions that **have a clear direction**. The core is to "follow the trend and follow the profit" and pursue "cutting off losses and letting profits run". Representatives are trend following (F12) and breakout trading (F13).
- **Shock strategy**: Suitable for market conditions that have no clear direction and go back and forth between ranges. The core is "sell high and buy low within the range" or "bet on price reversion to the mean". Representatives are mean reversion (F14) and grid trading (F15).

![F11 with picture 2](/academy-img/F11-配图2.png)

Remember one key point: **The profit environments of these two types of strategies are almost opposite**—trend strategies are most afraid of shocks, and oscillation strategies are most afraid of the start of a trend. This is why "identifying whether the current trend is a trend or a shock" is so critical, but also so difficult.

### Dimension 2: Divided by "direction" - going with the trend vs going against the trend

- **Follow the Trend Strategy**: Trade in the current dominant direction (Go Long for ups, Short/Avoid for downs). Trend following and breakthroughs are all trend-following. Its logic is to "stand on the side of the dominant force" and is relatively stable.
- **Counter-trend strategy**: When the price reaches extremes or at expected turning points, perform **reverse** operations (such as Go Short at high levels and buy at low levels). Mean reversion and bottom buying on the left are counter-trend.

> ⚠️ It must be emphasized: **The risk of counter-trend strategies is usually significantly higher than that of follow-trend strategies. ** Because you are working against the current dominant force - once your judgment is wrong and the trend continues, the loss may be huge (typically such as "catching a flying knife" by buying the bottom during a decline). Counter-trend play requires extremely high discipline and Stop Loss, and novices must be especially cautious.

### Dimension three: divided by "entry timing" - left side vs right side

This is an important dimension that F16 will specifically expand on:

- **Left side trading**: Make arrangements in advance before the **turning point is confirmed** (such as predicting that the bottom is coming and buying in advance). It is inherently counter-trend, and is characterized by "buying the bottom to find the top" - the cost may be better, but it is easier to copy on the mountainside and catch the flying knife.
- **Trading on the right side**: Wait for the trend or breakthrough to be confirmed before entering the market (for example, wait for the price to really turn upward and stabilize before buying). Its essence is to follow the trend/follow the trend, and is characterized by "higher certainty, but giving up the initial profit".

## 6. Key understanding: There is no "best" strategy

Before going into the details later, let me put the most important sentence of this batch here, it will appear again and again:

**There is no "best" strategy, only a strategy that "suits you and adapts to the current market". **

- No playing style is inherently "more advanced" than the other - trend strategy and shock strategy are just suitable for different market conditions, and there is no distinction between them.
- There is no so-called "holy grail strategy" - a method that can make money stably in all markets and at all times does not exist (detailed in F17).
- Adapting to you (personality, time, risk tolerance, see F10) + adapting to the current market is the only criterion for choosing a strategy.

With this understanding in mind when looking at each of the following strategies, you will not fall into the misunderstanding of "looking for a universal magic skill" - and that misunderstanding is the starting point for countless people losing money.
