## 1. Principle of strategy (and its "confusion")

Martingale is derived from the gambling betting method. The core action is: **After every loss, double the bet (double the position to cover), hoping that as long as the market rebounds once, it can recover the capital in one fell swoop, or even make a profit. **

Its logic sounds very "beautiful": as long as the price finally rebounds, all previous losses will be earned back by doubling the position this time. And - **its "winning rate" looks very high**: most of the time, the price will rebound after a small fluctuation, and Martingale can indeed "end with a small profit."

**It is this kind of "high-frequency small profits" that constitutes Martingale's biggest deception** - it uses the appearance of "making money most of the time" to cover up the essence of "destroying a few times". Many people were deceived by the continuous small profits in the past and thought they had found the "holy grail of guaranteed profits" until that devastating market hit.

## 2. Fatal risk: why it explodes sooner or later

Martingale has an insurmountable mathematical knot:

**1. It theoretically requires "unlimited funds". **
Doubling the position to cover every loss means that the position is 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32... **exponential expansion**. To sustain "N consecutive reversals", the funds you need are astronomical. In reality, everyone’s funds are limited. **

**2. As long as you encounter a unilateral market that is long enough, it will explode. **
In the market, "moving in one direction many times in a row" (unilateral trend) is bound to happen. When it happens, Martingale's doubling of the position will quickly expand the position to the point where your funds cannot bear it - the margin will be broken down, Liquidation, and the principal will return to zero. **

**3. In the contract (with leverage), the explosion is faster and harder. **
Leverage magnifies the exposure of each position replenishment, greatly accelerating the process of "continuous reverse → doubling → Liquidation". Playing Martingale in the contract will only bring you closer to Liquidation.

**Conclusion: Martingale used "high-frequency small profits" to accumulate a little profit, but used "low-frequency destruction" to swallow up all the principal and interest. In the long run, using it will almost inevitably lead to disaster in one continuous loss. **

## 3. Applicable scenarios? ——It has no "safe applicable scenarios"

Many strategies have "applicable scenarios + failure scenarios", but Martingale is different: **It has the potential to "return to zero if it continues to reverse direction" in any market situation. **

- You **cannot predict** when that devastating unilateral market will come;
- You can’t guarantee that your funds will last until the price rebounds;
- So **no "applicable scenario" is safe** - its danger is **intrinsic and structural** and cannot be "avoided by using the right scenario".

**This is why this article does not classify it as "applicable scenarios", but tells you directly: don't use it. **

## 4. Disadvantages and risks (core of this article)

- ★**Destruction, return to zero, Liquidation**: Continuous reverse → Double position replenishment → Position index expansion → Principal return to zero, this is Martingale’s **inevitable destination** (it’s just a matter of sooner or later);
- ★**Requires unlimited funds**: The actual funds are limited, and it is destined to be unable to carry a long enough unilateral position;
- ★**"High winning rate" is a trap**: High-frequency small profits cover up low-frequency destruction, making people mistakenly think that profits are guaranteed and they relax their vigilance;
- ★**More deadly in the contract**: Leverage accelerates Liquidation;
- **This is by no means a "guaranteed way to make money"**: If you see any statement that "double your losses and make sure you make no losses", please be **highly alert** - it is a scam or ignorance of risks.
