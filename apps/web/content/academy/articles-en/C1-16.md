# A Complete Chan Theory Walkthrough: Consolidation

> Midas Academy · Chan Theory Advanced · Complete Review

The first two articles deduced the rise and fall (trend). But in reality, the market is consolidating most of the time. This article deduces a section of **Consolidation Market**, focusing on one of the most practical things: **Consolidation and trend, what are the essential differences in the way they are handled in Chan Theory** - if used incorrectly, it will be repeatedly repaired by the market.

> ⚠️ Fiction for teaching purposes/indicates the market consolidation, does not name the real target, **does not predict whether it will break through upward or downward**, and does not make trading recommendations.

**Quick overview of this article**

- **Consolidation = only includes 1 Central Hub of this level**; the price returns back and forth in the Central Hub range (ZG~ZD) / shock range (GG~DD).
- The Divergence in consolidation is **Consolidation Divergence**: it has little meaning and **mostly only returns to the Central Hub**, not a trend reversal (different from trend Divergence).
- ★**Handling Differences (Core)**: **Follow the trend** (let profits run), **Cope with consolidation ranges** (Don't force one side); if used incorrectly, they will be repaired repeatedly.
- End of consolidation: Leave the Central Hub upward/downward to form a new Central Hub (change to trend), or the Central Hub extends/expands to upgrade; judge whether there are **Three Types of Buy and Sell Points + whether the Central Hub has been effectively left**; **the direction of the breakthrough is unpredictable**.
- ⚠️ **Suggestions/fictitious market conditions, non-real targets, no prediction of breakthrough direction, and not trading recommendations**.

## 1. Step 1: Identify "This is consolidation"

To deduce a consolidation, the first step is to confirm that it is really a consolidation. According to the definition of C1-4:

> **Consolidation = The trend of a certain level only contains one Central Hub of that level. **

The specific approach is still from the bottom up (K line includes → Fractal → Stroke → Segment → Central Hub), counting down - if there is only one Central Hub**, and the price returns back and forth in the Central Hub range (ZG~ZD)** and the shock range (GG~DD)** of this Central Hub, it is consolidation.

## 2. The second step: "Divergence" in consolidation - Divergence in consolidation

There is also Divergence in consolidation, but it is **Consolidation Divergence** (the back of the consolidation), which has a different meaning from trend Divergence (see C1-7):

- **Consolidation Divergence**: Compares the strength of two trends in the same direction of **entering the Central Hub** and **leaving the Central Hub** (judged by the pullback of the MACD yellow and white lines + the shrinkage of the red and green column areas);
- Its meaning is **weak**: consolidation divergence usually only results in price **returning to the Central Hub** (retracing within the range) ** rather than a trend level reversal**.

Therefore, the consolidation period is more about "structural opportunities within the range" (the price exhausts and falls near the upper edge of the Central Hub; it exhausts and rebounds near the lower edge), rather than unilateral following the trend.

## 3. ★Step 3: Differences in handling consolidation and trends (core)

This is the most important thing to remember in this article: **consolidation and trends must be handled with completely different ideas. **

- **Trend (≥2 Central Hubs in the same direction)**: **Follow the trend** - Following the direction of the Central Hub relay, it is not easy to talk about the top/bottom before the trend Divergence, and you can "let profits run" (echoing the trend play of Group F).
- **Consolidation (1 Central Hub)**: **Range Thinking** - The price goes back and forth in the range, **Don't use the trend following logic to force a unilateral direction** (otherwise you will be repeatedly slapped in the face by the back and forth of the range); Consolidation Divergence has little meaning, most of the time it returns to the Central Hub; Only when the consolidation is effectively broken** (Three Types of Buy and Sell Points appear, and the Central Hub is truly left) can it turn into a trend.

**In one sentence: follow the trend and respond within the consolidation range. ** Treating consolidation as a trend (chasing the rise and falling within the range), or treating the trend as a consolidation (frequently selling high and buying low, and leaving the market prematurely during the trend) are both common sources of losses. The value of Chan Theory lies in helping you first count the Central Hub, distinguish whether it is consolidation or a trend, and then choose the right way to deal with it.

## 4. Step 4: How to end the consolidation

Consolidation (single Central Hub) will not last forever, and there are several possibilities for its ending (continued from C1-2 Central Hub growth):

- **Up/down leaves the Central Hub and forms a new Central Hub** → The consolidation ends and may turn into a **trend** (the "new life" of the Central Hub);
- **Central Hub extension/expansion/expansion** (with or without Three Types of Buy and Sell Points) → Central Hub **upgrade** to a larger level.

The key to judgment is still those two items (C1-2): whether the Three Types of Buy and Sell Points appear and whether the Central Hub has been effectively left. **

> ⚠️ Note: The direction in which the consolidation will eventually break through cannot be predicted in advance. Consolidation prompts "the current direction is unclear and range response", which does not mean "it must go in a certain direction next"**. Wait until the breakthrough actually occurs (structural confirmation), and don’t bet on the direction.

## 5. Summary of review

In the consolidation market, the most taboo thing is to "use trend thinking to force the direction" - to chase the rise and fall in a range, and get beaten back and forth. What Chan Theory helps you do is to first objectively determine "whether it is consolidation or a trend" (number of Central Hubs), and then use the corresponding processing method (responding to the consolidation range, following the trend).

Again, I emphasize: ** indicates market conditions, is not a real target, does not predict the direction of breakthrough, and is not a trading recommendation. **
