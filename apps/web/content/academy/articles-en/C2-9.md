# The Cost of Emotional Trading

> Midas Academy · C2 Contract Case Library (Virtual Suggestion Review)

The biggest enemy in trading is often not the market, but your own emotions. In contracts, leverage will multiply the cost of emotional trading. This article uses a schematic case to see how trader L was dragged into a "vicious cycle" step by step by his emotions and his account shrank rapidly.

> ⚠️ The entire process is virtual/indicative, no real targets are named, no predictions of real market conditions are made, and no recommendations are constituted.

## 1. Scene setting

Trader L originally had his own plan, but on this day, **emotions took over his operations** (the process is fictitious, but extremely typical):

- Starting point: L made a stroke of a normal contract transaction, **misreading, Stop Loss** - originally this was nothing, losses are normal;
- But L** could not calmly accept this small loss of Stroke**, and his emotions started to get worse...

What happened next was a series of actions driven by emotions.

## 2. Operation process (emotionally driven vicious cycle)

**The first step·Retaliatory trading. **
The small loss in Stroke made L very unwilling: "I must make it back immediately!" So he immediately backhand, took a heavy position, and added leverage, hoping to "get it back." The result - **misread again, this time due to heavy position + high leverage, the loss was much more than the previous Stroke** (echoing F5 retaliatory trading).

**Step 2·FOMO Chase High. **
After losing two strokes in a row, L became even more panicked. At this time, he saw that another variety of ** was rising sharply, and he was afraid of "missing out on this wave" (FOMO), so he caught up with it as soon as his brain became hot. But what he was chasing was a high price that had already risen for a long time - as soon as ** caught up, the price pulled back, and he lost another Stroke** (echoing F5 to chase the rise and kill the fall).

**Step 3·Vicious cycle. **
Loss → Unwilling → Retaliatory heavy positioning → Lose more → Be more panic → FOMO chasing higher → Lose again → Even more unwilling... L fell into a **emotionally driven vicious cycle**, with increasingly chaotic operations, heavier positions, higher leverage, and **accounts shrinking rapidly in a short period of time**.

At the end of the day, L's losses were far more than the initial small stroke loss - it was emotion + leverage that turned a normal small stroke loss into a disaster. **

## 3. Dismantling of key nodes

**Node 1: There was nothing wrong with the starting point - the fault was in the "reaction to small losses". **
L The initial small StrokeStop Loss was **completely normal** (misunderstanding is normal, C2-2). The real problem lies in his **emotional reaction** to Stroke's small loss - he is unwilling to accept it and wants to make it back immediately. **Many disasters are not caused by the Stroke loss itself, but from "the next step of losing control after the loss." **

**Node 2: Revenge trading - turning trading into gambling. **
The idea of "must make money back immediately" made L abandon all rules (heavy positions, increased leverage, and hasty entry). At this time, what he was doing was no longer trading, but gambling - the goal changed from "making the right decision" to "recovering the Stroke loss". **Orders placed with emotion are almost destined to be bad orders. **

**Node 3: Leverage amplifies every mistake made emotionally (continued from F24). **
This is the key risk of the contract: **Leverage not only magnifies profits and losses, but also magnifies the consequences of "emotional operations"**. Retaliatory heavy positioning + increased leverage magnified the loss of L's second stroke; FOMO chasing higher + leverage magnified the loss of the third stroke. **In spot trading, being emotional may make you lose money slowly; in contracts, being emotional + leverage can make you lose money quickly. **

![C2-9 with picture 2](/academy-img/C2-9-配图2.png)

**Node 4: The horror of the vicious cycle - losses come from "compound interest". **
In ordinary trading, a stroke's loss is isolated. But **losses in emotional trading will be "chained"**: every loss in a stroke aggravates emotions and prompts even worse operations in the next stroke. This kind of "loss snowball" is much scarier than the loss of a single Stroke - it will drain the account in a short period of time. **

## 4. Key points of review

- **Disasters often originate from "the next out-of-control step after a loss" rather than the Stroke loss itself**: First, a small Stroke loss is normal, and the fault lies in the emotional reaction;
- **Retaliatory trading = turning trading into gambling**: Once the goal changes from "making the right decision" to "recovering losses", the order placed is almost guaranteed to be a bad order (F5);
- **FOMO chasing high = entering the market at the most dangerous position**: Chasing a high that has risen for a long time out of fear of missing out, and often pulls back (F5) just after chasing;
- ★**Leverage magnifies the cost of emotion** (continued from F24): "Emotional + Leverage" in the contract can make you explode quickly, which is far more dangerous than spot;
- **How to fight emotions**:
  - **Stop first after losing money** - leave the screen and review the Stroke (wrong judgment or execution, F7), **instead of immediately retaliate**;
  - **Rely on rules and disciplines beforehand** to combat emotions during events (F6) - for example, **set a "maximum daily loss limit", and if you lose, you will be forced to stop trading for the day**;
  - **Accepting losses is the norm**: It is far better to accept a small loss calmly than to roll into a big loss emotionally.

One sentence to end: **In contracts, what can destroy an account is often not a stroke of misreading a transaction, but the series of "want to get it back" impulses after the misreading. **Learning to **stop** after a loss is one of the most valuable disciplines in contract trading.
