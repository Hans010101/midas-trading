# Adding and Reducing Positions

> Midas Academy · C2 Contract Case Library (Virtual Suggestion Review)

When making money, people will face a temptation: "It has risen so well, should you add more positions to make more money?" This article uses two illustrative cases to compare - **J Adding positions randomly with floating profits** vs **K Reasonably reducing positions and locking profits** to see "how to operate the position when making money", which often determines whether you take the profits away** or spit them back** in the end.

> ⚠️ The entire process is virtual/indicative, no real targets are named, no predictions of real market conditions are made, and no recommendations are constituted.

## 1. Scene setting

Set **the same period to indicate an upward trend** (first rise and then pullback, numbers are fictitious): the price rises from 100 to 120, and then pulls back to 105. Both traders started at 100 Go Long and both made profits, but then operated their positions in completely opposite ways:

- **J: Randomly increase positions with floating profits** - The higher the price goes up, the more excited you become, adding more positions along the way and increasing leverage;
- **K: Reasonably reduce positions and lock in profits** - reduce positions in batches and pocket profits during the rising process.

## 2. Operation process

**J’s approach (increasing positions with floating profits):**

- 100 Open Long, rose to 105 → "The trend is really good, add!", add position;
- It rose to 110 → "It's still rising, add more!", added again, **even increased the leverage**;
- Rising to 115, 118 → Continue to add... Positions get bigger and bigger, **nominal exposure expands sharply**, and **average holding cost is raised all the way** (the higher you go, the more you add);
- As a result, the price rose to 120 and peaked, and **retraced to 105**: Since J's position was large and full at the high level, and the leverage was high, this wave of correction **void back all his previous floating profits**, and even **lost/approached Liquidation**.

**K’s approach (reasonably reduce positions and lock in profits):**

- 100 Open Long, rises to 110 → **Reduce part of the position and pocket part of the profit**;
- Rise to 118 → **reduce another part** to further lock in profits and lighten the position;
- The price peaked at 120, **pushed back to 105**: Because K's remaining position was already very light, this wave of correction **had little impact** on him - he **had already safely pocketed most of the profits**.

In the same market situation, J spit back the floating profit, and K took away the profit.

## 3. Dismantling of key nodes

**Node 1: J's core error - floating profit is not "the capital to increase the position indefinitely". **
J regards "book floating profit" as "capital that can be increased indefinitely". But the floating profit has not fallen into the bag yet and will change at any time. Adding positions to higher positions means: **The exposure is getting bigger and bigger, the average cost is getting higher and higher, and the overall position is getting closer to Liquidation**. **Floating profits are not capital, they are paper numbers that have not yet been obtained** - Once there is a correction, the positions added to the high position will backfire the most.

**Node 2: J’s situation is even worse - adding more positions and increasing leverage. **
J not only increases the position, but also increases the leverage, which makes the Liquidation price closer to the current price (the higher the leverage, the closer the Liquidation is, Group E caliber). So a normal callback (120 → 105) is fatal to a "large, full, and highly leveraged" position. **"Add positions with floating profits + increase leverage" exposes the account to huge risks. **

![C2-8 with picture 2](/academy-img/C2-8-配图2.png)

**Node 3: K's wisdom - reducing positions is "turning uncertainty into certainty". **
The essence of K's reduction of positions in batches during the rise is to gradually turn "floating profits on the books" into "real money in pockets"**. After reducing the position, the position becomes lighter, and the taking back during the correction will be smaller. It gave up the possibility of "if the price continues to rise, the remaining positions could have earned more", in exchange for the certainty of "the profit has been obtained" - in an environment of high volatility + leverage such as contracts, this certainty is very valuable.

**Node 4: It is not impossible to add positions, but it must be "disciplined". **
Let me make it clear: it is not wrong to add positions in and of itself, what is wrong is "undisciplined and random additions". ** Adding positions in a disciplined way (pyramid style) looks like this:

- **The more you add, the less you add** (the bottom warehouse is the heaviest, and the added parts are smaller each time to prevent the center of gravity from being raised to a high position);
- **There is an upper limit** (the total position has a ceiling and will not expand infinitely);
- **Stop Loss moves up simultaneously** (after adding a position, the overall Stop Loss moves up to protect existing floating profits);
- **Do not add leverage to increase your position**.

J's position increase accounted for all negative positions (adding more and more, no upper limit, no stop loss, and increased leverage), so it was "increasing indiscriminately". **Pyramid-style disciplined adding of positions and J's kind of random adding are two different things. **

## 4. Key points of review

- **Floating profit is not "the capital to add unlimited positions"**: The floating profit on the book will change at any time. The higher the increase, the greater the exposure, the higher the cost, and the closer to Liquidation. Once the correction is made, it will all be given back or even lose money;
- **Add position with floating profit + increase leverage = high-risk combination**: The higher the leverage, the closer the Liquidation, and a normal correction may be fatal;
- **Reasonable reduction of positions = turning uncertainty into certainty**: Reduce positions in batches during the rise, pocket profits, and take back little during the correction - very valuable in a high volatility + leverage environment;
- **Add positions in a disciplined manner (pyramid style)**: add less and less, have an upper limit, Stop Loss moves up simultaneously, do not increase leverage for the purpose of adding positions - this is "adding positions", J that kind is "increasing indiscriminately";
- **There is no rigid rule of "must increase position/must reduce position"**: The core is to "control the overall risk exposure" and don't let floating profits get to your head.

One sentence to end: The most dangerous thought when making money is "add more, earn more". **The people who can take away the profits are often not the ones who add the most aggressive positions, but the ones who know how to slowly make money in the rising market and not let the floating profits get to their heads**.

## 5. Main points of this article

- The same period of 100→120→105 market conditions: **J Floating profit and random increase in positions (the higher the increase, the more + increase leverage) → Exposure expansion, callbacks are all given up, and even Liquidation**; **K Reasonable lightening of positions and profit locks (position reduction in batches during the rising period) → Small impact of callbacks, profits pocketed**.
- **Floating profit is not the cost of unlimited position increase**; **Floating profit increase position + leverage** will make the Liquidation price close to the current price, and a normal correction will be fatal.
- **Reasonable reduction of position = turning uncertainty into certainty**; **Addition of positions must be disciplined (pyramid style: add less and less, have an upper limit, Stop Loss moves up, and do not add leverage)**, which is different from undisciplined addition.
- ⚠️ The whole process is virtual/signaled; the core is to control the overall risk exposure; it does not constitute suggestions, predictions, and high leverage is not encouraged.
