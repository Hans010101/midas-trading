# Complete Review of a Trend Trade (Method Demonstration)

> Midas Academy · Level 5 · Module 19 Practical Case Review

**Quick overview of this article**

- A complete demonstration of Stroke trend trading using a schematic case: **Identify the trend (F8: Raise the high and low points + Moving Average long) → Enter the market on a callback (F12, decide the reason/Stop Loss/position before entering the market) → Follow the moving Stop Loss (let profits run) → Leave the market with trend reversal (it is normal not to sell at the highest point) → Right or wrong in the review process (F7)**.
- What the review looks at is whether the **process complies with the rules** (identification/entry/Stop Loss/emotion), not a single profit or loss; the process is also acceptable for + small losses.
- ⚠️ This is a **process/method demonstration, not a script that is bound to succeed** - in reality, there may be a false breakthrough and a Stop Loss when entering the market; the winning rate of trend following is not high.
- ⚠️ Red line: **Quotes are indications/teachings, not individual stock recommendations**; entry and exit are **logical indications, not trading signals**; talking about the past does not guide the present, does not predict, and does not guarantee profits.

## 2. Case setting (suggestion)

For demonstration, we set up a scenario (showing the whole process without naming the varieties):

- There is a **suggestive upward trend market**;
- A trader using the **trend following system** we learned earlier (F8 framework + F12 specific rules);
- We followed him through the entire process of identification → entry → holding → exit → review.

Remember: the focus is not on "how much the market has risen since then", but on "how the judgment and execution of each step are made according to the method**".

## 3. Step 1: Identify trends (then F8)

The first step is to determine "what structure the market is in at that time" - use the information that can be seen at the time (without peeking behind).

Objective structure observed by traders:

- **High-low point structure**: The high points of the price are constantly rising, and the low points are also rising (review A7, F8 - this is a typical feature of the upward trend);
- **Moving Average**: The price runs above the upward Moving Average, and the Moving Average shows Bullish Alignment;
- Comprehensive judgment: ** is currently in an upward trend. **

![F26 with picture 1](/academy-img/F26-配图1.png)

Pay attention to the discipline of this step: he is judging "the trend already exists" based on the structure that has already occurred, but not predicting "how much more it can rise." Trend following is about following, not predicting (Review F12).

## 4. Step 2: Call back to enter the market (then F12)

After confirming the upward trend, the next step is "how to enter".

According to the rules of trend following, traders should not chase the highest point (which would lead to high cost difference and high risk), but rather wait for the price to pull back - when it pulls back to near a certain key Moving Average/early support and there are signs of stabilization, go long (enter the market on a pullback, review F12).

While entering the market, he pressed "Plan First, Trade Later" (F2) to set the elements of the Stroke transaction at once:

- **Reason for entry**: In the upward trend, when the support is stable, go long;
- **Entry point**: the area where the callback stabilizes;
- **Stop Loss Level**: falls below the low point of this callback / below the key Moving Average (once it falls below, it means that the "callback" may become a "reversal", and the judgment is falsified);
- **Position**: Use the fixed proportional risk method (F4), so that this Stroke will lose up to 1% of the total funds even if it is a Stop Loss.

![F26 with picture 2](/academy-img/F26-配图2.png)

The key to this step: Before entering the market, the limit of loss (Stop Loss) and the size of the investment (position) have been determined** - he knows "what will happen in the worst case".

## 5. Step 3: Move Stop Loss to follow (then F12)

After entering the trade, assume the trend continues to advance upward. Traders do not set a hard target of "must sell when the price reaches a certain price", but follow it with **Moving Stop Loss**:

- As the price rises, move the Stop Loss** up in a step-by-step manner** (for example, following rising lows, or moving up a certain Moving Average);
- This not only locks in part of the existing floating profits, but also gives room for the trend to continue to run (review F12 "Let profits run").

What he has to do is to control his position and hold it according to the rules - not to run away anxiously in advance because he has "already made a little profit" (that is the fear of F5), nor to remove the Stop Loss because "he wants to make more" (that is greed). The discipline of the holding phase is to respect the mobile Stop Loss rules set when entering the market.

## 6. Step 4: Exit when the trend reverses

All trends must come to an end. Assume that the price rally eventually fizzles out—a signal that the trend may be reversing:

- The price** falls below the moving Stop Loss** (or the trend structure deteriorates: the high point no longer rises, falls below the key Moving Average, or the Chan Theory structure deteriorates);
- According to the rules, traders **leave**.

Here is a fact that must be accepted:

> He **didn't sell at the top**. Moving the Stop Loss naturally means that the price must first fall back from the top and touch the Stop Loss before leaving the market, so you must always make profits from the period "from the highest point to the Stop Loss level". This is not a mistake, but a **normal cost** of trend following (recall F12). It is an unrealistic fantasy to "sell at the highest point". According to the rules, leaving the market after a reversal is already a correct execution.

## 7. Step 5: What is right and wrong in the review process (continue to F7 four quadrants)

After the transaction is over, the most critical stage of review is entered. The core of the review is not "how much did Stroke earn", but "whether the process was done correctly"** (Reviewing the results of F7 doing things correctly):

- **Identify trend**: Is the basis (high and low points, moving average) sufficient to judge the upward trend at that time? ——Whether the process is reasonable.
- **Entry**: Do you enter according to the "callback and stabilization" rule, or do you enter impulsively to chase high prices? ——Whether it complies with the rules.
- **Stop Loss and Holding**: Is Stop Loss set and strictly implemented? Does mobile Stop Loss follow the rules? Have you ever run or canceled a Stop Loss early due to emotions? ——Whether the execution is in place.
- **Emotion**: Were you disturbed by fear/greed throughout the process?

Then use the four quadrants of F7 to position: If the ** process conforms to the rules** - then whether it is a big profit, a small profit or a small loss this time, it all belongs to the category of "**doing it right**" (doing it right + making money = ideal; doing it right + small loss = acceptable, continue to persist). What the review needs to do is to confirm which processes are correct and which can be improved (for example, whether the timing of entering the market can be better, whether the Stop Loss can be more reasonable), and to polish the method more precisely.

## 8. Key reminder: This is a process demonstration, not a success script.

After this walk, you must pour a basin of "cold water on sobriety":

- In real trading, **this process may not be successful at all** - what you identify as a trend may be a false breakthrough; what you call back to enter the market may fall below the Stop Loss as soon as you enter the market (wrong judgment, Stop Loss out). Trend following has a low winning rate and has to endure many such small losses (reviewing F12).
- So what this article demonstrates is **"methods and processes"** - that is, "how to follow the rules step by step", **not a script** of "if you do this, you will definitely make money".
- Again: **Quotes are for indication/teaching purposes, not individual stock recommendations; entry and exit are logical indications, not trading signals. **

The value of review lies in this: **No matter whether you win or lose a single time, use the same set of processes and the standard of "approaching the matter, not the result" to polish the process** - long-term depends on the stability of the process, not the luck of a certain stroke.
