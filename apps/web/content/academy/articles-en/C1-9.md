# Timeframes, Recursion, and Multi-Timeframe Analysis

> Midas Academy · Chan Theory advanced · Multi-level joint chapter

"Level" is the soul of Chan Theory, and it is also the most easily misunderstood concept - many people directly regard it as "1 minute, 5 minutes, daily line", and the more you use it, the more confusing it becomes. This article explains two things clearly at once: what exactly is level and how it is "recursively" defined; and the most valuable method in actual combat - combining large and small levels (large levels set the direction, small levels find buying and selling points).

**Quick overview of this article**

- **Levels are built recursively** (K line → Stroke → Segment → Central Hub → Trend → Upgrade): This level of Central Hub is composed of ** sub-level trends**, and levels are ** relative and self-nested ** concepts.
- ★**Level ≠ Time Period**: The period is the **chart viewing scale**, and the level is the **trend structure size**; in practice, the usable period is **approximate** to the level, but in theory it is different (multiple level structures can be included in the same period chart), do not draw the equal sign.
- **Recursion** is the essence of Chan Theory: small-level Divergence→upgrade to large-level parts→large-level Divergence→upgrade again, cycle-big market is built up by small market recursively.
- **Small and large levels combined**: **The large level determines the direction and frame range; the small level finds the precise buying and selling point (entry/Stop Loss)**; the advantage is that the direction winning probability + cost/Stop Loss is better.
- Risks: **level mismatch**, **too much noise at small levels** (large levels are the main one, small levels are the supplement).
- ⚠️ They are all **analysis frameworks**, not predictions, not buying and selling orders, and not eliminating misjudgments.

## 1. Levels are created by "recursion" and are not prescribed.

Looking back at the structure we built along the way:

**K line → Stroke → Segment → Central Hub → Trend (this level) → (upgrade) to higher level**

The key is: the Central Hub at this level is composed of the overlapping trends of the "sub-level"; and the trend of this Central Hub is one level higher than the "sub-level". So the levels are stacked up layer by layer like this——

- Small-level trends constitute large-level "parts" (Stroke/Segment/Central Hub);
- Large-level trends can become larger-level parts...

Therefore, the level is a "relative and recursive" concept: there is no absolute "level", only "who is the lower level and who is the higher level". This is the essence of the Chan Theory level - **self-nesting, layers of recursion**.

## 2. Level ≠ time period (the most critical clarification)

This is a big pit that must be cleared:

> **Level, not equal to time period (1 minute / 5 minutes / 30 minutes / daily line). **

- **Time period** is your **scale of looking at the chart** - how long do you set the K-line to be (one per minute or one per day);
- **Level** is the size of the **trend structure** - the structure level defined recursively by Central Hub and trend.

The two are related, but not the same thing:

- In practice, people often use time periods as the "approximate carrier" of levels - for example, the 1-minute trend is regarded as the minimum level, and the upward trend is 5 minutes, 30 minutes, and daily. This is for convenience and operability;
- But strictly speaking, the same time period chart may contain several levels of structures at the same time (a daily chart has both daily level trends and smaller internal levels of Stroke, segment, and Central Hub). The level depends on "whether the structure has been completed and whether the Central Hub is of sufficient level", not "which K-line is used".

Remember: **You can use cycles to "approximate" levels (convenient in practice), but don't equate the two (theoretically different). ** Confusing this is a common source of confusion when using Chan Theory.

## 3. Recursion: the essence of Chan Theory

Taking "recursion" a step further into dynamics, it describes the process of **self-growth** of market trends (echoing the upgrade of C1-3):

- A small-level trend comes to an end, **Divergence** → Upgrade → It, together with the last Central Hub, becomes a **large-level Central Hub/part**;
- This large-level trend continues to develop, reaches the end, and then **Divergence** → then upgrades → becomes a **larger level** part again;
- ...and so on.

This cycle of "small-level completion → upgrade to large-level parts → large-level completion → further upgrade" is what Chan Theory calls **recursion**, and it is also its core insight into the market structure: **big market prices are built up by layer-by-layer recursion and upgrades of small market prices. **

## 4. Combine large and small levels: large levels determine the direction, small levels find buying and selling points

Once you understand levels and recursion, you can use Chan Theory's most practical method - **multi-level simultaneous analysis**. Core sentence:

> **Large levels determine the direction and frame range; small levels find precise buying and selling points (entry and Stop Loss). **

Specifically how to cooperate:

1. **Large Level Determines Direction**: First use **Large Level** to determine whether the current trend is a trend or consolidation, and which direction it is (use the Large Level Trend Type and buying and selling points, see C1-4 and C1-6). For example, if the large level is in a 1 buy zone, the direction may be upward.
2. **Find entry at a small level**: Near the "should pay attention" position at a large level, **switch to a small level**, use **small level buying and selling points/structure** to find a more accurate entry point, and Stop Loss can also be placed closer (echoing C1-8: This level's buying and selling points often correspond to sub-level buying and selling points).

The benefits are very real: **The large level guarantees the "probability of winning in the direction", and the small level helps you "lower the entry cost and narrow the Stop Loss"** - you have some advantages on both sides.

## 5. Risks during joint operation: level mismatch and small-level noise

Although Lianli is good, there are two pitfalls to avoid:

- **Level Mismatch** (continued from C1-6): Don’t use small-level buying and selling points to build large-level positions/expectations, and don’t use the patience of large-level to bear the fluctuations of small-level. Liaison means "everyone, big and small, performs his own duties", not "mixing levels together".
- **Small level means more noise**: The smaller the level, the more false signals and noise. The reason for entering the market at a small level is for "accuracy", but the direction judgment of a large level cannot be overturned just because of a slight disturbance in a small level - **The large level is the main one and the small level is the supplement**.

> ⚠️ Levels, recursion, and combination are all Chan Theory's **analysis framework**, which are used to understand the trend structure more three-dimensionally. They **do not predict prices and do not constitute buying or selling orders**. Multi-level analysis can improve the comprehensiveness of judgment, but it does not eliminate misjudgments - level judgment itself is also subjective.
