# K-Line Inclusion: The First Step of Chan Theory

> Midas Academy · Level 3 · Module 10 Basic Concepts of Chan Theory

**Quick overview of this article**

- **Inclusion relationship**: Among two adjacent K lines, the high and low range of one **completely covers** the other (the high point is not lower than the other, and the low point is not higher than the other).
- Processing the inclusion relationship is to eliminate structural ambiguity and make the top and bottom clear, making it easier to identify Fractal and draw Stroke.
- Processing rules: **Take "high high"** for upwards** (the higher of high point and low point is taken), **Take "low low"** for downwards (take the lower of high point and low point); the direction is determined by the trend of the previous K line.
- After processing, adjacent K lines no longer include each other, and the structure is clear-this is the **first basic skill** of Chan Theory.

## 1. Concept introduction: Before drawing the structure, first "straighten out" the K-line

Chan Theory needs to identify Fractal and draw Stroke on K lines, but the original K lines are often messy - some K lines are completely "wrapped" by adjacent K lines, making the structure look blurry. Therefore, the first basic step of Chan Theory is to deal with this "**inclusion relationship**" and straighten out the K-line sequence so that the structure can be clearly revealed.

## 2. What is an inclusion relationship?

**Inclusion relationship** refers to the fact that among the two adjacent K lines, the high and low range of one K line completely covers the high and low range of the other K line. **

Specifically, if the highest point of one K line is not lower than the highest point of the other, and at the same time its lowest point is not higher than the lowest point of the other - that is, its "upper and lower range" completely covers the other - the two K lines form an inclusive relationship.

For comparison: If two K lines "each have their own strengths" (one has a higher high point and the other has a lower low point, and do not cover each other), then it does not constitute an inclusive relationship.

## 3. Why should we deal with inclusion relationships?

The reason is straightforward: **The inclusion relationship will make the trend structure blurred. ** When the K lines wrap around each other, it is difficult for you to clearly judge where is the top and where is the bottom, and it is also difficult to identify Fractal and draw Stroke.

Dealing with the inclusion relationship is essentially to merge the included K lines into one according to the rules to eliminate this ambiguity. After processing the K-line sequence, adjacent K-lines will no longer include each other, and the top and bottom will become clear, laying a solid foundation for later identifying Fractal and drawing Stroke.

## 4. How to deal with it: take "high high" upwards and "low low" downwards

The core rules for handling inclusion relationships depend on whether the current ** is in the process of rising or falling ** (this direction is determined by the direction of the K line where the inclusion occurs and the previous K line: if the previous one is rising, it will be treated as upward, and if the previous one is falling, it will be processed as downward).

The rules are as follows:

- **Upward processing (during the rise)**: Merge the two K-lines that have an inclusive relationship, and take "**High High**" after the merger - that is, **the higher point of the two will be taken as the high point, and the higher of the two will be taken as the low point**. (Intuitive understanding: On the way up, merge the combined K lines in the "high" direction as a whole.)
- **Downward processing (during the decline)**: After merging, the "**low low**" is taken - that is, the **high point is taken as the lower of the two, and the low point is also taken as the lower of the two**. (On the way down, the whole thing merges in the "lower" direction.)

One sentence to remember: ** Take high and high when going up, low and low when going down. ** Never reverse the direction.

## 5. Comparison before and after treatment

After processing an original K-line containing an inclusion relationship one by one according to the above rules, you will get a "clean" K-line sequence: there is no inclusion between adjacent K-lines, and each local high and low point is clear.

Comparing the results before and after processing, the difference is obvious: before processing, the structure is ambiguous and the top and bottom are difficult to distinguish; after processing, the structure is clear and it is easy to identify Fractal and Stroke. This is why it is said that the processing of relationships is the first step of Chan Theory.
