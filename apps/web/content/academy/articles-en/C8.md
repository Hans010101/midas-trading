Three Types of Buy and Sell Points by #Chan Theory

> Midas Academy · Level 3 · Module 11 Chan TheoryCentral Hub and Buying and Selling Points

**Quick overview of this article**

- **Chan Theory's "buying and selling points" are terms describing structural positions, not buying and selling orders**; they are not forecasted, not guaranteed, and do not constitute investment advice.
- **Type 1 Buy Point**: At the end of the downtrend, the last period of decline is the turning low relative to the **Divergence** (momentum exhaustion) of the previous period.
- **Type 2 Buy Point**: 1. After buying, it rises and then pulls back. The low point of the pullback **does not break the previous low**.
- **Type 3 Buy Point**: The price **breaks through the Central Hub** upwards and then pulls back, and the price **does not fall back to the Central Hub** (does not break the upper edge of the Central Hub).
- The selling point is the above-mentioned **symmetrical mirror**; the buying and selling point will fail and rely on subjective judgment, and must cooperate with risk management. The relevant details need to be checked according to standards.

## 1. The most important words must be placed at the front

Before talking about "buying and selling points", there is one sentence that must be made clear first, and it runs throughout the article:

**The "buying and selling point" in Chan Theory is a set of terms that describe the trend [structural position]. It is not an operation instruction that "told you to buy" or "told you to sell". **

Although the names "Type 1 Buy Point" and "Type 3 Sell Point" contain the words "buy" and "sell", they refer to the specific position of the price trend in the Chan Theory structure, which is a label for structural analysis. They:

- **Does not constitute any investment advice or operating instructions**;
- **Do not predict that prices will definitely rise or fall**;
- **No guarantee of profit**, it is entirely possible to continue to fall when it reaches the "buying point", and it may continue to rise when it reaches the "selling point".

Please take this premise to understand the following content. We are talking about "how are these structural positions defined", not "where should you buy and where should you sell". (The following uses "buying point" as an example to explain, and "selling point" is a completely symmetrical mirror image.)

## 2. Type 1 Buy Point: the turning point after trend divergence

**Type 1 Buy Point (abbreviated as 1 Buy)** appears at the end of a downward trend**, and the core is "**Divergence**".

Recall the "kinetic energy exhaustion" we talked about in MACD Divergence: In a downward trend, if the **last period of decline** is compared with the **previous period of decline**, although the price has reached a new low, the **downward momentum (intensity) has obviously weakened** - this is **Divergence**. This **turning low** where Divergence occurs and the trend may turn from falling to rising is called Type 1 Buy Point in Chan Theory.

The structural meaning it describes is: "The downward force may have exhausted, and this is a potential turning point in the trend." - But please note that this is just a structural description, and it does not mean that it will definitely rise.

## 3. Type 2 Buy Point: The withdrawal does not break the previous low.

**Type 2 Buy Point (2 buy)**, appears after Type 1 Buy Point**.

The process is like this: after the Type 1 Buy Point appears, the price rises (a sub-level trend), and then there is a **sub-level callback**. If the **low of this callback does not fall below the low of the Type 1 Buy Point** (i.e. "**does not break the previous low**"), then the low of this callback is the Type 2 Buy Point.

Its structural meaning is: "After a rise and a correction, the low point was raised (no new lows were made), and the structure shows a certain stabilization." - Again, this is a structural description, not a signal of "it's time to buy".

## 4. Type 3 Buy Point: After breaking through the Central Hub, you cannot withdraw it back to the Central Hub.

**Type 3 Buy Point (3 buy)** is directly related to the **Central Hub** mentioned in the previous article.

The process is: the price breaks through a Central Hub upwards (leaves the Central Hub range upwards), and then there is a pullback. If the pullback does not fall back into the Central Hub (that is, the low of the pullback does not fall below the upper edge of Central Hub), then the low of the pullback is the Type 3 Buy Point.

Its structural meaning is: "The price has left the shock zone (Central Hub) upward, and the retracement has confirmed that it has not returned to the shock zone - the structure shows that it has left the Central Hub and may enter a new stage." - Still, this is a description of the structure and does not constitute any operation instruction.

## 5. Overview of Three Types of Buy and Sell Points

Looking at the three types of buying points in a period of time, it roughly follows the structure sequence: falling Divergence (1 buy) → pulling back after rising but not breaking the previous low (2 buying) → forming a Central Hub, and pulling back after an upward breakthrough but not returning to the Central Hub (3 buying). The selling points are completely symmetrical mirror images (the high point of the Divergence turning point, the rebound that does not break the previous high, the downward breakthrough of the Central Hub and the retracement of the Central Hub).

## 6. Again: this is a structural term, not an operation instruction.

After reading the definition of Three Types of Buy and Sell Points, please be sure to remember again:

- They are terms used by Chan Theory to mark the position of the trend structure, and are not buy and sell orders.
- Chan Theory **does not predict** the future: when it reaches the "buying point", the price may rise or continue to fall; it is normal that the "buying and selling point" will **fail**.
- Identifying these structural positions is highly dependent on the judgment of "Divergence" and "level", and these judgments are subjective and difficult, and different people may produce different results (this is also the "ambiguity" of Chan Theory to be discussed in the next article).
- At any time, you must put it into a **complete trading system** and **cooperate with risk management** to look at it, instead of taking action when you see a "buying point".

> ⚠️ Details that need to be checked: The precise definition of Three Types of Buy and Sell Points, especially regarding the criteria for judging "Divergence", the definition of trend "level", etc. There are some detailed differences between Chan Theory's original text and various interpretations. This article gives the common core definition; if strict uniformity is required, it is recommended to check these details according to the standard version of Chan Theory you adopt.
