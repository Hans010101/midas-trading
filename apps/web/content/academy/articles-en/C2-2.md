# Stop Loss Execution vs. Holding a Losing Position

> Midas Academy · C2 Contract Case Library (Virtual Suggestion Review)

Group E said that "Stop Loss must be set before Liquidation price". This article uses a schematic case to walk through the two paths of "**Set Stop Loss, follow discipline**" and "**Don't Stop Loss, carry orders to Liquidation**" side by side - you will see that if you look in the wrong direction, the ending can be very different**.

> ⚠️ The entire process is virtual/indicative of market conditions and fictitious operations. It does not name any real targets, does not predict real market prices, and does not constitute buying or selling advice.

## 1. Scene setting

Set a period of **indicative of a falling market** (the numbers are all fictitious):

- The price of a certain product is on the **100** line, and two traders C and D ** both judge that it will rise, and both open Long (Go Long) **, with the opening price being 100;
- The result was **both misread** - the price did not rise but fell, all the way down;
- The direction judgments of the two people are exactly the same. The only difference is: **C sets Stop Loss and executes it, while D does not set Stop Loss and chooses to carry the order. **

Let's just look at how far this only difference makes the ending.

## 2. Operation process

**C’s approach (by discipline):**

- Before opening a position, use the **1% risk method** (F4) to determine: the maximum loss of this Stroke is **1%** of the total funds. Accordingly, set the **Stop Loss at 95** (leave the market with a 5% drop), and the position should be reversed according to this Stop Loss range;
- The price fell below 95 → C **Without a word, Stop Loss and exit as planned**;
- Result: C lost **1%** of the total capital - a small loss acceptable to Stroke, and the account was almost unscathed.

**D’s approach (taking orders):**

- D **Stop Loss** is not set, and a higher leverage is used (assuming 10 times, the theoretical Liquidation price of long orders is ≈ 100×(1−1/10) = **90**, maintaining margin will actually lead to earlier);
- When the price fell below 95, D thought: "Wait a little longer, it will come back" - **Hold the order but not leave**;
- The price continued to fall towards 90, and the floating losses became larger and larger, and finally hit the liquidation → D ** was Liquidated and lost all the margin of this Stroke **.

## 3. Dismantling of key nodes

**Node 1: Before opening a position - C has a plan, D has no plan. **
C thought clearly "what will happen in the worst case" before entering the market (Stop Loss 95, 1% loss at most); D only had "it will rise" in mind when entering the market, and was not prepared at all "what to do if something goes wrong". **The outcome is actually determined before opening a position. **

**Node 2: Breaking below 95 – a watershed moment for discipline. **
A price break below 95 is the point where the two paths diverge completely. C regarded it as the **preset exit signal** and executed it mechanically; D regarded it as **the emotional struggle of "should I cut?" and chose "wait a little longer". **The meaning of Stop Loss is to turn the "emotional struggle during the event" into "pre-event mechanical rules" in advance. **

**Node 3: Stop Loss price vs Liquidation price - why Stop Loss comes before Liquidation. **
C's Stop Loss of 95 is **earlier and less extreme** than D's Liquidation price of 90 (long order: Stop Loss price > Liquidation price > 0). This is the iron rule of Group E - **Stop Loss must be set before the Liquidation price**: It is always better to actively leave the market with a small loss at 95 than to passively carry it to 90 Liquidation with a loss. **Active Stop Loss means "you decide how much to lose", while passive Liquidation means "the market decides how much to lose for you". **

**Node 4: Ending comparison. **
Also looking in the wrong direction - **C loses 1% (can still fight a hundred more times), D loses all the Stroke deposit (vital energy is severely damaged)**. The difference is not in judgment, it's all about "what you did at the moment it fell below 95."

## 4. Key points of review

The most important thing to remember about this case is this sentence: **In a contract, what determines whether you can survive is often not "whether you read it correctly", but "how much you lose when you read it wrong". **

- **Misreading is normal, Stop Loss is the bottom line**: No matter how powerful people are, they will make mistakes (both C and D are wrong); the difference lies in whether there is an **unshakable Stop Loss** to lock the loss in a small range when they are wrong;
- **Stop Loss should be set before the Liquidation price**: Let yourself **actively leave the market with a small loss** instead of passively carrying the Liquidation - this is the iron law of Group E, and it is also proved by D at a painful cost;
- **The essence of carrying orders is to "leave the decision-making power to the market"**: D thinks that carrying orders is "giving the market another chance", but in fact it means giving up the right to control losses and leaving the outcome to luck and leverage;
- **Leverage magnifies the cost of carrying orders**: D If low leverage is used, carrying orders will not be so fast. Liquidation - high leverage + carrying orders is the fastest "elimination combination" in the contract.

One sentence to end: **C and D both saw it wrong, but C is still sitting at the card table and D has left. ** This is the difference between "Stop Loss execution" and "Carrying orders" - ** It has nothing to do with whether you are smart or not, it only has to do with whether you are disciplined or not. **

## 5. Main points of this article

- The same falling market, the same Go Long misunderstanding: **C Set Stop Loss 95, exit the market according to discipline and only lose 1% of the total capital**; **D Without Stop Loss, carry the order to Liquidation (10 times ≈ 90, actually earlier), and lose all the margin**.
- **Stop Loss must be set before the Liquidation price** (Multiple orders: Stop Loss price > Liquidation price) - It is always better to take an active small loss than a passive loss; this is the iron law of Group E.
- The outcome is determined before opening a position (whether there is a plan or not); falling below the Stop Loss is the watershed of discipline (mechanical execution vs. emotional struggle).
- **Carrying orders = leaving the decision-making power to the market**; **High leverage + Carrying orders = The fastest exit**.
