#Central Hub: Core Concepts of Chan Theory

> Midas Academy · Level 3 · Module 11 Chan TheoryCentral Hub and Buying and Selling Points

**Quick overview of this article**

- **Central Hub = The overlapping range of three consecutive sub-level trends**; the three segments must overlap together, otherwise it will not be a Central Hub.
- **Central Hub upper edge = the lowest of three highs; Central Hub lower edge = the highest of three lows** (commonly overlapping boundaries).
- The essence of Central Hub is the **oscillating area of ​​long and short balance**, which often corresponds to consolidation.
- Central Hub will be **formed, extended, reborn**, and **have levels** (reflecting level recursion); more detailed advanced rules will be developed according to standard definitions.

## 1. Concept introduction: the "heart" of Chan Theory

If [Fractal](/academy/glossary#Fractal), [Stroke](/academy/glossary#Stroke), and [Segment](/academy/glossary#Segment) are the "skeleton" of Chan Theory, then **Central Hub** is the "heart" of Chan Theory - it is the most core and most difficult concept in the entire system. Many of the buying and selling points to be discussed later are based on Central Hub.

Central Hub describes a "balanced area" in the price trend where long and short forces repeatedly compete and no one can overwhelm the other for the time being. A thorough understanding of Central Hub is the key to making good use of Chan Theory.

## 2. The definition of Central Hub: the overlapping range of three consecutive sub-level trends

The definition of **Central Hub** is: **The overlapping range of three consecutive sub-level trends. **

This sentence contains a lot of information. Let’s take a look at it:

- **Three consecutive sub-level trends**: At a certain level, take **three consecutive segments** of smaller-level (sub-level) trends.
- **Overlapping range**: Each of these three trends covers a certain price range; the price area where they **overlap** together is the Central Hub.

Intuitively speaking: the three-segment trend is like three "price bars" that fluctuate up and down. If you stack them together, the price band covered by the three together is the Central Hub. If these three sections have no overlapping parts at all, then a Central Hub will not be formed.

## 3. The upper and lower edges of Central Hub: the boundary of the overlapping interval

Since Central Hub is an "overlapping interval", it has clear upper and lower boundaries:

- **Central Hub Upper Edge**: Take the lowest of these three trend **high points**.
- **Central Hub Lower Edge**: Take the highest of these three trend **low points**.

Between the upper edge and the lower edge is the Central Hub interval. (Why is it "the lowest among the high points and the highest among the low points"? Because only by taking it this way, it is the part where the three segments **commonly** overlap - the area that is higher than the lowest high point of any segment, or lower than the highest low point of any segment, is no longer covered by all three.)

## 4. What does Central Hub represent: a shock zone of long and short balance

The essential meaning of Central Hub is: **A shock area where the long and short forces are roughly balanced. **

The reason why the price goes back and forth repeatedly in this period, forming three overlapping trends, is precisely because the long and short parties have repeatedly seen each other in this price range, and no one can decisively gain the upper hand**. Therefore, Central Hub often corresponds to a period of shock and consolidation. Understanding this, Central Hub is no longer an abstract geometric definition, but has a market meaning.

## 5. The formation, extension and rebirth of Central Hub

Central Hub is dynamic and goes through several states:

- **Formation**: When three consecutive sub-level trends overlap, a Central Hub is born, and the range is their overlapping part.
- **Extension**: If the subsequent trend continues to go back and forth in this range without effectively leaving, the Central Hub range will be **repeatedly confirmed and continued**, this is called Central Hub extension.
- **Rebirth**: When the trend finally **effectively leaves** the old Central Hub and forms three consecutive overlaps in the new price area, a **new Central Hub** is born.

## 6. Central Hub levels

Central Hub is **level**: it is composed of sub-level trends and therefore corresponds to a certain level. Small-level Central Hubs can be combined in larger structures and upgraded to large-level Central Hubs - this once again reflects Chan Theory's "level recursion". Different levels of Central Hub have different meanings and scopes of influence.

> ⚠️ Advanced tips: The extension, expansion, upgrade of Central Hub, and the precise definition of "sub-level trends" are the more complex advanced content in Chan Theory. This article clearly explains the core definition of Central Hub (the overlapping range of three consecutive sub-level trends), the upper and lower edges, and the market meaning**; more detailed dynamic rules are recommended to be separately expanded/checked according to the standard definition.
