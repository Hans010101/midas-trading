# Combining Type 2 and Type 3 Buy/Sell Points

> Midas Academy · Chan Theory Advanced · Practical Practice of Buying and Selling Points

Earlier we talked about the Three Types of Buy and Sell Points (C1-5) and their levels (C1-6) category by category. In actual combat, they rarely "appear alone" - more commonly, several types of buying and selling points appear in succession and mutually confirming each other. This article specifically talks about how to combine the second type and the Three Types of Buy and Sell Points, and what kind of structural sequence they can combine.

The old rule is to nail the red line first:

> ⚠️ Buying and selling points are **structural terms**, and the use of combinations is an **analytical perspective** (to help you read the structure more three-dimensionally), **not a signal to "buy/sell when a certain combination appears"**. This article does not make predictions and does not constitute buying or selling advice.

**Quick overview of this article**

- **2 Buy to the left** (bottom, early, cheap, low confirmation, safe); **3 Buy to the right** (after breaking through Central Hub, late, expensive, high confirmation, stable) - corresponding to the left/right trade-off.
- Classic structure sequence **"1 buy → 2 buy → 3 buy"**: The essence is the progression of risk and degree of confirmation. The further you go, the more confirmed you are, but the more bottom space you miss.
- **The buying and selling points of different levels often correspond to/overlap** (the 3 buys at this level may be the 1/2 buys at the sub-level); commonly used in practice **The sub-level buying and selling points refine the buying and selling points of this level** (leading to "the combination of large and small levels").
- Two disciplines in combination: **levels must be consistent**; combination is an **analytical perspective, not the more signals, the heavier the position** (the buying and selling point will fail and needs to be included in the complete system).
- ⚠️ Buying and selling points are **structural terms/analytical frameworks, not operational instructions**; not predicted or guaranteed.

## 1. Compare first: 2 buys and 3 buys have different "characters"

Although they are both buying points, the positions and personalities of 2-buy and 3-buy in a market are very different (mirror of selling points):

- **Type 2 Buy Point (2 buys)**: Appears after **1 buy** (retracement does not break the previous low). It is **leftward** - closer to the bottom, earlier, and cheaper; but it is based on the assumption that "the turning point of buying 1 is true", with low confirmation and high risk** (in case the 1 buy is a misjudgment).
- **Type 3 Buy Point**: Appears after **Central Hub is broken upward** (retracement does not return Central Hub, does not break ZG). It is skewed to the "right" - the position is higher, later, and the price is more expensive; but what it confirms is that "a new rising period is about to unfold", and it has a high degree of confirmation and is relatively stable.

In one sentence: **2 Buy to the left, cheap but risky; 3 Buy to the right, expensive but stable. ** This corresponds to the "left vs. right" trade-off that Group F talked about - only it is expressed in the structural language of Chan Theory.

## 2. Classic structure sequence: "1 buy → 2 buy → 3 buy"

In a healthy "bottom reversal → rising trend" process, three types of buying points often appear one after another, forming a sequence of layered confirmations:

1. **1 Buy**: Divergence of downward trend, turning point (bottom, riskiest);
2. The price rebounds and then pulls back, and the pullback **does not break through 1, buy the low** → **2, buy** (the first confirmation of the turning point);
3. The price continues to rise, forming a Central Hub, and then **breaks through the Central Hub** upwards** and pulls back **without returning to the Central Hub** → **3 buy** (confirm the rising segment/new trend unfolds).

This sequence of "1 buy → 2 buy → 3 buy"** is essentially a progression of risk and confirmation: the further back, the more fully the turn/up will be verified, but the more bottom space you will miss. Understanding this sequence can help you figure out "which confirmation stage you want to participate in" - this is a choice of system and personality (echoing the C1-6 operation level, F10 is suitable for you).

## 3. Cross-level correspondence: buying and selling points of this level ≈ buying and selling points of sub-levels

An advanced perspective (also the bridge to C1-9, C1-10): **Buying and selling points at different levels often correspond to each other or even overlap. **

For example:

- A **3 buy** of this level (withdrawal after breaking through the Central Hub), looking at a smaller **secondary level**, this position of "end of withdrawal and re-up" may be exactly a **sub-level 1 buy or 2 buy**;
- On the contrary, the accumulation of several **sub-level buying and selling points** may be building a **this level buying and selling point**.

This shows that the buying and selling points are not isolated points, but nested in the hierarchical structure. A very useful approach in actual combat is to use "sub-level buying and selling points" to refine "this level's buying and selling points"** - this level tells you "roughly the structural position that you should pay attention to", and the sub-level helps you "position it more accurately". This is exactly the idea that will be developed in the next article "Union of Large and Small Levels".

## 4. Two disciplines for combined use

To use "combine and look" correctly, there are two disciplines that must be observed:

**1. The levels must be consistent (continued from C1-6). **
The several types of buying and selling points you combine are either a continuous connection of the same level (such as 1→2→3 of this level), or a clear combination of large/small levels (this level + sub-level). **Don't mix levels together** - If you put a 5-minute point and a daily point together regardless of level, the conclusion will be very confusing.

**2. Combination is an "analytical perspective", not "signal superposition = increasing bets". **
Seeing "Buy 1 + Buy 2 + Buy 3 are all in place" does not mean "the more signals there are, the more positions should be taken". The value of the combination is to help you more completely understand where the trend structure has gone; whether you want to do it, where the Go Long is, and where the Stop Loss is is still determined by your complete system (risk management, position, discipline). And every type of buying and selling point will fail, and combinations are no exception.
