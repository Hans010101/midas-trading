# Mean Reversion Strategy

> Midas Academy · C3 Strategy Topic Library

"If it rises too much, it will fall; if it falls too much, it will rise." This is the simple intuition of the mean reversion strategy. It's useful in a volatile market, but can be a disaster in a trending market. This article explains its logic and the boundary between life and death.

**Quick overview of this article**

- Mean reversion = **Betting on "the price will return after excessive deviation from the mean" and doing reverse** (opposite to trend following); the essence is **range/oscillation thinking**.
- **Applicable to volatile markets/with a clear mean Central Hub, invalid in trending markets**; ★Doing mean reversion against the trend in the trend = **Catch a flying knife** (the most fatal risk).
- Method: Use **Moving Average/Bollinger Bands/Deviation Rate** to measure deviation → reverse reference for excessive deviation → **Stop Loss** must be set (to prevent "non-regression").
- Advantages: high winning rate in shock; Disadvantage: disaster in trend + flying knife + possible huge loss in single failure + no guarantee of profit**.

## 1. Strategy principles

The core assumption of mean reversion is: ** After the price deviates excessively from its "mean" (average level) in the short term, it will tend to revert to the mean. ** (echoes F14)

Behind it is a statistical intuition - prices fluctuate around a certain Central Hub, and if they deviate too far, they may be "pulled back". Therefore, the mean reversion strategy is "reverse": when the price rises too high, it is bearish, and when it falls too low, it is bullish, betting that it will return to the mean. This is the exact opposite of trend following (following the trend).

## 2. Applicable scenarios and failure scenarios

- **Applicable to: market shocks and market conditions with a clear mean Central Hub. ** When the price fluctuates around a mean and returns repeatedly, the mean reversion strategy has a relatively high winning rate.
- ★**Expiration: Trending market (fatal). ** In a unilateral trend, the price will continue to deviate in one direction and not return** - at this time, doing mean reversion is to "catching the flying knife" against the trend: you thought "if it fell too much, it should rebound" and went to buy the bottom, but it continued to fall (halfway up the mountain); you thought "if it rose too much, it should fall back" and went to Go Short, but it continued to rise (Go Short was at the take-off point). **The biggest risk of mean reversion is encountering a unilateral trend. ** (Echoes C2-5, the risk of bargain hunting on the left)

## 3. How to use (signals and rules)

1. **Measuring the "Degree of Deviation"**: Use tools to determine how far the price deviates from the mean——
   - **Moving Average**: How far the price is from the Moving Average;
   - **Bollinger Bands**: The price touches the upper and lower bands (mean reversion usage of C3-5);
   - **Deviation rate (BIAS)**: (price − Moving Average) ÷ Moving Average ×100%, directly quantifying the deviation percentage (see D dictionary).
2. **Reverse reference when the deviation is too large**: The deviation is too high → bear reference; the deviation is too low → bullish reference (**range/oscillation thinking**, not trend thinking).
3. ★**Stop Loss** must be set: In case of "non-return" (the trend is coming), Stop Loss is the only protection to prevent "catching a flying knife and receiving serious injury".

**The essence of mean reversion is "range thinking" - it defaults to the fact that the price will come back; once the price "does not come back" (trend), you must rely on Stop Loss to admit your mistake and leave the market. **

## 4. Advantages, Disadvantages and Risks

**Advantages**: Relatively high winning rate** in **shocky markets** (prices do often return); statistical basis; suitable for range markets.

**Disadvantages and Risks**:

- ★**The trend is disaster**: going against the trend, not returning, and may continue to lose money - this is the biggest and most fatal risk** of mean reversion;
- **Risk of "catching a flying knife"**: Buying the bottom/top against the trend may lead to sinking deeper and deeper in the wrong direction;
- **A single failure may cause a huge loss**: Mean reversion often means "win more often, but lose a lot once you encounter a trend" (encountering a trend), **No Stop Loss will be hit hard by a trend**;
- **Profitability not guaranteed**.
