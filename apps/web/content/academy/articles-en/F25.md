# How to Study Trade Cases Without Copying Them

> Midas Academy · Level 5 · Module 19 Practical Case Review

**Quick overview of this article**

- Don't "copy the homework" (look at the answers/memorize the winning operations) when looking at the case - the case has a known result, is deceptive from a God's perspective, and the market conditions are not repeated. What you should learn is the process, not the result.
- The correct posture for case review: stand from the perspective of "at that time" and dismantle ① the objective structure at the time ② feasible responses (multiple reasonable choices, not the only answer) ③ the process is right or wrong (connected to the four quadrants of F7, the right thing is not the right result)**.
- The review is "your own decision-making process", not the "answer to the market"; it is an extended exercise of F7 review thinking.
- ⚠️ Red line: The case is a **scientific method, not a sure-win template**; it talks about the past, does not guide the present, and does not predict the future; it is a general indication and does not name individual stocks for "what to buy or sell."

## 1. Concept introduction: Looking at the case, what are you looking at?

Having learned this, we enter the "Practical Case Review" module. But before looking at any case, we must first correct one of the most common misunderstandings - **Many people look at transaction cases for the "answer". **

They stare at a completed market, looking at "where to buy and where to sell at that time", and then think: I will just do the same next time. This is a "**copy homework**" mentality-and copying homework is the fundamental reason why you can't learn anything from the cases.

This article, as the beginning of Module 19, needs to make it clear: what is the **correct posture** for case review. What it learns is **methods and thinking**, not a standard answer of "just do it and you will win".

## 2. Why you can’t learn anything by “copying homework”

There are three fatal problems when copying cases as answers:

**1. The case has a "known result" and is deceptive from God's perspective. **
When a market trend has ended and you look back afterwards, anyone can point out that "here is the bottom and there is the top." But at that time - when the market was still going on and the right side had not yet emerged - you had no idea whether this was the bottom. The "hindsight" style review is to deceive yourself from God's perspective: "Look, it was so obvious at the time." - In fact, it was not obvious at all at the time.

**2. Each market trend will not be repeated, and memorizing "must-win operations" is useless. **
If you memorize the "operation" in a case, the market trend you encounter next time will never be exactly the same as it. The market does not replay. Memorizing a specific "must-win operation" is like memorizing the answer to a math problem but not understanding the solution - you will be dumbfounded if you change the question.

**3. When copying homework, you focus on "results", but what you really need to learn is "process". **
"Whether you made money this time" is the result, which is greatly affected by luck; "Whether the judgment and execution at the time were good or not" is the process, and it is what you can take away and reuse. By staring at the results and copying, what you learn is luck; by studying the process, what you learn is the method.

## 3. The correct posture for case review: dismantle three things

The correct case review is to look at the "then" perspective (without peeking at the subsequent results) and dismantle three things:

**1. The objective structure at that time. **
Use the information that can be seen at the time (trend, high and low structure, indicators, support and resistance, etc.) to objectively describe: "At that point in time, what structure was the market in?" - Is it a callback in the upward trend? Is it the boundary of the shock range? Is this where the trend is likely to fail? **The key is: only use the information you have at the time and never peek at what happened later. **

**2. Feasible responses (what are the reasonable options). **
Based on the structure at the time, what are some reasonable responses that comply with the system/rules? Note that what is mentioned here is "coping methods" (plural), not "the only correct answer" - in real situations, there are often several reasonable choices (waiting for a pullback, waiting for confirmation of a breakthrough, or simply waiting and watching), each with its own risks and benefits. What the review should look at is "the logic and cost of these responses", not "which is the standard answer".

**3. The process is right or wrong (connected to the four quadrants of F7). **
Use F7's saying of "**Don't focus on the results**" to evaluate: Did the decision-making process at that time comply with the methods and rules? Instead of just looking at "did you make money this time?" Recall the four quadrants of F7 - **Getting the process right + losing money = acceptable** (the process is right, losing money is a probability); **Getting the process wrong + making money = dangerous** (relying on luck will reinforce bad habits). Case review evaluates the process dimension, not the profit and loss dimension.

## 4. Review "your own decisions", not review "the market itself"

This is a point that is easy to go astray: the core of ** case review is to review "whether the decision was good or not in that situation", rather than to study "how the market went after this". **

- Deviated review: Spend a lot of energy analyzing "why the market is going like this and what's the news behind it" - this is studying the "answer" of the market, which has limited help in improving your decision-making ability.
- Correct review: Focus on "**Facing the current structure, what should a reasonable judgment and execution be**" - the focus is on the "process/method", not the "answer to the market".

**You can't control the direction of the market, but you can polish your judgment process and execution discipline. ** The review should focus on the latter.

## 5. Case review vs. your own transaction review (F7)

How does it relate to the review mentioned in F7?

- **F7** talks about: the cycle of reviewing **your own real trading** (record → review → summary → improvement).
- **Case review**: It is to use "**historical and generalized cases**" to practice the **same method** - it is the **extended training ground** for F7 review thinking.

The two are of the same origin: they both use the method of "focusing on the matter, not the result" to dismantle the decision-making process, distinguish between wrong judgment and wrong execution, and summarize and improve. The advantage of case review is that you can practice this set of thinking repeatedly on **other people's/historical** cases, and the cost is lower.

## 6. ★The red line of this module: The case is a learning method, not a winning template

This sets the tone for the entire module 19 and must be at the forefront:

> ⚠️
> - **The case is only for "learning methods and practicing review thinking", and is by no means to give you a template or "sure-win operation" for "just do this and you will win". **
> - The case is about the "past" - it does not guide what to do "now", let alone predict what will happen in the "future". The same structure may have completely different results next time.
> - All the cases in this module are **generalized and schematic** (no specific stock codes are named, and no judgments on "should be bought or sold" are made) - what you want to learn is **ideas**, not copying the operation of a certain Stroke.

With this premise, we will use schematic cases in the next few articles to demonstrate trend trading, failed transactions, and review of system cooperation - remember, we are looking at **method**.
