# Trends and Breakouts in Futures: The Opportunity and Cost of Leveraging with the Trend

> Midas Academy · Level 5 · Module 16 Trading Strategy Types (Contract Practice)

**Quick overview of this article**

- There are "opportunities" to increase leverage following the trend (increasing capital efficiency and being relatively stable following the trend), but the price is that the error tolerance space is compressed**.
- ★Three-price relationship (Go Long): **Opening price ＞ Stop Loss price ＞ Liquidation price **; **Stop Loss price must be set before Liquidation price **, the person who takes the initiative to Stop Loss (Go Short mirror: Opening ＜ Stop Loss ＜ Liquidation).
- ★**The higher the leverage, the closer the Liquidation price is, and the smaller the room for error**; trends/breakthroughs require room for fluctuations, and high leverage will make you **suffered from losses/Liquidation** during normal corrections, and you will not be able to hold on to the trend.
- In the contract, **false breakthroughs, low winning rates, and repeated Stop Loss are all amplified**; **Leverage does not change the winning rate, but only amplifies the results**.
- ⚠️ Red line: Use low leverage + strict Stop Loss (set before Liquidation price) + position control; contract trend/breakthrough** does not guarantee profit, requires risk management, and the past validity does not represent the future**; the price is a logical indication, not a buying and selling signal.

## 1. Concept introduction: Is adding leverage according to the trend really "amplifying profits"?

F12 and F13 talked about trend following and breakthrough - two methods of playing with the trend (it was spot logic at the time). In this article, move them to the contract: **What will happen if we follow the trend and add leverage? **

The first reaction of many people is: "Following the trend has a high chance of winning. Wouldn't it be better to add leverage to amplify profits?"

This statement is only half true. Adding leverage to take advantage of the trend does have its opportunities**, but its cost is often ignored - while leveraging amplifies profits, it also reduces your space to "bear with fluctuations"**. This article will explain this "opportunity and price" thoroughly. The core is to understand the relationship between three prices: **Opening price, Stop Loss price, and Liquidation price**.

## 2. "Opportunities" to take advantage of the trend and increase leverage

Let’s talk about opportunities first, but stop there:

- **Improve capital efficiency**: With the same margin, you can control larger positions. If you follow the trend correctly, leverage can make the return on funds brought by this trend higher.
- **Following the trend itself is relatively stable**: The trend/breakthrough is to follow the trend. Standing on the side of the dominant force, compared with the counter-trend strategy, the basis for winning is relatively better (review F8, F11).

But be warned – the flip side of **opportunity is closely followed by price**. While capital efficiency increases, you also put yourself in a situation where "fault tolerance space is compressed." Here’s the real highlight of this piece.

## 3. ★Core: The relationship between the opening price, Stop Loss price, and Liquidation price

When making a contract, you should especially keep the relationship between the three prices in your mind. Take **Go Long** as an example:

**Opening price ＞ Stop Loss price ＞ Liquidation price ** (from top to bottom)

- **Opening Price**: The price at which you open a position.
- **Stop Loss Price**: The price at which you **take the initiative to admit your mistake and leave** (review A9, F2). When Go Long, it is **below** the opening price - when the price drops here, you Stop Loss as planned.
- **Liquidation Price**: The price at which you are **Forced Liquidation** (review E4). When Go Long, it is further below the opening price - the price drops to this point and the position is liquidated.

The correct order of the three is: **Opening price is at the top, Stop Loss price is in the middle, and Liquidation price is at the bottom. ** In other words——

> **Stop Loss price must be set at Liquidation price [before (above)]** (review E9). In this way, when the price falls, it will first touch your Stop Loss price (you take the initiative to admit your mistake and the loss is controllable), instead of falling all the way to the Liquidation price and being forced to liquidate (heavy losses).

**Go Short is a complete mirror**: opening price ＜ Stop Loss price ＜ Liquidation price - the Stop Loss price is above the opening price and the Liquidation price is above (the Liquidation price for short orders is above, review E4).

This sequence is the foundation of contract risk control: **You must be the master of your own Stop Loss, rather than leaving life and death to the Liquidation line. **

## 4. How does leverage compress "fault tolerance space"

Now add leverage and see how it changes the pattern above.

Review the key conclusion of E4: **The higher the leverage, the closer the Liquidation price is to the position price** (Approximately, under N times leverage, the reverse direction is about (100÷N)%, which is close to Liquidation - 10 times is about 10%, 20 times is about 5%, and 50 times is about 2%).

This means:

- **Low Leverage**: The Liquidation price is **far** away from the position price. You have enough space to set Stop Loss before the Liquidation price. Normal price fluctuations will not easily hit the Liquidation line. **Large room for error. **
- **High Leverage**: The Liquidation price is **very close** to the position price, and the space left for Stop Loss is compressed. A **normal callback fluctuation** may directly hit your Stop Loss (being wiped out) under high leverage, or even hit the Liquidation line directly. **Small margin for error. **

![F20 with picture 2](/academy-img/F20-配图2.png)

Here lies a **fatal** contradiction to trend/breakout strategies:

- Trend and breakthrough strategies, **naturally need to leave "room for fluctuations" in the price** - there will be a correction in the middle of the trend, and there will be a retracement after the breakthrough (review F8, F13), these are normal and healthy fluctuations.
- But **high leverage squeezes out this space**. The trend should be "caught", because the Liquidation price is too close under leverage, you may be wiped out by a normal callback before the trend really unfolds, or even Liquidation will be eliminated.

![F20 with picture 3](/academy-img/F20-配图3.png)

In a word: **The higher the leverage, the harder it is for you to "catch" the trend - the easier it is to be washed out in the normal noise of the market. ** This is the biggest cost of "adding leverage with the trend": you think you can make more money by adding leverage, but in the end, the leverage makes you unable to control the trend.

## 5. In the contract, the risks of trend/breakthrough are not missing (and amplified).

Confirm the risks discussed in the general chapter again in the context of the contract - none of them have disappeared, and have been amplified by leverage:

1. **False breakthroughs still exist** (Review F13): Breakthroughs in the contract will still be false breakthroughs, and the **cost of false breakthroughs is magnified** under leverage - being wiped out by a false breakthrough may be a big Stroke under high leverage.
2. **The low winning rate and repeated Stop Loss of trend following still exist** (Review F12): The winning rate of trend following in the contract is still not high, and you have to endure continuous small Stop Loss; and under leverage, **Continuous Stop Loss will consume the margin faster** and the psychological pressure will be greater.
3. **Leverage does not change the winning rate, it only amplifies the results**: Let me emphasize again - adding leverage will not make your trend/breakthrough judgment more accurate, it just amplifies the results of every right and wrong. Counting on "increasing leverage to increase profitability" is to get the cause and effect wrong.

## 6. How to make trends/breakthroughs in contracts relatively steadily

If you want to do it, the following are the principles for reducing risks (only reducing risks, not guaranteeing profits):

- **Use low leverage**: Leave enough room for error in the trend so that normal corrections will not wipe you out. Low leverage is the prerequisite for "holding the trend" in the contract.
- **Strict Stop Loss, and set it before the Liquidation price** (Review E9): Always be the one who takes the initiative to Stop Loss, and do not leave your fate to the Liquidation line.
- **Reasonable position control** (Review F4, see the next article F23 for details): Use the fixed proportional risk method to control the maximum loss of single Stroke.
- **Accept the inherent characteristics of trend/breakthrough**: low winning rate, need to endure corrections and consecutive losses - these are just more in the contract, and the mentality must keep up.
