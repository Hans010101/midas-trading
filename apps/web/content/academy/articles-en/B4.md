# MACD Indicator: Principles and Composition

> Midas Academy · Phase 2 · Module 6 Classic Technical Indicators

**Quick Overview**

- MACD consists of three parts: **DIF line (Fast line), DEA line (Slow line), and Red/Green bars**, drawn below the K-line, centered around the zero line.
- **DIF = Fast MA − Slow MA** (Commonly 12, 26); **DEA = MA of DIF** (Commonly 9), smoother and lags behind DIF.
- **Red/Green bars = DIF − DEA**: DIF above is positive (Red, above zero line), DIF below is negative (Green, below zero line), intersection is zero.
- MACD reflects **trend momentum**; it is a **lagging indicator and does not predict the future**. Specific usage will be discussed later.

## I. Concept Introduction: From Moving Averages to "The Relationship Between Moving Averages"

Moving Averages can show the trend direction, but they have a shortcoming: they mainly tell you "up or down," but not very well how strong the "momentum" of the rise or fall is, or whether it is accelerating or exhausting. The **MACD indicator** is designed to fill this gap—it is built on moving averages and reflects **momentum** by observing the changes in the relationship between the fast and slow moving averages.

MACD is one of the most classic and widely used technical indicators. This section first clarifies its **composition and principles** (specific usage, Golden Cross, Death Cross, and Divergence will be covered later) to lay a solid foundation.

## II. The Three Components of MACD

When you open MACD, you will see an independent sub-chart below the K-line containing three things:

1. **DIF line** (Difference, also called Fast line) — A curve with faster fluctuations.
2. **DEA line** (also called Signal line, Slow line) — Another relatively smooth curve.
3. **Red/Green bars** (Histogram / Energy bars) — Bars arranged up and down around a central "zero line."

Among these three, DIF and DEA are the two lines, while the Red/Green bars are calculated from these two lines. Let's look at how they are derived one by one.

## III. How DIF and DEA are Derived

**Step 1: Calculate DIF (Fast line) first.**

DIF comes from the **difference between two moving averages of different periods**: subtracting a **Fast (Short-term) MA** from a **Slow (Long-term) MA**. The common parameters are Fast line 12, Slow line 26 (i.e., DIF = 12-period MA − 26-period MA).

> Note: MACD internally uses "Exponential Moving Average (EMA)" — a type of MA that gives higher weight to recent prices (a variant mentioned in B1). For understanding, you only need to remember DIF = Fast MA − Slow MA.

When the Fast MA is above the Slow MA, DIF is positive; otherwise it is negative. Therefore, DIF itself reflects the relative strength of the short term versus the long term.

**Step 2: Then calculate DEA (Slow line).**

DEA is the **MA of DIF** — averaging DIF again (commonly with a period of 9) to further smooth DIF. The common notation is **MACD(12, 26, 9)**, where the last 9 is the period of DEA.

Since DEA is the "averaged version" of DIF, **DEA is smoother and reacts more slowly (lags behind DIF)**. This point is crucial: **DIF is the Fast line, DEA is the Slow line.**

![B4 Figure 2](/academy-img/B4-配图2.png)

## IV. How Red/Green Bars are Derived: Bars = DIF − DEA

With the DIF and DEA lines, the third thing — the Red/Green bars — appears. Its algorithm is:

**Red/Green bars = DIF − DEA** (the "gap" between the two lines)

- When **DIF is above DEA** (DIF > DEA), the bar is **positive**, drawn **above the zero line**, and displayed as **Red**.
- When **DIF is below DEA** (DIF < DEA), the bar is **negative**, drawn **below the zero line**, and displayed as **Green**.
- When DIF intersects DEA (DIF = DEA), the bar length is **zero** (the bar crosses the zero line at this point).

Therefore, the Red/Green bars essentially intuitively draw out "how far apart the DIF and DEA lines are, and who is on top" using the **length and color of the bars**: the longer the bar, the further apart the two lines (the stronger the momentum); the bar turning from Green to Red, or Red to Green, corresponds exactly to the crossing of DIF and DEA.

> Tip: Some software displays the bars as 2×(DIF − DEA) for visual clarity, but the positive/negative direction and meaning are exactly the same — positive is Red, negative is Green.

## V. MACD Reflects "Momentum"

Combining the three parts, the core function of MACD is to reflect **trend momentum**:

- The **relative position** of DIF and DEA (who is above whom) reflects whether the short-term strength is relatively stronger or weaker than the medium-to-long term.
- The **length changes of the Red/Green bars** reflect whether this strength's "momentum" is increasing or decreasing: if Red bars keep getting longer, it means upward momentum is strengthening; if Red bars start to shorten, it means upward momentum is decaying (the same logic applies to Green bars for downward movement).

It needs to be emphasized: like Moving Averages, MACD is calculated from historical prices, so it is a **lagging indicator** that reflects momentum changes that have already occurred and **does not predict future prices**.
