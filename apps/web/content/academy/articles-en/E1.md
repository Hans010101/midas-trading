# What is a Perpetual Contract: A Contract Without an Expiration Date

> Midas Academy · Level 4 · Module 12 Contracts and Derivatives

## 1. Concept introduction: a contract that "will not expire"

In A11 we compared spot and contract. At this level, we specifically talk about **Contracts**—especially the **Perpetual Contract**, which is the most mainstream in the encryption market—in depth.

**Perpetual Contract (Perpetual Contract)**, as the name suggests, its biggest feature is "perpetual": **It has no expiration date and no delivery date, and can theoretically be held forever. ** This is very different from traditional futures contracts and is the key to why it is so popular in the crypto market.

## 2. Core Features: No Expiration Date

To understand sustainability, you must first know one of the characteristics of traditional futures contracts: they have an expiration date. The futures contract stipulates a future delivery date, which must be settled/delivered upon expiration. The position cannot be held indefinitely - if you want to continue holding it near expiration, you must "move the position to another month" (settling the contract that is about to expire and replacing it with one that is further away).

**Perpetual Contract removes the "expiration date" restriction. ** As long as your margin is sufficient and there is no forced liquidation, you can always hold this position without having to worry about delivery or moving the position to another month.

## 3. Perpetual vs traditional futures

Compare the two side by side:

| | Traditional Futures | Perpetual Contract |
|---|---|---|
| Maturity date | Yes, delivery on maturity | No, can be held for a long time |
| Move positions to another month | Required when expiration is approaching | Not required |
| Price anchoring method | Naturally converge to the spot when it expires | Rely on "Funding Rate" to anchor the spot |
| Common scenarios | Traditional finance, commodities, etc. | Mainstream of the encryption market |

There is a key point here: because futures have an expiration date, the contract price will naturally converge to the spot price when it expires (because it will be settled according to the spot price at expiration). **And perpetual has no expiration date, so it lacks the power of "natural convergence"** - so it needs another mechanism to keep the price close to the spot, which is the **Funding Rate** that I will talk about in the next article.

## 4. Why is the encryption market dominated by sustainability?

Perpetual Contract occupies a mainstream position in the crypto derivatives market, and it is not difficult to understand why:

- **No expiration, no need to move positions**: You can continue to hold and trade, eliminating the trouble of changing months.
- **Continuous trading and concentrated liquidity**: In conjunction with the 7×24 hours of encryption, Perpetual has become a variety with high activity and liquidity.
- **Two-way, with leverage**: Go Long or Go Short to meet various analysis and trading needs.

Because of this, the analysis of crypto derivatives mostly revolves around Perpetual Contract.

## 5. Components of Perpetual Contract

A Perpetual Contract usually involves the following elements:

- **Underlying/Index Price**: The spot underlying and its reference price (index price) tracked by the contract.
- **Margin + Leverage**: The margin you deposit, and the leverage multiple to enlarge your position.
- **Long and short direction**: You can Open Long (bullish) or Open Short (bearish).
- **Funding Rate**: A mechanism to anchor the perpetual price to the spot (see E2 for details).
- **Mark price and liquidation mechanism**: The price system used to calculate profit and loss and trigger Forced Liquidation (see E4 for details).

## 6. Risk warning (must be explained clearly first)

Perpetual Contracts are almost always traded with leverage, which means:

> ⚠️ Perpetual Contract comes with leverage, which will **magnify profits and losses** in both directions - while amplifying profits, **equally amplify losses**, and there is a risk of **Liquidation** (loss of margin, forced liquidation of positions). It is a **high-risk instrument**, and the mechanism itself is neutral knowledge, but it is in no way meant to encourage you to do high-leverage transactions. Understanding its risks is more important than learning how to use it.

## 7. Main points of this article

- **Perpetual Contract = a contract that has no expiration date and can be held for a long time**, which is the most mainstream contract form in the encryption market.
- The core difference from **traditional futures**: futures have an expiration date and converge to the spot at maturity; perpetual has no expiration date and relies on **Funding Rate** to anchor the spot.
- Cryptocurrency is mainly sustainable because it does not require position shifting, continuous trading, and two-way leverage.
- ⚠️ Perpetual leverage, **two-way amplification of profits and losses, with Liquidation risk**, is a high-risk tool - understanding the risk is the first priority.
