# What is Chan Theory: A Complete Market Analysis System

> Midas Academy · Level 3 · Module 9 Introduction to Chan Theory

**Quick overview of this article**

- Chan Theory is an analysis system that uses **strict geometric structures** to decompose and describe price trends. It originated from Stroke's online serial called "Tangled in Zen".
- The core structure is from small to large: **Fractal → Stroke → Segment → Central Hub**, and has the characteristics of **level recursion**.
- Compared with traditional technical analysis, Chan Theory emphasizes **geometric structure, complete classification (trend/consolidation), and level recursion**.
- Chan Theory** has a huge system, has a learning threshold**, and is an **analysis tool rather than a prediction tool** - it is not deified and does not promise guaranteed profits.

## 1. Concept introduction: from "looking at indicators" to "looking at structure"

After learning this, you have already learned about moving average, MACD, Bollinger Bands, RSI and other technical indicators. Most of them "superimpose a calculated value on the price" to assist judgment. And **Chan Theory** provides a very different perspective: it does not rely on a certain indicator, but **uses a strict geometric structure to dismantle and describe the price trend itself layer by layer**.

Chan Theory is a very influential and controversial analysis system in the Chinese trading circle. It has a large system and self-consistent logic, but it also has a high learning threshold. This article will first explain clearly: what Chan Theory is, where it comes from, and how it is different from ordinary technical analysis.

## 2. Source of Chan Theory

Chan Theory originated from an online serial: a series of articles published on the blog by the author under the name of Stroke **"Zhongzhongshuochan"** (the part about trading is often called the "Teaching You to Trade in Stocks" series). These articles were later compiled by readers and collectively referred to as "Chan Theory".

It should be noted that: **"Chen Theory" is the author's Stroke name**, and Chan Theory was originally published in the form of an online blog series. This training camp introduces the core concepts of the analysis method itself and does not involve the verification of the author's identity.

## 3. The core concept of Chan Theory: using geometric structures to decompose trends

The core feature of Chan Theory is to decompose the chaotic price trends into a set of progressive geometric structural units**. From small to large, the relationship is roughly as follows:

**Fractal → Stroke → Segment → Central Hub**

- **Fractal**: The minimum turning structure composed of three (processed) K lines, marking the local top or bottom.
- **Stroke**: Connects an adjacent Top Fractal and a Bottom Fractal, which is the most basic trend unit.
- **Segment**: A higher-level trend composed of several Strokes (at least three Strokes).
- **Central Hub**: It is composed of overlapping intervals of three consecutive trends. It is a very core concept in Chan Theory.

(Let’s establish the overall impression here first. The precise definitions of Fractal, Stroke, Segment, and Central Hub will be expanded one by one in the following articles.)

This set of structures has an important feature - **level recursion**: the same structure (Fractal, Stroke, Central Hub...) will **reappear** at different time levels. The combination of small-level structures into large-level structures is like building the same building blocks repeatedly on different scales. This allows Chan Theory to use the same language to describe trends at multiple levels.

## 4. How is it different from ordinary technical analysis?

Chan Theory and the technical indicators we learned earlier have very different ideas:

- **Traditional technical analysis**: relies more on **indicators** (Moving Average, MACD, RSI, etc.) and **classic forms**, with strong statistical and empirical color.
- **Chan Theory**: Emphasizes the use of **strict geometric structures** to describe trends, and pursues a goal called **"complete classification"** - that is, trying to exhaustively classify all possible trends into a limited number of types (the core are the two categories of "trend" and "consolidation"). In conjunction with level recursion, Chan Theory attempts to give the trend a structured and systematic description framework.

![C1 with picture 2](/academy-img/C1-配图2.png)

To put it simply: traditional indicators are more like "several indicators that conduct a physical examination of prices", while Chan Theory is more like "drawing a complete set of anatomical structure diagrams for prices".

## 5. Scope of application and learning difficulty (must be viewed rationally)

As an analysis framework, Chan Theory can theoretically be used in any market with continuous prices and K lines that can be drawn. But there are two points that must be said before:

1. **It has a huge system and a learning threshold. ** Fractal, Stroke, Segment, Central Hub, trend level, Divergence...the concepts are intertwined and need to be understood **step by step** and cannot be expected to be achieved quickly.
2. **It is an analysis tool, not a prediction tool. ** This point is extremely important: Chan Theory can help you **structuredly describe and understand trends**, but it **cannot guarantee predicting the future, let alone profit**. The claims on the market that "you can make sure money by learning Chan Theory/precisely copy the bottom and escape the top" are all exaggerated and misleading.
