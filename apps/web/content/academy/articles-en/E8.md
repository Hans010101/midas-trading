# Contract Risk: Why Contracts Are a Double-Edged Sword

> Midas Academy · Level 4 · Module 12 Contracts and Derivatives

## 1. Concept introduction: This article only talks about risks

The previous articles talked about mechanisms such as sustainability, Funding Rate, margin, Liquidation, and OI. This article does not talk about the new mechanism, but talks about the **risks** of the contract in a concentrated and frank manner.

Why use a separate article to talk about risks? Because contracts are **high-risk instruments**, too many people only see that they can "amplify profits", but fail to see that they also magnify losses and a series of risks behind them. **The purpose of this article is to let you put risk awareness before everything else when understanding the contract - do not beautify or exaggerate opportunities, only make the price clear. **

## 2. Multiple risks of contracts

The risk of a contract is not a single one, but several superimposed risks:

**1. Leverage amplification - two-way. **
Leverage can magnify profits, and it can magnify losses in the same way. A small reverse fluctuation in price may turn into a substantial reduction in principal under high leverage. Any description that only says "magnifying gains" and does not say "magnifying losses" is dangerously misleading.

**2. Liquidation - The principal may return to zero. **
This is the most deadly one. When the loss is close to the margin, the position is forced liquidated, and the margin may suffer a large loss or even return to zero. The higher the leverage, the closer the Liquidation price is, and an ordinary fluctuation may put you out (see E4).

**3. Funding Rate - ongoing holding costs. **
If you need to pay for long-term holding, Funding Rate will continue to erode your income (see E2), which is a cost that is easily ignored.

**4. High Volatility – Especially Crypto. **
The crypto market itself is highly volatile and operates 24/7. After adding leverage, the risk is further amplified; a wave of violent fluctuations in the middle of the night may trigger Liquidation.

## 3. It’s not just money that’s amplified, but also emotions.

In addition to the above "mechanical" risks, contracts will also amplify a more hidden type of risk - **Emotional and Behavioral Risk**:

- **Leverage amplifies emotions**: Floating losses and floating profits are magnified, and panic and greed are also amplified. People are more likely to make impulsive decisions (chasing the rise and killing the fall, taking orders, and adding positions in retaliation).
- **24-hour market-induced excessive trading**: The market never closes, which makes people addicted to watching the market and trading frequently. It not only accumulates handling fees and capital fees, but also makes it easier to make mistakes due to fatigue and emotion.
- **Fast feedback brings gambling nature**: Under high leverage, profits and losses come quickly and violently, making it easy for people to regard trading as gambling, rather than decision-making based on methods and probability.

These behavioral risks are often more harmful than single stroke losses.

## 4. Why novices should be extra cautious

When you add up the above, you will understand: ** contracts amplify almost all the risks in the transaction. ** For novices who are inexperienced and have not yet established risk awareness and discipline, directly starting high-leverage contracts often means ** a high probability of rapid losses or even liquidation being eliminated **.

This does not mean that a contract cannot be touched, but that before touching it, you must fully understand its risks and establish risk management capabilities. ** The order cannot be reversed—revere first, then talk about use.

## 5. Importance of risk management

Precisely because the risk is so high, **Risk management is not optional for the contract, but a line between life and death**:

- Control leverage, set Stop Loss, control positions, and reserve sufficient margin (see the next article E9 for specific methods).
- Always assume that "you may be wrong" and prepare an escape route if you are wrong.
- Put survival and controlling losses before "how much you earn".

> ⚠️ In one sentence: A contract is a **double-edged sword**, and the two sides of this sword are not equal** - the risk of loss (especially Liquidation) is often closer and more severe than imagined. This article does not predict, recommend, or encourage any high-risk transactions. I only hope you will take away one thing: **Remain in complete awe of the risks of contracts. **

## 6. Main points of this article

- The contract is a **high-risk instrument**, and the risks are superimposed: **two-way amplification of leverage, Liquidation (the principal may return to zero), Funding Rate costs, and high volatility**.
- It also amplifies **emotional and behavioral risks**: impulsive decision-making, over-trading, gambling--often more harmful than a single Stroke loss.
- Newbies who directly enter high-leverage contracts have a high probability of losing money quickly or even being eliminated; they must first understand the risks and establish risk management capabilities.
- **Risk management is the life and death line of a contract** - Put control of losses and survival first; this article does not encourage any high-risk transactions.
