## 1. Introduction of concepts: I know how to use all the tools, but I am still confused when faced with real charts.

We have learned a bunch of analysis tools before: K-line (Group A), Moving Average / MACD / Bollinger Bands / RSI (Group B), Chan Theory’s Fractal / Stroke / Central Hub / Buy and Sell Points (Group C). Take out each tool individually and you will understand.

But many people have a common confusion: they can use a single tool, but when they open the real chart, they are so densely packed that they don't know how to "read" it together. **

This article does not repeat "what each tool is" (that is the content of Groups B and C), but talks about how to combine these tools in Midas and "read structures and find opportunities" in a practical way - putting analytical skills into actual observation of charts.

## 2. The level of reading structure: from large to small

Reading charts is not about grabbing eyebrows and beards, but there are **levels** - it is recommended to go from large to small and go deeper:

**The first level: first look at the general direction - trend or shock? **
This is the most important first step (echoing F17 "Identify the market environment"). Using the high and low point structure of the **K line** (whether the high points and low points are rising/lowering) and the direction and arrangement of **Moving Average** (long upwards/short downwards/winding and moving flat), first determine: **Is the current trend market or a volatile market? ** Because this directly determines whether you should use the trend strategy or the shock strategy (F8 / F9). If you use it in the wrong direction, everything will be wrong in the future.

**Level 2: Look at the key positions again - where is the focus of long and short competition? **
Find the **key price areas** in the current structure: support and resistance (A6), Bollinger Bands’ upper and lower rails (Group B), and Chan Theory’s Central Hub range (Group C). These are important locations where price is likely to react.

**Level 3: Look at the kinetic energy and state - how strong and how is the position? **
Use **MACD** to assist in judging the strength of momentum, and use **RSI** to see whether it is in an overbought/oversold "deviation" state. They help you determine "whether the current movement has strength and where it is."

**Level 4: Look at the structural details - Chan Theory’s fine characterization. **
Use Chan Theory's **Fractal, Stroke, and Central Hub** annotations (Group C) to understand the internal structure of the trend more structuredly (note: Chan Theory's "buy and sell points" are **structural terms, not operating instructions**).

## 4. ★Principles of multi-tool verification (key points)

The core purpose of stacking multiple tools together is to **cross-verify** - but there are several principles that must be adhered to:

**1. Don’t rely on a single tool. **
Every indicator and every tool has its limitations and times when it will fail (Groups B and C have repeatedly emphasized: Moving Average lag, MACD passivation, Bollinger Bands hitting the rails and not reversing, Chan Theory has "to be checked" complications). If you only look at one tool, it's easy to be led astray by its limitations.

**2. Multi-tool verification improves certainty but does not eliminate misjudgments. **
When the trends (Moving Average/High and Low Points), key levels (Support and Resistance/Central Hub), momentum (MACD/RSI), and structure (Chan Theory) point to the same direction, your judgment will be more certain. But please keep in mind: **Confirmation only increases the probability, never eliminates misjudgment** - **No combination of tools can be 100% accurate**. If multiple tools are consistent, they may also be wrong together.

**3. Tools are references for "describing structures", not "buy and sell orders". **
The function of all these tools is to help you objectively describe "what structure the current market is in", rather than telling you "should you buy or sell". They are **references, not predictions** - treating the output of the tool as a buy and sell signal for mechanical execution is a dangerous misuse.

## 5. Read the structure → look for opportunities (but not signals)

After understanding the structure, how to "find opportunities"?

- Combine your **Strategy** (F11–F18) and **System** (F1) to judge: **With the current structure, is there any chance of meeting my rules? **
- For example: if you read the "callback of the upward trend" and your system is trend following, then this may be an opportunity point for "callback entry" (F12) - but this is **your judgment based on the structure and rules**, not the "signal" given to you by the tool.

**The key difference: it is "after you understand the structure, you can judge whether there is an opportunity according to your own rules", rather than "the tool pops up a buy and sell signal and you do it". ** The initiative and judgment are always in your hands.
