#Fractal:Top Fractal and Bottom Fractal

> Midas Academy · Level 3 · Module 10 Basic Concepts of Chan Theory

**Quick overview of this article**

- **Fractal** consists of **three adjacent K lines** (which have processed the inclusion relationship), marking local turning points.
- **Top Fractal**: The middle K line has the highest **high point and the highest low point** (the highest overall) → partial top.
- **Bottom Fractal**: The middle K line has the lowest **low and the lowest high** (the lowest overall) → local bottom.
- Fractal only marks the **local** turning point, which is the end point of drawing Stroke; it does not represent the reversal of the general trend, and it cannot be used to predict.

## 1. Introduction of the concept: "local turning point" of the trend

After processing the inclusion relationship of the K-line, the K-line sequence becomes clean. Next, Chan Theory needs to find the **local top and bottom** on this clean sequence - this is **Fractal**.

Fractal is the smallest turning structure in Chan Theory. It uses the relative positions of three adjacent K lines to mark a local top or bottom. There are two types of Fractal: Top Fractal and Bottom Fractal.

> Note: The "three K lines" mentioned below all refer to K lines that have been processed and contain relationships (see the previous article).

## 2. Top Fractal: The middle K-line is the "highest"

**Top Fractal** consists of **three adjacent K lines**. Its characteristics are:

**The high point of the middle K-line is the highest among the three; and its low point is also the highest among the three. **

In other words, the middle K-line is the **overall highest** - its high point is higher than the high points of the left and right K-lines, and its low point is also higher than the low points of the left and right K-lines. The two K lines on the left and right are "shorter" than the middle one.

This structure means that the price saw a local high when it rose to the middle bar, and then fell back - so the Top Fractal marked a **local top (a downward turning position)**.

## 3. Bottom Fractal: The middle K-line is the "lowest"

**Bottom Fractal** is just the opposite, also composed of three adjacent K lines:

**The low point of the middle K-line is the lowest among the three; and its high point is also the lowest among the three. **

In other words, the middle K-line is **the lowest overall**—the low point is lower than the low points of the left and right K-lines, and the high point is also lower than the high points of the left and right K-lines. The left and right roots are both "higher" than the middle one.

This means that the price made a local low when it fell to the middle bar and then recovered - the Bottom Fractal marks a **local bottom (a point where it turns upward)**.

Compare the two and remember, **never do them the other way around**:

- **Top Fractal**: The middle K-line has the highest **high point and the highest low point** (the highest overall) → local top.
- **Bottom Fractal**: The middle K line has the lowest **lowest point and the lowest high point** (the lowest overall) → local bottom.

## 4. Identify Fractal in the sequence

Apply this set of standards to a processed K-line sequence, and you can mark the Top Fractal and Bottom Fractal one by one. They appear alternately, outlining the local turning points of prices going up and down - and these turning points are the end points of the next article "Drawing a Stroke".

Please note: **Fractal marks a "local" transition, which is of a small level. **The appearance of a single Fractal only indicates the occurrence of a small local high/low point. **It does not mean that the general trend will definitely reverse**, nor can it predict the subsequent trend based on this. It is just a basic building block in the structure of Chan Theory.
