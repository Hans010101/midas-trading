# Preventing Liquidation in Contract Trading

> Midas Academy · Level 4 · Module 12 Contracts and Derivatives

## 1. Introduction of concepts: from "understanding risks" to "managing risks"

In the last article, we talked frankly about the risks of contracts, especially Liquidation. This article goes on to talk about how to prevent Liquidation and control risks at the practical level**.

Let’s put the ugly words first, this is also a red line:

> ⚠️ **There is no way to guarantee "absolutely no Liquidation". **The following are general principles for **reducing the probability of Liquidation and controlling risks**, and are not a guarantee of "just follow them and you will be safe". There will always be uncertainty and extreme market conditions in the market. Risk management can help you live longer, but it cannot make you immune to risks.

## 2. First place: control leverage, start with low leverage

The lever is the switch closest to Liquidation. **The higher the leverage, the closer the Liquidation price is to you and the smaller the room for error. **

- Beginners should start with low leverage, or even no leverage at first**. Low leverage means that when prices move in the opposite direction, you have a greater buffer and will not be liquidated as quickly.
- Always be clear: **High leverage magnifies risks first, not returns. ** Don’t take risks you can’t bear just to maximize your profits.

## 3. Set Stop Loss: draw a line for loss

Stop Loss (see A9) is one of the most important proactive means to prevent Liquidation.

- **Actively leave the market before Liquidation**: A reasonable Stop Loss price should be set at a position that triggers **earlier than the Liquidation price** - allowing you to admit losses and leave the market according to your own plan, instead of passively waiting for forced liquidation.
- Stop Loss is the discipline of "controlling small losses and not turning them into big losses (or even Liquidation)".
- Note: Stop Loss may also slip under extreme market conditions, but it still significantly reduces the risk.

## 4. Control positions to avoid full positions

Position management (see the entry on position management) determines how much you bet at one time.

- **Don't be full, don't put all your funds in one position**. A full position means one misreading can cause serious damage.
- A reasonable approach is to determine how large a position should be for a single stroke based on the loss you can bear - so that when any stroke transaction is misunderstood, the loss will be within the tolerable range.

## 5. Keep enough margin and don’t follow the red line

- **Leave a margin for margin**, and do not let the account be in a critical state of "almost liquidated" for a long time.
- The more sufficient the margin, the greater the reverse fluctuation that can be tolerated, and the less likely it is to be swept out by a normal fluctuation.
- The choice of Cross Margin / Isolated Margin (see E3) should also be determined based on your own risk tolerance, and you should be clear about the impact of each Liquidation.

## 6. Manage emotions: Don’t let impulse make decisions for you

For many Liquidations, the last straw is emotion, not the market itself:

- **Don't carry orders**: If you read wrong, leave the market according to discipline. Don't rely on the luck of "waiting and you will come back".
- **No retaliatory trading**: If you are eager to recover after a loss, you often increase leverage and place heavier positions, resulting in greater losses.
- **Don’t be kidnapped by the 24-hour market**: Avoid tired market-watching and addictive over-trading.

![E9 with picture 3](/academy-img/E9-配图3.png)

## 7. String these together

Preventing Liquidation does not rely on a single trick, but rather a combination of a set of habits: low leverage + Stop Loss + Position control + Keep enough margin + Manage your emotions. They have only one common purpose - to control risks and allow themselves to survive in the market long enough**. But again, remember: they reduce risk, not eliminate it.

## 8. Main points of this article

- ⚠️ There is no way to guarantee "never liquidation"; the following are the principles of **risk reduction**, not a promise of "just follow this and you will be safe".
- **Control Leverage** (starting with low leverage, more room for error) comes first.
- **Set Stop Loss** (set it earlier than the Liquidation price and take the initiative to leave the market), **Control the position** (the position is not satisfied), **leave sufficient margin** (do not post a red line).
- **Manage emotions**: Do not take orders, do not trade with revenge, and do not be kidnapped by the 24-hour market.
- These are a stacking set of habits designed to **manage risk and live longer**, but reducing risk ≠ eliminating risk.
