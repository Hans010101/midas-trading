# Arbitrage Strategy

## 1. Strategy principles

Arbitrage generally refers to the **neutral strategy** idea of **taking advantage of the price difference between related markets/varieties and earning the price difference** through simultaneous reverse operations (see D Dictionary, F21). Its characteristic is to try not to bet on unilateral increases or decreases, but to earn something relatively certain such as "price difference/rate". There are two common categories in encryption:

**1. Funding Rate arbitrage (neutral rate):**
Utilize the Funding Rate mechanism of Perpetual Contract (**positive rate: longs pay shorts; negative rate: shorts pay longs**, the direction cannot be reversed). Typical idea: **Hold spot long + open an equal amount of contract short** to hedge the directional risk; in the **positive rate** market, contract shorts continue to **charge** funding fees → net profit fees (echoing F21, C2-3).

**2. Futures price difference (basis) arbitrage:**
Utilize the spread between the contract price and the spot price (basis = contract price − spot price): When the basis deviates from the reasonable range and it is expected to return, earn profits from the spread return by operating the contract and spot at the same time.

## 2. Applicable scenarios and failure scenarios

- **Applicable to situations where there is a stable spread/rate, the ability to hedge both legs, and controllable costs. ** Arbitrage is suitable for participants who have funds, can operate both legs at the same time, and can strictly control costs and monitor **.
- **Invalidation/Risk Trigger**: Spread/Rate **Inverse**, Hedging Leg **Liquidation** Breaking the Hedge, **Cost Eating** Meager Gains, Liquidity Depletion - More in the next section.

## 3. How to use (concept and threshold)

The basic method of arbitrage (**Conceptual science, not a tutorial on making money**):

1. **Establish two hedging legs**: For example, spot long position + contract short position (in opposite directions, to hedge out the risk of rising and falling prices);
2. **Earn price difference/rate**: Net collection of capital fees under positive rate, or earn basis return;
3. ★**Strictly control costs and risks**: Handling fees, slippage, direction of funding fees, and the Liquidation risk of the hedging leg must be included in the calculation and monitoring.

**Where is the threshold? **Arbitrage** is not "just place an order and make a profit"** - it requires: sufficient funds (both legs will occupy funds), the ability to operate both legs at the same time, actuarial calculation of costs (the benefits are meager, and the costs are slightly higher and you will lose), and continuous monitoring (to prevent liquidation of the hedging leg and reversal of rates). **The bar is much higher than it seems. **

## 4. Advantages, Disadvantages and Risks

**Advantages**: **Neutral direction** (no betting on ups and downs, no reliance on predictions); theoretically **small fluctuations** and relatively stable returns.

**Disadvantages and risks (★Arbitrage is by no means risk-free)**: Arbitrage just replaces "directional risk" with **a basket of other risks**——

- **Basic fluctuation risk**: The spread does not return as expected, or even expands in the opposite direction;
- ★**Contract leg Liquidation risk (the most fatal)**: If the short position of the hedging contract is hit by the market market, the **hedging structure will be broken**, and it will be instantly exposed to unilateral risks;
- **Rate/spread reverse risk**: positive rate changes to negative rate (from charging to payment), price difference reverses;
- **Transaction costs**: Handling fees and slippage will **eat up the meager arbitrage returns** - thin returns are the norm for arbitrage;
- **Liquidity Risk**: One of the two legs has insufficient liquidity and cannot open/close a position in time.

**In a word: The returns from arbitrage are often meager, but the risks are real. "Risk-free arbitrage" is a misunderstanding. **
