## 1. Concept introduction: Discipline does not depend on determination, but on practice

F6 once said: Knowing that you can’t do something is due to emotion, luck, and laziness; crossing the gap relies on discipline. But where does discipline come from?

Many people think that discipline depends on "determination" - "I will definitely be able to control it next time." Unfortunately, determination is often vulnerable to emotions. Really reliable discipline is like an athlete's movements, which rely on repeated practice and become muscle memory.

And practicing discipline also requires a low-cost environment. This article talks about: **How ​​to use Midas' virtual trading to make "executing according to the rules" a habit, and make those anti-human mistakes in virtual trading at zero cost, and then correct them. **

## 2. Why "execution" must be practiced

Analytical ability can be accumulated by looking at pictures, but execution ability can only be accumulated by "really executing":

- The "Stop Loss" action is essentially **anti-human** - it requires you to take the initiative to admit your mistakes and accept losses. Just knowing "Stop Loss" in your mind does not mean that you will actually move your hands at that moment.
- "Don't carry orders", "Don't chase highs", "Short positions without a chance" - these are all actions that require fighting instinct.

**These actions must be practiced repeatedly in actual operations before they can be changed from "knowing" to "doing it reflexively". ** Virtual trading gives you a place to practice these actions repeatedly without paying the real price.

## 3. What to practice with virtual transactions: execution chain

Specifically in Midas virtual trading, it is recommended to practice this "execution chain" systematically:

**1. Practice "plan first and then trade" (continue F2). **
Force yourself: **Before every Stroke virtual transaction, write down the trading plan** - reason for entry, entry point, Stop Loss level, Take Profit target, and position size. Even if it is virtual, it is written without touching the ground. Make it a habit to "not fight unprepared battles".

**2. Practice "execute as planned and not make changes on the spot" (continue F6). **
After entering the market, **strictly follow the plan**: do not temporarily move the Stop Loss back, run away in advance, or increase the amount of money because of the floating profits and losses of the virtual account. What ** practices is "respecting the calm self when entering the field." **

**3. Practice "Stop Loss when it's time to Stop Loss". **
When the price hits the planned Stop Loss level, **execute Stop Loss** mechanically without hesitation or making excuses. Practice this most inhumane movement virtually to the point of "walking away when you reach the point".

**4. Practice "don't do it if you don't have a chance". **
Practice **short position waiting** and restrain your itchy hands (receive F6 for over-trading). When there is no opportunity to comply with the rules, "not trading" is also a correct operation - this ability to "hold back" also needs to be practiced.

## 4. ★Make these mistakes virtually at zero cost

This is the **core idea** of using virtual transactions to practice execution:

Those execution errors that will make you lose a lot of money in the real market - holding orders, randomly taking profit (making a little profit and running away), retaliatory trading, chasing the rise and killing the fall (these are the products of the emotional traps mentioned in F5) - instead of using real money to make them, it is better to make enough in the virtual world:

- Take an order virtually and see with your own eyes how the floating losses get bigger and bigger;
- Do a virtual revenge trade and see how you lose more and more when your emotions get the better of you;
- Then **review, recognize the mistakes, and deliberately correct them**.

**If you make these mistakes in virtual reality, the price is just virtual numbers + a lesson. ** It is the most cost-effective to pay your "tuition" here.

## 5. Cooperate with transaction log + review

"Practice" alone is not enough. Recording and review are required to form a closed loop of correction (connect to F2 and F7):

- **Keep a transaction log**: Carefully record the plan and execution of virtual transactions;
- **Review and Execution**: Look back after each Stroke - "**Did I execute as planned? Where did I break the rules? Why?**" Take "whether the execution is in place" as one of the core points of the review.

Logs and reviews are the "supervisors" and "correctors" of your discipline execution - black and white words can continuously expose loopholes in your execution.

## 6. ★The Boundary of Honesty

We must honestly state clearly the limitations of virtual trading in "practice execution":

> ⚠️ **The biggest limitation of virtual trading is the lack of real financial pressure. ** In the real market, watching real money lose money, the emotional impact is far stronger than in the virtual market - just because you can calm down and stop loss in the virtual market does not mean that you can do it in real life with real money. Therefore: Practicing virtual execution is a good foundation, but when switching to the real market, you must still be clearly prepared for "emotions will be stronger and execution may be distorted", and continue to remain cautious.
>
> In addition: What is practiced is **execution itself**; **virtual results do not represent real results**; this article does not promise any profits.
