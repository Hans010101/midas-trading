# Thinking in Probabilities with Chan Theory

> Midas Academy · Chan Theory Advanced · Advanced Special Topics

After learning this, you must establish the **most important cognition** - what Chan Theory gives you is "**structure and probability**", not "**inevitability and prediction**". This article talks about how to look at Chan Theory from a **probabilistic perspective**. It is the "heart-warming" chapter of the entire C1 advancement, and also echoes the mentality and long-termism discussed by Group F.

**Quick overview of this article**

- What Chan Theory gives is **structure and probability**, **not necessity and prediction**; it helps you objectively describe "where the structure has gone", but "the structure has reached a certain position" only means "a certain move has a higher probability", not necessarily.
- The buying and selling points will fail, Divergence has three evolutions, the trend has multiple meanings, and the boundaries are entangled with thousands of people - all of this shows that **Chan Theory is a probability framework, not a fortune-telling machine**.
- "Inevitability" does not exist: The market is full of uncertainties, and no framework can eliminate uncertainties and can only find probabilistic advantages; claiming that "Chan Theory must be accurate" is a myth.
- Four key points of probabilistic thinking: **Improving the odds of winning is not about pursuing certainty, always being prepared for misreading (Stop Loss/Risk Control), relying on repeated repetitions to make the probability manifest in the long term, and being right about things but not about the results**.
- **Probabilistic thinking vs. Gambler's thinking**: The former admits and manages uncertainty and relies on long-term probability and compound interest; the latter denies uncertainty, bets on certainty, and doubles losses.
- ⚠️ Chan Theory is an **analysis framework/probabilistic reference**. It does not predict, guarantee, or deify; it can only be used with probabilistic thinking and awe.

## 1. Chan Theory is not "fortune telling", but a "structural probability framework"

Many people learn Chan Theory for the purpose of "whether they can calculate every stroke accurately." This was wrong from the start.

- The strength of Chan Theory is that it **strictly defines the trend structure** (Stroke / Segment / Central Hub / Level / Buying and Selling Point), allowing you to **objectively describe "where the structure has gone now"**;
- But "the structure reaches a certain position" only means "the probability of a certain move next is relatively higher", and it does not mean "it must move this way"**.

Many of the things we have talked about along the way are actually saying the same thing over and over again: **Chan Theory gives probability, not certainty**——

- **The buying and selling point will fail** (C1-19);
- **There are three kinds of evolution after Divergence** (C1-18), Divergence is not necessarily reversed;
- **The trend has multiple meanings** (C1-17), and different levels have different interpretations;
- **Segment, expansion/extension has "Thousands of People and Thousands of Entanglements"** (C1-1, C1-2)...

All of this is telling you: **Chan Theory is a structural framework that helps you grasp probabilities, not a fortune-telling machine. **

## 2. Why "inevitable" does not exist

The reason is simple: the foreign exchange market is the result of games played by countless participants and is full of uncertainties. ** No analytical framework - Chan Theory, traditional technical analysis, or even AI - can ** eliminate this uncertainty ** and can only ** look for relative probabilistic advantages ** within it.

Therefore: **Any claim that "Chan Theory is bound to be accurate" or that "learning Chan Theory can accurately calculate the top and bottom" is deification and misleading. ** Those who truly understand Chan Theory know its boundaries best - it allows you to "see more clearly and have a higher degree of certainty", but it cannot give a "definite answer".

## 3. How to use probabilistic thinking

Put the "probability perspective" into several executable principles:

1. **Use structure to improve the "probability of winning" instead of pursuing "certainty"**: Chan Theory helps you stand on the side of higher probability, but it does not guarantee that it will be right this time - the goal is "long-term probability of winning", not "guaranteed success every time".
2. **Always prepare for "misreading"**: Since it is probability, **there will always be some mistakes** - so Stop Loss and risk management are necessary (echoing F4, C1-19). If you don't prepare for mistakes, you will be hit hard by that "part of the mistake" sooner or later.
3. **Rely on multiple repetitions to let the probability advantage manifest in the long term**: Single Stroke trading depends on luck, and long-term results will reflect your probability advantage and system (echoing Group F: Single Stroke relies on luck and long-term dependence on the system). Don’t use the one or two stroke method of judging winning or losing.
4. **Right to the matter but not to the result** (F7): Do not be arrogant just because you are right once, or deny the entire framework just because you are wrong once - what is evaluated is "whether the process/structure judgment is reasonable", not a single profit or loss.

## 4. Probability thinking vs gambler’s thinking

Comparing the two kinds of thinking side by side, the difference becomes clear:

- **Gambler's thinking**: Pursue "this one will win", bet heavily on "certainty", and double the desire to get back if you lose - the essence is **denial of uncertainty**;
- **Probabilistic thinking**: Accept single uncertainty, strictly control risks, and slowly win by relying on **long-term probability advantages and compound interest** - the essence is to **acknowledge and manage uncertainty**.

**People who use Chan Theory well are long-termists who "structure probability + strict risk control", rather than gamblers who "rely on Chan Theory to calculate every stroke". ** The same set of Chan Theory, paired with probability thinking is a good tool, and paired with gambler's thinking is an amplifier that accelerates losses.

## 5. C1 Advanced Heart Retraction

Borrow this article to give the entire C1 Chan Theory an update:

- Chan Theory is a set of profound and precise structural analysis framework, which is worthy of your serious study and slow practice;
- But its value lies in "helping you better understand the structure and grasp the probability", rather than "giving you a definite answer";
- **Use Chan Theory with probabilistic thinking and awe of the market, it is a good tool; treat it as fortune telling and pursue the inevitable, and sooner or later you will fail** (echoing F37 awe, F38 long-termism).

Once you understand this, you can truly use Chan Theory "right" - it is not an extra fortune-telling tool, but an extra pair of glasses that can clearly see the market structure and grasp the probability advantage.
