# What is Leverage: The Double-Edged Sword of Amplified PnL

> **Midas Academy · Phase 1 · Module 4: Fundamentals of Trading**

**Quick Overview**

- **Leverage = Using smaller margin to control larger positions**; the principal is called margin.
- Leverage **amplifies P&L in both directions**: profits are magnified based on the large position, and losses are magnified equally.
- The higher the leverage, the **poorer the resistance to volatility and the higher the risk of liquidation** (margin wiped out, position forcibly closed); approximately, under N-times leverage, a reverse fluctuation of about (100÷N)% could wipe out the margin; in reality, it triggers earlier due to maintenance margin, fees, etc.
- Beginners should **treat it with caution and start with low leverage**, understanding the principles first; leverage does not improve win rate, it only amplifies results and must be paired with risk management.

## I. Concept Introduction: Using Small Money to Move Large Positions

The word "leverage" comes from physics—using a lever to lift heavy objects with a small force. In trading, leverage means similar: **using a smaller principal to operate a position much larger than itself.**

For example: You only have 1,000 yuan, but using 10x leverage allows you to operate a position worth 10,000 yuan. The extra amount is essentially the "amplification limit" lent to you by the platform, and your 1,000 yuan is called **margin**—the capital you stake for this trade.

It sounds great: using small money for big returns. But we must state this upfront in this article—**leverage is a double-edged sword; it amplifies not just profits, but losses. For beginners, it is primarily a risk tool, not a profit accelerator.**

## II. How Leverage Amplifies P&L: Amplified in Both Directions

The core mechanism of leverage is: **your P&L is calculated based on the "amplified large position," but your principal is only that small margin.**

Let's use 10x leverage and 1,000 yuan margin (corresponding to a 10,000 yuan position) to see what happens if the price moves up or down by 2%:

- Price **rises 2%**: The 10,000 yuan position earns 200 yuan. Relative to your 1,000 yuan principal, this is **+20%**.
- Price **falls 2%**: The 10,000 yuan position loses 200 yuan. Relative to your 1,000 yuan principal, this is **−20%**.

Do you see it? The price only moved 2%, but your principal P&L was amplified to 20%—**amplified by a full 10 times**.

The key is: **this amplification is bidirectional.** It can double your profits, and it can double your losses. Leverage itself is neither good nor bad; it simply "faithfully and equally" amplifies the result of your trade.

## III. Higher Leverage Means Faster Losses and Closer to Liquidation

This is the most important part of this article; please read it clearly.

Since P&L is amplified, **losses will also eat into your margin at a faster speed**. When losses approach your margin, the platform will **forcibly close your position** to prevent your losses from exceeding your principal—this is **liquidation** (margin wiped out, position forcibly closed).

The higher the leverage, the smaller the reverse fluctuation you can withstand:

- **1x** (No leverage): The price must fall close to 0 to wipe out the principal; liquidation is almost non-existent.
- **5x**: A reverse price fluctuation of about **20%** could wipe out the margin.
- **10x**: A reverse price fluctuation of about **10%** could wipe out the margin.
- **100x**: A reverse price fluctuation of **about 1%** is enough to trigger liquidation—and a 1% fluctuation can occur in many markets within minutes.

*(Note: The above is to illustrate the approximate relationship between "leverage and resistance to volatility." Actual liquidation is also affected by maintenance margin, fees, funding rates, etc., and usually triggers **earlier** than the critical points calculated above.)*

You will discover a cruel fact: **The higher the leverage, the closer you are to liquidation, and the smaller your margin for error.** Under high leverage, a very ordinary price fluctuation could wipe out your principal.

## IV. How Beginners Should View Leverage

Based on the principles above, here are some objective reminders for beginners (not profit advice, but risk warnings):

1. **Understand before using.** Do not touch high leverage until you fully understand how P&L is amplified and how liquidation occurs.
2. **Start with low leverage, or no leverage at all.** Low leverage means a larger margin for error; you won't be forcibly closed as quickly when the price moves against you.
3. **Leverage cannot compensate for judgment.** It only amplifies results and does not improve the accuracy of your judgment. If you are wrong, high leverage will only make your mistake faster and more severe.
4. **Always leave room for "being wrong."** Pairing with stop-loss and position control (which will be covered later) is a prerequisite for using leverage, not an option.

A final thought for beginners: **Don't treat leverage as a shortcut to "making more," but as a risk source that causes you to "lose faster."** Only when you truly respect its risks do you qualify to discuss how to use it.
