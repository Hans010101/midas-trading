# How to Use the AI Decision Card: Treat as Reference, Not Instruction

> Midas Academy · Level 5 · Module 18 Practice trading with Midas

**Quick overview of this article**

- The AI decision card is **an analysis perspective/reference opinion** that can make mistakes, **not a standard answer, not a stock recommendation, not a buying and selling order**.
- ★**Never copy AI**: AI will be wrong, has limitations, is not predictable, and has no guarantee; copying = no learning + no control over risks + no ability to defend against AI mistakes.
- ★**Correct posture**: When there is "one more analysis dimension/check item", **make your own judgment first, and then compare it with the AI** (consistency increases confidence, inconsistency, explore the reasons, and comparison means learning); **Understanding the principle + independent judgment is more important than copying the AI**.
- AI is only an auxiliary reference for the "analysis method" in the system. It must be incorporated into the complete system (rules/risk control/discipline/review) and cannot be relied upon alone.
- ⚠️ Red line: AI decision card** is for reference only, must carry disclaimer, does not constitute investment advice, does not predict, and does not guarantee profit**; Midas is completely virtual, and virtual results do not represent real results.

## 1. Concept introduction: The most dangerous usage is "if it says buy, I will buy"

There is a function in Midas called **AI Decision Card** - AI will give a judgment, score and confidence level on market structure, long and short.

It's very convenient, but it's also the easiest to use incorrectly. The most dangerous usage is: **"When AI says it's bullish, I'll buy it; when AI says it's bearish, I'll sell it."** Treat AI's output as stock recommendations, buy and sell orders, and the answer to a guaranteed profit.

This article is dedicated to the **correct posture** of the AI ​​decision card: **use it as a "reference perspective/check item" and compare it with your own judgment, rather than as an instruction to make a decision for you. ** The red line in this article is particularly heavy, please read it carefully.

## 2. What is the AI decision card (brief description)

The AI decision card is an **analytical perspective** given by Midas's AI on the current market based on data and models - usually including the judgment of the long and short direction, some kind of score, and a confidence level.

But we must first nail its positioning: **It is "an analysis dimension/a reference opinion", not a "standard answer", let alone an "operational instruction". ** It is the same structure as the one you read using K-lines, indicators, and Chan Theory. It is just one of many references - and it is a reference that can make mistakes.

## 3. ★Why we must not “copy AI” (core red line)

This is the most important part of the entire article. There are several fatal problems in copying AI decision cards as instructions:

**1. AI can make mistakes and have limitations. **
AI research and judgment are calculated based on historical data and models. But the market is full of uncertainty, and no model can accurately predict the future. AI will make mistakes in judgment, and will have its limitations and blind spots - it is not a "smarter brain that won't make mistakes", it is just an analysis tool that will make mistakes. **

**2. AI does not predict the future and does not guarantee results. **

> ⚠️ Midas’ AI decision card (and all AI functions), **are for reference only, do not constitute any investment advice, do not predict prices, and do not guarantee profitability**. The judgments, ratings, and confidence levels it gives are all auxiliary reference information, not a promise of "what will happen in the future"**.

**3. Copying AI = giving up your own judgment. **
If you "do whatever the AI says", there will be three consequences:

- **You can’t learn anything**: If you outsource judgment, your analytical skills will never grow;
- **You cannot be responsible for the results, nor can you control the risks**: The decision-making is not made by you, and naturally the risk control cannot keep up;
- **Once the AI is wrong, you are helpless**: because you don’t know its logic at all, and you don’t have your own judgment to back it up.

**Outsourcing trading decisions to a tool that makes mistakes, has no guarantees, and is not responsible for you - this is very dangerous. **

## 4. ★Correct posture: treat AI as a "reference perspective/check item"

So how to use the AI decision card? The core is: **Treat it as "one more analytical perspective" and "one check item", and compare it with your own judgment. **

**1. Treat it as "one more dimension of analysis". **
Think of AI research and judgment as a "partner who will give you opinions" - it provides an additional perspective of observation, but the final judgment and decision are always in your own hands.

**2. Make your own judgment first, and then compare it with AI. **
The correct sequence is: **First use your own method** (F30: read K-line/indicator/Chan Theory structure) to get your opinion, **then** look at the research and judgment of the AI decision card for comparison:

- **If consistent**: can add a little confidence (but still not a "guaranteed");
- **If they are inconsistent**: This is precisely the most valuable learning moment - think "**Why do we have different opinions? Who missed something? Whose logic is more tenable?**" This **process of comparison and speculation** itself improves your analytical skills.

**3. Consider it a "check item". **
Use AI's perspective to **check whether you have missed anything** - for example, it mentions a dimension you didn't pay attention to and reminds you to take another look. It is an assistant to help you check for omissions and fill in the gaps, not a boss who makes decisions for you.

**4. Always: Understanding the principles + independent judgment is more important than copying AI. **
This is a sentence that runs through - **What really allows you to grow and make you responsible for yourself is the ability to "understand principles and make independent judgments"**, rather than "copying the conclusions of AI". No matter how much AI is used, it cannot replace your own judgment.

## 5. AI + your system

Finally, put the AI decision card back into the entire system (F1) to see:

- AI decision card is just an auxiliary reference for the "analysis method" dimension in the system**;
- It needs to be incorporated into your **complete system** for use - you also need your own **entry and exit rules, risk management, position management, psychological discipline, and review** (F1 six modules);
- **Never rely on AI alone to trade**. AI only gives "a reference opinion", and whether to do a transaction, how to control risks, and when to leave the market are all determined by your entire system.

## 6. How to use AI decision cards in Midas

- In the **full virtual** environment of Midas, **first analyze by yourself, then look at the AI decision card**, and practice "comparison and reasoning" - pay special attention to "inconsistencies" to delve into the reasons;
- Treat AI as a check item, check for omissions and fill in the gaps, but **decided to always do it yourself**;
- Incorporate AI references into your complete system (rules/risk control/discipline/review) for comprehensive judgment.
