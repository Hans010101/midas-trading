# Margin Mode: Cross and Isolated

> Midas Academy · Level 4 · Module 12 Contracts and Derivatives

## 1. Concept introduction: same margin, two "cover" methods

When making a contract, you must first put down a deposit. But there are two different modes of "using the money in the account to fund this position" - **Cross Margin (Cross Margin)** and **Isolated Margin (Isolated Margin)**.

The difference between them seems to be just the setting options, but in fact it directly determines how much you will lose during Liquidation. This article explains the differences between the two and their respective risks.

## 2. Isolated Margin: Each position uses an independent margin.

In the **Isolated Margin** mode, **each position uses a margin assigned to it separately**, and the risk is "isolated" in this Stroke position.

- The profit and loss of this position only uses the part of the margin allocated to it.
- Once the market reverses and Liquidation is triggered, **you will only lose the margin assigned to this position** at most, and the remaining funds in the account will not be affected.
- Risks are **clear, isolated, and controllable**—you know exactly how much loss this Stroke will suffer at worst.

For example: Isolated Margin is like placing each position in a separate "water-proof compartment". If water enters one compartment, it will not flood other compartments.

## 3. Cross Margin: Use the entire account balance to cover the bottom line

In **Cross Margin** mode, **use all the available balance in the account to jointly fund the position**.

- The floating loss of a position can be borne by the entire available balance of the account, so **a single position is not easily liquidated so quickly** (the buffer is thicker).
- But the price is: ** Once Liquidation occurs, the loss may not only be a certain Stroke deposit, but also affect more or even all the funds in the account. **
- The risk is **shared** - the buffer is larger, but if something goes wrong, the scope of the loss is also larger.

Continuing the above analogy: Cross Margin seems to open up all the cabins into one large space, which is more buoyant and can withstand small winds and waves, but once water enters, the entire boat will bear it together.

## 4. Comparison of the Liquidation impact of the two

Let’s explain the most critical difference - **How much is the difference in losses during Liquidation**:

| | Isolated Margin | Cross Margin |
|---|---|---|
| Margin source | The position is allocated separately | The entire available balance of the account |
| Single warehouse anti-fluctuation | Small (only the margin of this warehouse) | Large (all balances are covered) |
| Loss during Liquidation | Only the margin of this position | May affect more accounts / all funds |
| Risk characteristics | Isolation, clarity | Sharing, large buffer but large loss scope |

![E3 with picture 3](/academy-img/E3-配图3.png)

As for "which one to use", it depends on personal risk preference and usage scenarios, and there is no absolute advantage or disadvantage (this is a description of the mechanism, not an operational recommendation). But there is one thing you must remember:

> ⚠️ **Both Cross Margin and Isolated Margin, there is Liquidation risk. ** The choice of model only changes "how the risk is distributed", but does not eliminate the risk itself. Cross Margin has a large buffer but may cause more losses, Isolated Margin has limited losses but a single position is more likely to trigger forced liquidation - both need to be used with caution after full understanding, and in conjunction with controlling leverage and positions.

## 5. Main points of this article

- The contract has two margin modes: **Cross Margin** (all balances in the account are covered) and **Isolated Margin** (each position is independently margined).
- **Isolated Margin**: Risk isolation, during Liquidation, the **maximum loss of the margin** of the position will not affect other funds.
- **Cross Margin**: All balances are shared, and a single position is less likely to be liquidated (large buffer), but during **Liquidation, you may lose more, or even affect all funds**.
- ⚠️Both models **have Liquidation risk**. The choice only changes the risk distribution but does not eliminate the risk. Both models need to be used with caution and coordinate the control of leverage and positions.
