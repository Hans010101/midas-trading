# Multiple Central Hubs Within a Trend

> Midas Academy · Chan Theory Advanced · Advanced Special Topics

The same trend can look one way at the 5-minute level, and another way at the 30-minute level - this is the "**Trend/Central Hub Ambiguity**" in Chan Theory. It often confuses beginners: "Which one is correct? Why do I draw it differently from others?" This article explains the ambiguity thoroughly: where it comes from, whether it is a bug, and how to deal with it.

**Quick overview of this article**

- **Ambiguity = The same trend has different structural interpretations at different levels** (at a small level, it is a trend, and at a large level, it may be just a Central Hub or a Stroke).
- It comes from **level recursion** (multi-scale nesting of market structure), which is a **feature, not a bug**; different levels of interpretation are correct at their respective levels.
- The key is not "which level is right", but **which level you use as the operating level** (C1-6) - Once you set the operating level and use it as the main one, ambiguity will not bother you.
- Countermeasures: **Determine the operation level first (mainly) + combine large and small levels (large direction/small entry) + don’t jump repeatedly between levels**.
- Ambiguity is a major source of "thousands of people and thousands of entanglements" (many differences are just different levels of viewing), but there are still standards for basic skills (C1-13).
- ⚠️ The entire article is an **analysis framework** and does not make predictions or constitute buying or selling orders.

## 1. What is "ambiguity"

**Ambiguity means that the same trend can have different structural interpretations at different levels** - at a small level, it may be a "trend", but at a large level, it may be just a "fluctuation within the Central Hub"; a "Central Hub" at a small level may be just "a Stroke" or "a paragraph" at a large level.

In other words: **"Is this trend a trend, a Central Hub, or several Central Hubs?" It depends on which level you look at it. ** There is no single "standard answer" for the same K-line, but there are multiple interpretations that are "established at each level".

## 2. Where does ambiguity come from: level recursion

Ambiguity is not a flaw of Chan Theory, but an inevitable result of its level of recursion (C1-9):

- Levels are recursive and nested - **In the same K line, there are multiple levels of structures**;
- Several small Central Hubs seen at the **small level** form a small-level trend;
- Looking at the **large level**, this entire section may be just an "extension" of a Central Hub at a large level, or even just a "Stroke" at a large level.

The market structure is inherently **multi-scale nested** (like a fractal), so it is natural for "the same trend to be interpreted differently at different scales".

## 3. Give an example

Imagine a schematic quote (without naming the target):

- **Small level (e.g. 5 minutes) view**: There are several small Central Hubs in it, and they rise in sequence, forming a **small level upward trend**;
- **Look at large levels (such as the daily line)**: This entire section up and down may be just an **Central Hub internal shock (extension)** on the daily line, or even just a **stroke** on the daily line.

Therefore, for the same trend, the conclusion of "is it a trend or a Central Hub" is **completely different** at the small level and the large level - this is an intuitive manifestation of ambiguity. **Both interpretations are correct, but the scale is different. **

## 4. Ambiguity is a "feature", not a "bug"

Many people regard polysemy as evidence that "Chan Theory is unclear and unreliable" - this is a misunderstanding.

- The ambiguity faithfully reflects the fact that the market structure is inherently multi-scale;
- The key is not to argue about "which level is right" (**They are all right at their respective levels**), but - **Which level do you use as the "operational level"** (continued from C1-6).
- **Once the operation level is determined, use the structure of that level as the main one**, and ambiguity will no longer bother you: how to interpret other levels is "reference", you are only responsible for your own operation level.

From another perspective: ambiguity gives you the ability to flexibly choose the scale - if you want to do long-term, stick to the big level, if you do short-term, stick to the small level, each chooses what you need.

## 5. How to deal with ambiguity

Decide on three coping methods:

1. **Determine the operation level first**: Make it clear "your Stroke transaction Go Long", **take the Central Hub/buying and selling point of the operation level as the main basis**;
2. **Joining large and small levels** (C1-9): Use **large levels to set the direction and small levels to find entrance**, so that different levels can perform their duties instead of fighting each other;
3. **Don’t repeatedly jump between multiple levels to change your mind**: If you look at it for 5 minutes today and think it’s going to fall, and look at the daily line tomorrow and feel it’s going to rise, switching back and forth will only make you dizzy. **Select the main level and hold on. **

## 6. Ambiguity and "thousands of people and thousands of entanglements"

If you understand the ambiguity, you will understand a major source of "thousands of people and thousands of entanglements": **There are many disagreements, not at all because someone drew the wrong drawing, but because everyone has different levels of viewing. **

- A said this is a trend, B said this is Central Hub, maybe A is looking at the small level, and B is looking at the large level - **Both of them are right**;
- So don't worry about "what is the standard answer", but do the analysis at your own operational level.

This also echoes the reminder of C1-13: Thousands of people and thousands of entanglements does not mean that "Chan Theory has no standards" - there are standards for basic skills (values, conditions for Stroke); and the ambiguity brought about by levels is the characteristics of the structure itself, which can be resolved by "defining the operation level".
