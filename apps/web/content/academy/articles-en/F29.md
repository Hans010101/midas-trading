## 1. Concept introduction: from "knowing" to "doing", lack of a practice ground

At this point, you have learned the knowledge of the previous four levels and the systems and strategies of the fifth level. But F6 told a cruel fact: There is a huge gap between "knowing" and "doing". ** You all know the truth, but when you go to the market, you still chase high prices, carry orders, and fill positions.

Why? Because **trading is a practical skill** - like driving or swimming, you can never learn it by just reading books, you must practice it **by yourself. But the problem is: if you use real money to practice, the tuition is too expensive. Many people lose their principal before they can develop their skills.

This requires a training ground where "you can make mistakes without paying the real price". Midas, as a **full virtual** analysis and trading environment, was born for this purpose. This article serves as the general introduction to Module 18 and explains clearly: **What exactly Midas can help you practice, and what it cannot do for you. **

## 2. Why is a "practice" environment needed?

Let's understand one thing first: trading ability is not "learned" but "practiced".

- You can memorize all indicators, Chan Theory, and strategies by heart ("know");
- But whether you can **execute calmly and according to the rules** in real fluctuations, **withstand floating losses without wavering**, and **really Stop Loss** when it is time to Stop Loss** ("do it") - this can only rely on repeated practice.

And practice costs money. When practicing in the real market, the price of every mistake is real money, and real losses will also trigger strong emotions, causing you to be overwhelmed by emotions when you should learn the most.

**The ideal training ground should allow you to make repeated mistakes and trial and error, and the price is only "virtual numbers + a lesson". ** This is what virtual environments are about.

## 3. What Midas can help you practice: four dimensions

Specifically, in the full virtual environment of Midas, you can practice four things systematically (the following articles will expand on them one by one):

**1. Practice analysis (F30). **
Use K-line, Moving Average / MACD / Bollinger Bands / RSI (Group B), Chan Theory annotation (Group C), and contract dimension data (OI / Long/Short Ratio / Funding Rate) to **read the structure and look for opportunities** - apply the analytical tools you have learned to practical observations of real charts to develop a "disk feel".

**2. Practice execution and discipline (F31). **
Use virtual trading to practice "**plan first and then trade**" (F2), "**execute according to the rules and not make changes on the spot**" (F6) - make these anti-human mistakes such as "carrying orders, random take profit, and retaliatory trading" in the virtual world at zero cost, and then change them, and practice "doing according to the rules" into muscle memory.

**3. Practice your mentality. **
Experience floating profits and losses in virtual trading, **understand and learn to manage emotions** (F5) - Although the intensity of virtual emotions is not as strong as real ones, it allows you to understand your own emotional response patterns in advance.

**4. Practice review and iteration (F33/F34). **
Use the **Strategy Lab Backtest** to see the historical performance of the rules, use the **Review** (F7) of virtual trading to summarize the rights and wrongs of the process, and **polish** your own methods round by round.

## 4. The core value of the virtual environment: pay tuition in the virtual environment

Summarizing the above four points into one sentence, this is the core value of the Midas virtual environment:

**Put "the tuition fees that should be paid and the mistakes that should be made" into the virtual world at zero cost. **

- In the real market, if you make the mistake of "full position + no Stop Loss", the price may be a substantial loss of principal or even liquidation;
- In a virtual environment, the price for making the same mistake is just a reduction in virtual numbers + a profound lesson.

These "expensive lessons" must be paid sooner or later - instead of paying them with real money, it is better to pay them fully and understand them virtually** first. This is the biggest gift of the virtual environment to novices.

## 5. ★But virtuality cannot replace anything (the boundary of honesty)

This section is equally important - be honest about what a virtual environment can't do:

**1. Virtual ≠ real. **
In the virtual environment, there is no financial pressure of real money, and the intensity of ** emotions is greatly weakened**. In the real market, watching real money lose money, the kind of fear and greed can only be partially simulated in the virtual environment. In addition, real trading also has more complex real factors such as slippage and liquidity. **So "you can execute it calmly in the virtual world" does not mean "you can do it in the real world" - you need to be mentally prepared for this. **

**2. Practice well ≠ Make money in the real market. **
This is the most important one: **No matter how well you practice in a virtual environment and no matter how beautiful your virtual results are, there is no guarantee that you will make a profit in the real market. ** The real market is full of uncertainty, **virtual results do not represent real results**.

**3. It is a tool, not an answer. **
Midas is a "low-cost testing and practice tool", not a "guaranteed profit system". It can reduce the cost of practice and trial and error, but whether you can really do it well ultimately depends on you - on your understanding, discipline and fear of risk.
